So when the Lord knew that the strict religious ones had heard that Jesus was making and immersing more students than John 
(although Jesus himself didn't immerse, but his students), 
he left Judea and departed into Galilee. 
He needed to pass through Samaria. 
So he came to a city of Samaria called Sychar, near the parcel of ground that Jacob gave to his son Joseph. 
Jacob's well was there. Jesus, being tired from his journey, sat down by the well. It was about noon. 
A woman of Samaria came to draw water. Jesus said to her, "Give me a drink." 
His students had gone away into the city to buy food. 
The Samaritan woman said to him, "How is it that you, being a Jew, ask for a drink from me, a Samaritan woman?" (For Jews have no dealings with Samaritans.) 
Jesus answered her, "If you knew the gift of God, and who it's who says to you, 'Give me a drink,' you'd have asked him, and he'd have given you living water." 
The woman said to him, "Sir, you have nothing to draw with, and the well is deep. So where do you get that living water? 
Are you greater than our father Jacob, who gave us the well and drank from it himself, as did his children and his livestock?" 
Jesus answered her, "Everyone who drinks of this water will thirst again, 
but whoever drinks of the water that I'll give them will never thirst again. The water that I'll give them will become in them a spring of water welling up into the real life of the era." 
The woman said to him, "Sir, give me this water, so that I don't get thirsty, neither come all the way here to draw." 
Jesus said to her, "Go, call your husband, and come here." 
The woman answered, "I have no husband." Jesus said to her, "You said it well: 'I have no husband.' 
for you have had five husbands; and he whom you now have isn't your husband. This you have said truly." 
The woman said to him, "Sir, I can see that you're a spokesman. 
Our ancestors worshiped on this mountain, and you Jewish people say that Jerusalem is the place where people should worship." 
Jesus said to her, "Woman, trust me, the time is coming when you'll worship the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem. 
You worship what you don't know. We worship what we know, for healing and wholeness comes from the Jewish people. 
But the hour comes, and now is, when the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and truth, for the Father seeks such to be his worshipers. 
God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth." 
The woman said to him, "I know that Messiah is coming, he who's called Christ. When he has come, he'll declare to us all things." 
Jesus said to her, "I am he, the one who speaks to you." 
Just then, his students came. They were surprised that he was speaking with a woman, yet no one said, "What are you looking for?" or, "Why do you speak with her?" 
So the woman left her water pot, went away into the city, and said to the people, 
"Come, see a man who told me everything that I have done. Can this be the Christ?" 
They went out of the city, and were coming to him. 
In the meanwhile, his students urged him, saying, "Rabbi, eat." 
But he said to them, "I have food to eat that you don't know about." 
The students said to one another, "Has anyone brought him something to eat?" 
Jesus said to them, "My food is to do the will of him who sent me and to finish his work. 
Don't you say, 'There are yet four months until the harvest'? Look, I tell you, lift up your eyes and look at the fields: they're already ripe for harvest. 
The one who reaps earns wages and gathers fruit for the real life of the era, so that the one who sows and the one who reaps can celebrate together. 
In this the saying is true, 'One sows, and another reaps.' 
I sent you to reap what you haven't worked for. Others have done the work, and you have stepped into their work." 
From that city many of the Samaritans trusted in him because of the word of the woman, who testified, "He told me everything that I have done." 
So when the Samaritans came to him, they begged him to stay with them. He stayed there two days. 
Many more trusted because of his word. 
They said to the woman, "Now we trust, not because of your speaking; for we have heard for ourselves, and know that this is indeed the Christ, the Restorer of the world." 
After the two days he went out from there and went into Galilee. 
Jesus himself testified that a spokesman has no honor in his own hometown. 
So when he came into Galilee, the Galileans welcomed him, having seen all the things that he did in Jerusalem at the feast, for they also went to the feast. 
Jesus came again to Cana of Galilee, where he made the water into wine. There was a certain royal official whose son was sick at Capernaum. 
When he heard that Jesus had come out of Judea into Galilee, he went to him and begged him to come down and heal his son, for he was at the point of death. 
Jesus said to him, "Unless you people see signs and wonders, you'll never trust." 
The royal official said to him, "Sir, come down before my child dies." 
Jesus said to him, "Go your way. Your son lives." The man trusted the word that Jesus spoke to him, and he went his way. 
As he was going down, his servants met him and reported, saying, "Your child lives!" 
So he asked them what time it was when he began to get better. They said to him, "Yesterday at one in the afternoon, the fever left him." 
So the father knew that it was at that time in which Jesus said to him, "Your son lives." He trusted, as did his whole house. 
This is again the second sign that Jesus did, having come out of Judea into Galilee.
