This saying is trustworthy: if someone wants to be an overseer, he is reaching for something good. 
So an overseer must be above reproach, faithful to one wife, clear-headed, sensible, well-ordered, welcoming to strangers, and able to teach; 
not a heavy drinker, not a fighter, not out for money, but gentle, not quarrelsome, and not a lover of money. 
someone who leads his own household well, with children who put themselves under him, respectfully; 
(after all, if someone doesn't know how to lead his own household, how will he take care of God's gathering?) 
He must not be new to the trust, or he may swell up with pride and come under the same verdict as the Slanderer. 
He must also have a good name with those outside, so that he doesn't fall into insult and into the Slanderer's trap. 
Servants must be serious in the same way, not saying one thing to one person and another to the next, not drinking too much wine, and not out for money. 
They must hold the secret of the trust with a clean conscience. 
Let them be tested first, and then let them serve, if there is nothing to be held against them. 
Their wives must be serious in the same way, not slanderers, clear-headed, and trustworthy in everything. 
Servants must be faithful to one wife, leading their children and their own households well. 
Those who have served well gain a good standing for themselves, and great openness in the trust that is in Christ Jesus. 
I am writing this to you, hoping to come to you before long, 
but if I am delayed, so that you may know how people should live in God's house, which is the gathering of the living God, the pillar and foundation of the truth. 
And everyone agrees: the secret of devotion to God is great. He was made visible in the flesh, made right in the Spirit, seen by messengers, announced among the nations, trusted in the world, and taken up in weight.
