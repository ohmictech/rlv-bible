# RLV Constitution
## The working law of The Bible: Real Life Version

Read this before every chapter. Every new verdict goes in, no exceptions.

This file is the **fast lookup: one line per choice.** The full reasoning, the precedents log and the history of every disputed ruling are preserved in `rlv-constitution-full-2026-11-06.md`. When a row here is not enough to settle an argument, that file is the court of appeal.

---

## Part 1: Standing Rules

1. **Format and layout.** One folder per book, `<NN>-<book>`, numbered in 66-book canonical order (`01-genesis` … `66-revelation`, so the NT runs 40-66). One file per chapter, `<book>-ch-<NNN>.md`. Files carry **verses only**: no title line, no chapter line. **Verse V sits on line V.** UTF-8 with BOM, LF only, trailing newline, one trailing space on every line, straight quotes only.
2. **Verse-numbering caveat.** The source follows the critical text, so four chapters lack a tradition-only verse and every later verse drifts one line high: **Luke 17** (no 17:36), **Acts 8** (no 8:37), **Acts 15** (no 15:34), **Acts 24** (no 24:7). The lines are right; only the counting slips. Never renumber.
3. **Register test.** Plain English, 2026, a 20-year-old with no church background. If they would not say it, it does not go in. **Change an old word only when it has worn into a church abstraction that says nothing** (church, heresy, repentance, baptism); **keep it when it still carries its own force** to someone who has never been inside a church (Christ, Lord, the gathering, spokesman). Accuracy is the floor; the question is whether the new word is stronger in the ear.
4. **Read up, read down.** Where tradition reads a word small, point at the deed (truly loved, real life). Where tradition inflates a word, read it down to the deed (rests → relief, blessed → flourishing).
5. **The text wins over the system.** Any ruling, however elegant, yields to what the verse is actually doing.
6. **Consistency.** Every flagged form, every time. Release valves are part of the rule, not exceptions to it. Where the same prayer or formula is spoken twice, the two read word for word the same.
7. **Capitalization.** Capitalize God, the Father, the Son, the Holy Spirit, the Human One, the Slanderer, the Adversary, the Restorer. Lowercase the reign of the sky, the real life, wholeness, the drift, wrongdoing, trust, and all pronouns.
8. **Prose.** *Will* over *shall*, except a quotation of the law, which keeps its *shall* and its *lest* and is never swept. Break about 90% of semicolons. Keep two-beat pairings. Keep roughly 80% of WEB's sentence structure in the Gospels, less in the epistles.
9. **Idiom insinuation.** English may not complete more than the Greek leaves open. Matt 1:25 reads "did not sleep with her" only, with no English "before" implying a second event.
10. **Audit.** No chapter is done without a leftover sweep, a change log and three read-throughs. Sweep against Part 2's rendering column, **lemma by lemma, never against a hand-written list of WEB words.** Tool: `engwebu_usfm.zip`, which carries Strong's tags inline. **It tags only four-digit numbers (G1000 and up); anything lower returns a confident zero.** **And its alignment slips onto the wrong words** (John 5:38), so always reconcile the lemma list against the text itself.
11. **Stance.** Written objectively, on the assumption that the scriptures teach restorative justice: the rescue of all, **each in their own order**. Do not stretch infernalism into a text.
12. **No em dashes or en dashes. Ever.** No exceptions, not even inside a quotation of WEB. Choose the replacement by what the dash was doing: apposition → comma, two independent clauses → semicolon, a list or definition → colon, a parenthetical that would collide with commas → parentheses.
13. **One line per row.** Every row in Part 2 is a **single sentence** plus its anchors, about 150 characters, no essay. If the reason cannot be said in one line, the extra reasoning goes in `rlv-constitution-full-2026-11-06.md` and the row keeps only the verdict and the trigger. A row that needs a paragraph to defend itself is a row that has not been decided yet.

## Part 3: Hebrew Word Choices

Same format, same Rule 13: one line per lemma. **Only the divine name is ruled so far.** The rest of the Old Testament is imported and unswept, so until a row exists the text stands as WEB's wording and no precedent is created. A row appears the day the first book forces the question.

| Lemma | Rendering | Why |
|---|---|---|
| YHWH (the divine name) | God | Ruled 2026-11-06: one God for a modern reader, so every occurrence reads **God**. Deut 6:4 is where this costs most. Ex 20:2; Ps 23:1; Isa 45:5. |
| YHWH stacked with a title | God / your God | Stacked titles collapse: "the LORD God" and "Lord GOD" both read **God**, and "the LORD your God" reads **your God**. Gen 2:4; Ex 20:2; Ezek 2:4. |

| olam | for the eras | The Hebrew twin of aion, so never "for ever": adverbial forms read **for the eras**, doxologies **for all the eras**. Gen 6:3; Ps 90:2. |
| olam (adjective) | perpetual | Where it modifies a thing: "a perpetual covenant," "perpetual joy." The two divine titles read **of the eras** (Gen 21:33; Isa 9:6). |
| navi | spokesman | The Hebrew twin of prophetes, so the Old Testament follows Part 2. Gen 20:7; 1 Sam 9:9; Jer 1:5. Set phrase exception belongs to prophetes. |
| mal'ak | messenger | The Hebrew twin of angelos, ruled **messenger**, never "angel." Gen 16:7; Judg 6:11; Mal 3:1. |
| kaphar / kippur | make things right | The covering word, never "atonement," which no 20-year-old can define. Lev 16:16; Num 35:33. |
| mashiach | Christ / anointed | **Christ** where it means him (Ps 2:2; Dan 9:25-26); **anointed** for priests, kings, Cyrus, cherubim, objects. |
| ga'al / ge'ullah | buy back / buying back | The family claim, not "free": the relative who buys back land, a person, or shed blood. Job 19:25; Lev 25:26; Isa 43:14. |

| avon | guilt | The twistedness and the guilt it carries, never "iniquity." Gen 15:16; Ex 20:5. |
| pesha | rebellion | Breaking off, not a slip. Ex 23:21; Isa 53:5. |
| rasha / resha | guilty / guilt | The person and the state, merged with avon into guilt. Gen 18:23; Prov 21:4. |
| ra' | bad | Moral bad and plain calamity alike; **malicious** where the intent is deliberate. Gen 3:5; 6:5. |
| kavod / kaved | weight, honor, or splendor | The heavy word, from the root for heavy, which is also the root behind "honor your father." **Weight** where it fills or settles, **honor** where it is given, **splendor** where it shines. 1 Kgs 8:11; Ex 33:18; Ps 24:7. |

**The cases that will force a verdict first, and what each has to answer:**

1. **nephesh.** Traditionally "soul," but the word means the whole living person, the throat, or a life. Part 2 already rules psyche = **life**, and nephesh is its Hebrew twin, so it probably follows.
2. **ruach.** Wind, breath and spirit in one word, and the Hebrew does not distinguish them. A Rule 4 case: the sentence decides every occurrence, so this may never get a single rendering.
3. **hesed.** The famously untranslatable one: loyal love, mercy, steadfast love. It must not become "lovingkindness," which nobody has said since 1611.
4. **torah.** "Law" suggests a legal code; the word is instruction or teaching. Part 2 rules nomos = **law**, so the two must be kept apart or the Old Testament will read as legislation.
5. **elohim / el.** God or gods, and the plural form can carry a singular meaning, so the sentence decides. Shaddai is settled: **God Almighty** where it adds.

## Amendment procedure

The constitution grows only by new verdicts. A new lemma gets a row the day it is ruled on. Routine applications of rows already in the table are not logged. When a row is reversed, the reversal is recorded with its reason, and the text is conformed to the row and never the reverse.
