A Prayer by Moses, the man of God. Lord, you have been our dwelling place for all generations. 
Before the mountains were born, before you had formed the earth and the world, even from perpetual to perpetual, you're God. 
You turn man to destruction, saying, "Return, you children of men." 
A thousand years in your sight are just like yesterday when it's past, like a watch in the night. 
You sweep them away as they sleep. In the morning they sprout like new grass. 
In the morning it sprouts and springs up. By evening, it's withered and dry. 
We're consumed in your anger. We're troubled in your wrath. 
You have set our guilt before you, our secret mistakes in the light of your presence. 
All our days have passed away in your wrath. We bring our years to an end as a sigh. 
The days of our years are seventy, or even by reason of strength eighty years; yet their pride is but labor and sorrow, for it passes quickly, and we fly away. 
Who knows the power of your anger, your wrath according to the fear that's due to you? 
So teach us to count our days, that we can gain a heart of wisdom. 
Relent, God! How long? Have compassion on your servants! 
Satisfy us in the morning with your loving kindness, that we can rejoice and be glad all our days. 
Make us glad for as many days as you have afflicted us, for as many years as we have seen bad. 
Let your work appear to your servants, your glory to their children. 
Let the favor of the Lord our God be on us. Establish the work of our hands for us. Yes, establish the work of our hands.
