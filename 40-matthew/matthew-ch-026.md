When Jesus had finished all these words, he said to his students, 
"You know that after two days the Passover is coming, and the Human One will be handed over to be crucified." 
Then the chief priests, the Bible teachers, and the elders of the people gathered in the courtyard of the high priest, who was called Caiaphas. 
Together they plotted how to seize Jesus by deceit and kill him. 
But they said, "Not during the feast, or there may be a riot among the people." 
Now when Jesus was in Bethany, in the house of Simon, who had leprosy, 
a woman came to him with an alabaster jar of very expensive perfume, and she poured it on his head as he sat at the table. 
But when his students saw it, they were angry, saying, "Why this waste? 
This perfume could have been sold for a lot and given to the poor." 
Jesus knew this and said to them, "Why are you bothering the woman? She has done a good deed for me. 
You always have the poor with you, but you don't always have me. 
In pouring this perfume on my body, she did it to prepare me for burial. 
Truly I tell you, wherever this Good News is preached in the whole world, what this woman has done will also be spoken of in memory of her." 
Then one of the twelve, who was called Judas Iscariot, went to the chief priests 
and said, "What will you give me if I hand him over to you?" So they weighed out thirty pieces of silver for him. 
From that time he looked for a chance to hand him over. 
Now on the first day of the festival of bread without yeast, the students came to Jesus and asked, "Where do you want us to prepare for you to eat the Passover?" 
He said, "Go into the city to a certain man and tell him, 'The Teacher says, "My time is near. I will keep the Passover at your house with my students."'" 
The students did as Jesus commanded them, and they prepared the Passover. 
Now when evening had come, he was at the table with the twelve students. 
As they were eating, he said, "Truly I tell you that one of you will hand me over." 
They were deeply grieved, and each began to ask him, "It isn't me, is it, Lord?" 
He answered, "The one who dipped his hand with me in the dish will hand me over. 
The Human One goes even as it is written of him, but how terrible for that man who hands over the Human One! It would be better for that man if he had not been born." 
Judas, who was handing him over, answered, "It isn't me, is it, Rabbi?" He said to him, "You said it." 
As they were eating, Jesus took bread, gave thanks for it, and broke it. He gave it to the students and said, "Take, eat. This is my body." 
He took the cup, gave thanks, and gave to them, saying, "All of you drink it, 
for this is my blood of the new binding arrangement, poured out for everyone for the release of their mistakes. 
But I tell you that I will not drink from this fruit of the vine from now on, until that day when I drink it new with you in my Father's reign." 
When they had sung a hymn, they went out to the Mount of Olives. 
Then Jesus said to them, "All of you will be trapped because of me tonight, for it is written, 'I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock will be scattered.' 
But after I am raised up, I will go before you into Galilee." 
But Peter answered him, "Even if everyone is trapped because of you, I will never be trapped." 
Jesus said to him, "Truly I tell you that tonight, before the rooster crows, you will disown me three times." 
Peter said to him, "Even if I must die with you, I will not disown you." All of the students also said the same. 
Then Jesus came with them to a place called Gethsemane, and said to his students, "Sit here while I go over there and pray." 
He took Peter and the two sons of Zebedee with him, and began to be deeply grieved and troubled. 
Then Jesus said to them, "My life is crushed with grief, even to death. Stay here and stay awake with me." 
He went forward a little, fell on his face, and prayed, saying, "My Father, if it is possible, let this cup pass away from me. Still, not what I want, but what you want." 
He came to the students and found them sleeping, and said to Peter, "What, couldn't you stay awake with me for one hour? 
Stay awake and pray, so that you don't come into testing. The spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak." 
Again he went away a second time and prayed, saying, "My Father, if this cup can't pass away unless I drink it, let what you want be done." 
He came again and found them sleeping, for their eyes were heavy. 
He left them again, went away, and prayed a third time, saying the same words. 
Then he came to his students and said to them, "Are you still sleeping and resting? Look, the time has come, and the Human One is being handed over into the hands of those who have drifted. 
Get up, let's go. Look, the one who hands me over is near." 
While he was still speaking, look, Judas, one of the twelve, came, and with him a large crowd with swords and clubs, from the chief priests and elders of the people. 
Now the one handing him over had given them a sign, saying, "Whoever I kiss, he is the one. Seize him." 
Immediately he came to Jesus, and said, "Greetings, Rabbi!" and kissed him. 
Jesus said to him, "Friend, do what you came for." Then they came and laid hands on Jesus and seized him. 
Look, one of those who were with Jesus stretched out his hand and drew his sword, and struck the high priest's slave, and cut off his ear. 
Then Jesus said to him, "Put your sword back into its place, for everyone who takes the sword will die by the sword. 
Or do you think that I couldn't ask my Father, and he would even now send me more than twelve legions of messengers? 
How then would the Scriptures be fulfilled that it must be so?" 
At that time Jesus said to the crowds, "Have you come out against me with swords and clubs as if I were a robber? I sat daily in the temple courts teaching, and you didn't arrest me. 
But all this has happened so that the Scriptures of the spokesmen would be fulfilled." Then all the students left him and fled. 
Those who had seized Jesus led him away to Caiaphas the high priest, where the Bible teachers and the elders were gathered together. 
But Peter followed him at a distance as far as the courtyard of the high priest, and went in and sat with the officers to see how it would end. 
Now the chief priests, the elders, and the whole council sought false witness against Jesus, that they could put him to death, 
but they found none, though many false witnesses came forward. At last two came 
and said, "This man said, 'I can tear down the temple of God and build it in three days.'" 
The high priest stood up and said to him, "Have you no answer? What is this that these testify against you?" 
But Jesus stayed silent. The high priest said to him, "I put you under oath by the living God: tell us whether you are the Christ, the Son of God." 
Jesus said to him, "You have said so. Yet I tell you, from now on you will see the Human One sitting at the right hand of Power, and coming on the clouds of the sky." 
Then the high priest tore his clothing, saying, "He has spoken slander! Why do we need any more witnesses? Look, now you have heard this slander. 
What do you think?" They answered, "He deserves death!" 
Then they spat in his face and beat him with their fists, and some slapped him, 
saying, "Speak for God to us, you Christ! Who hit you?" 
Now Peter was sitting outside in the courtyard, and a servant girl came to him, saying, "You were also with Jesus, the Galilean!" 
But he denied it in front of them all, saying, "I don't know what you are talking about." 
When he had gone out to the gateway, someone else saw him and said to those who were there, "This man also was with Jesus of Nazareth." 
Again he denied it with an oath, "I don't know the man." 
After a little while those who stood by came and said to Peter, "Surely you are also one of them, for your speech makes you known." 
Then he began to curse and to swear, "I don't know the man!" Immediately the rooster crowed. 
Peter remembered the words Jesus had said to him: "Before the rooster crows, you will disown me three times." Then he went out and cried bitterly.
