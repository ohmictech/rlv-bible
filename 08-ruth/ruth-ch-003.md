Naomi her mother-in-law said to her, "My daughter, will I not seek rest for you, that it might be well with you? 
Now isn't Boaz our kinsman, with whose maidens you were? Look, he'll be winnowing barley tonight on the threshing floor. 
So wash yourself, anoint yourself, get dressed, and go down to the threshing floor; but don't make yourself known to the man until he has finished eating and drinking. 
It will be, when he lies down, that you will note the place where he's lying. Then you will go in, uncover his feet, and lie down. Then he'll tell you what to do." 
She said to her, "All that you say, I'll do." 
She went down to the threshing floor, and did everything that her mother-in-law told her. 
When Boaz had eaten and drunk, and his heart was merry, he went to lie down at the end of the heap of grain. She came softly, uncovered his feet, and lay down. 
At midnight, the man was startled and turned himself; and look, a woman lay at his feet. 
He said, "Who are you?" She answered, "I'm Ruth your servant. So spread the corner of your garment over your servant; for you're a near kinsman." 
He said, "You're blessed by God, my daughter. You have shown more kindness in the latter end than at the beginning, because you didn't follow young men, whether poor or rich. 
Now, my daughter, don't be afraid. I'll do to you all that you say; for all the city of my people knows that you're a worthy woman. 
Now it's true that I'm a near kinsman. However, there's a kinsman nearer than I. 
Stay this night, and in the morning, if he'll perform for you the part of a kinsman, good. Let him do the kinsman's duty. But if he won't do the duty of a kinsman for you, then I'll do the duty of a kinsman for you, as God lives. Lie down until the morning." 
She lay at his feet until the morning, then she rose up before one could discern another. He said, "Let it not be known that the woman came to the threshing floor." 
He said, "Bring the mantle that's on you, and hold it." She held it; and he measured six measures of barley, and laid it on her; then he went into the city. 
When she came to her mother-in-law, she said, "How did it go, my daughter?" She told her all that the man had done for her. 
She said, "He gave me these six measures of barley; for he said, 'Don't go empty to your mother-in-law.'" 
Then she said, "Wait, my daughter, until you know what will happen; for the man won't rest until he has settled this today."
