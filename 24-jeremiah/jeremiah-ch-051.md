God says: "Look, I'll raise up against Babylon, and against those who dwell in Lebkamai, a destroying wind. 
I'll send to Babylon strangers, who will winnow her. They'll empty her land; for in the day of trouble they'll be against her all around. 
Against him who bends, let the archer bend his bow, also against him who lifts himself up in his coat of mail. Don't spare her young men! Utterly destroy all her army! 
They'll fall down slain in the land of the Chaldeans, and thrust through in her streets. 
Israel isn't forsaken, nor Judah, by his God, by the God of Armies; though their land is full of guilt against the Holy One of Israel. 
"Flee out of the middle of Babylon! Everyone save his own life! Don't be cut off in her guilt, for it's the time of God's vengeance. He'll give to her a recompense. 
Babylon has been a golden cup in God's hand, who made all the earth drunk. The nations have drunk of her wine; the nations have gone mad. 
Babylon has suddenly fallen and been destroyed! Wail for her! Take balm for her pain. Perhaps she can be healed. 
"We'd have healed Babylon, but she isn't healed. Forsake her, and let's each go into his own country; for her judgment reaches to the sky, and is lifted up even to the skies. 
'God has produced our righteousness. Come, and let's declare in Zion the work of our God.' 
"Make the arrows sharp! Hold the shields firmly! God has stirred up the spirit of the kings of the Medes, because his purpose is against Babylon, to destroy it; for it's the vengeance of God, the vengeance of his temple. 
Set up a standard against the walls of Babylon! Make the watch strong! Set the watchmen, and prepare the ambushes; for God has both purposed and done that which he spoke concerning the inhabitants of Babylon. 
You who dwell on many waters, abundant in treasures, your end has come, the measure of your covetousness. 
The God of Armies has sworn by himself, saying, 'Surely I'll fill you with men, as with locusts, and they'll lift up a shout against you.' 
"He has made the earth by his power. He has established the world by his wisdom. By his understanding he has stretched out the sky. 
When he utters his voice, there's a roar of waters in the sky, and he causes the vapors to ascend from the ends of the earth. He makes lightning for the rain, and brings the wind out of his treasuries. 
"Every man has become stupid and without knowledge. Every goldsmith is disappointed by his image, for his molten images are falsehood, and there's no breath in them. 
They're vanity, a work of delusion. In the time of their visitation, they'll perish. 
The portion of Jacob isn't like these, for he formed all things, including the tribe of his inheritance. The God of Armies is his name. 
"You're my battle ax and weapons of war. With you I'll break the nations into pieces. With you I'll destroy kingdoms. 
With you I'll break in pieces the horse and his rider. 
With you I'll break in pieces the chariot and him who rides therein. With you I'll break in pieces man and woman. With you I'll break in pieces the old man and the youth. With you I'll break in pieces the young man and the virgin. 
With you I'll break in pieces the shepherd and his flock. With you I'll break in pieces the farmer and his yoke. With you I'll break in pieces governors and deputies. 
"I'll give to Babylon and to all the inhabitants of Chaldea all their bad that they have done in Zion in your sight," says God. 
"Look, I'm against you, destroying mountain," says God, "which destroys all the earth. I'll stretch out my hand on you, roll you down from the rocks, and will make you a burned mountain. 
They won't take a cornerstone from you, nor a stone for foundations; but you'll be desolate for the eras," says God. 
"Set up a standard in the land! Blow the trumpet among the nations! Prepare the nations against her! Call together against her the kingdoms of Ararat, Minni, and Ashkenaz! Appoint a marshal against her! Cause the horses to come up as the swarming locusts! 
Prepare against her the nations, the kings of the Medes, its governors, and all its deputies, and all the land of their dominion! 
The land trembles and is in pain; for the purposes of God against Babylon stand, to make the land of Babylon a desolation, without inhabitant. 
The mighty men of Babylon have stopped fighting, they remain in their strongholds. Their might has failed. They have become as women. Her dwelling places are set on fire. Her bars are broken. 
One runner will run to meet another, and one messenger to meet another, to show the king of Babylon that his city is taken on every quarter. 
So the passages are seized. They have burned the reeds with fire. The men of war are frightened." 
The God of Armies, the God of Israel says: "The daughter of Babylon is like a threshing floor at the time when it's trodden. Yet a little while, and the time of harvest comes for her." 
"Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon has devoured me. He has crushed me. He has made me an empty vessel. He has, like a monster, swallowed me up. He has filled his mouth with my delicacies. He has cast me out. 
May the violence done to me and to my flesh be on Babylon!" the inhabitant of Zion will say; and, "May my blood be on the inhabitants of Chaldea!" will Jerusalem say. 
So God says: "Look, I'll plead your cause, and take vengeance for you. I'll dry up her sea, and make her fountain dry. 
Babylon will become heaps, a dwelling place for jackals, an astonishment, and a hissing, without inhabitant. 
They'll roar together like young lions. They'll growl as lions' cubs. 
When they're inflamed, I'll make their feast, and I'll make them drunk, that they can rejoice, and sleep a perpetual sleep, and not wake up," says God. 
"I'll bring them down like lambs to the slaughter, like rams with male goats. 
"How Sheshach is taken! How the praise of the whole earth is seized! How Babylon has become a desolation among the nations! 
The sea has come up on Babylon. She's covered with the multitude of its waves. 
Her cities have become a desolation, a dry land, and a desert, a land in which no man dwells. No son of man passes by it. 
I'll execute judgment on Bel in Babylon, and I'll bring out of his mouth that which he has swallowed up. The nations won't flow any more to him. Yes, the wall of Babylon will fall. 
"My people, go away from the middle of her, and each of you save yourselves from God's fierce anger. 
Don't let your heart faint. Don't fear for the news that will be heard in the land. News will come one year, and after that in another year news will come, and violence in the land, ruler against ruler. 
The days are coming that I'll execute judgment on the engraved images of Babylon; and her whole land will be confounded. All her slain will fall in the middle of her. 
Then the sky and the earth, and all that's therein, will sing for joy over Babylon; for the destroyers will come to her from the north," says God. 
"As Babylon has caused the slain of Israel to fall, so the slain of all the land will fall at Babylon. 
You who have escaped the sword, go! Don't stand still! Remember God from afar, and let Jerusalem come into your mind." 
"We're confounded because we have heard reproach. Confusion has covered our faces, for strangers have come into the sanctuaries of God's house." 
"The days are coming," says God, "that I'll execute judgment on her engraved images; and through all her land the wounded will groan. 
Though Babylon should mount up to the sky, and though she should fortify the height of her strength, yet destroyers will come to her from me," says God. 
"The sound of a cry comes from Babylon, and of great destruction from the land of the Chaldeans! 
God lays Babylon waste, and destroys out of her the great voice! Their waves roar like many waters. The noise of their voice is uttered. 
The destroyer has come on her, even on Babylon. Her mighty men are taken. Their bows are broken in pieces, for God is a God of retribution. He'll surely repay. 
I'll make her princes, her wise men, her governors, her deputies, and her mighty men drunk. They'll sleep a perpetual sleep, and not wake up," says the King, whose name is the God of Armies. 
The God of Armies says: "The wide walls of Babylon will be utterly overthrown. Her high gates will be burned with fire. The peoples will labor for vanity, and the nations for the fire; and they'll be weary." 
The word which Jeremiah the spokesman commanded Seraiah the son of Neriah, the son of Mahseiah, when he went with Zedekiah the king of Judah to Babylon in the fourth year of his reign. Now Seraiah was chief quartermaster. 
Jeremiah wrote in a book all the bad that should come on Babylon, even all these words that are written concerning Babylon. 
Jeremiah said to Seraiah, "When you come to Babylon, then see that you read all these words, 
and say, 'God, you have spoken concerning this place, to cut it off, that no one will dwell in it, neither man nor animal, but that it'll be desolate for the eras.' 
It'll be, when you have finished reading this book, that you will bind a stone to it, and cast it into the middle of the Euphrates. 
Then you will say, 'Thus will Babylon sink, and won't rise again because of the bad that I'll bring on her; and they'll be weary.'" Thus far are the words of Jeremiah.
