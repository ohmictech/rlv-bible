Paul, a sent one of Christ Jesus by the will of God, and Timothy our brother, to the gathering of God that is at Corinth, with all the set-apart ones who are in the whole of Achaia: 
Favor and wholeness to you from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. 
Praised be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies and the God of all comfort, 
who comforts us in all our hard times, so that we can comfort those in any hard times, with the comfort we ourselves are given by God. 
Just as the sufferings of Christ overflow to us, so our comfort also overflows through Christ. 
But if we are in hard times, it is for your comfort and being made whole. If we are comforted, it is for your comfort, which works in you staying power under the same sufferings we also suffer. 
Our expectation for you is firm, knowing that as you share the sufferings, so you also share the comfort. 
We don't want you to be unaware, brothers, of the hard times that came on us in Asia: we were weighed down far beyond our strength, so much that we gave up hope even of living. 
Yes, we had the sentence of death within ourselves, so that we wouldn't trust in ourselves, but in God who raises the dead, 
who rescued us out of so great a death, and does rescue, and on whom we have set our expectation that he will also still rescue us, 
you also helping together on our behalf by your prayer; so that, for the gift given to us through many, thanks may be given by many people on your behalf. 
Our pride is this: the witness of our conscience that we behaved in the world with set-apartness and God-given sincerity, not with fleshly wisdom but with the favor of God, and all the more toward you. 
We write no other things to you than what you read or even acknowledge, and I hope you will acknowledge to the end, 
as also you acknowledged us in part, that we are your reason to be proud, even as you also are ours, in the day of our Lord Jesus. 
In this confidence, I intended to come to you first, so that you might have a second gift, 
and by you to pass into Macedonia, and again from Macedonia to come to you, and to be sent forward by you on my journey to Judea. 
When I planned this, then, was I being fickle? Or do I plan the way I plan by human standards, so that with me there would be both "Yes, yes" and "No, no"? 
But as God is trustworthy, our word to you wasn't "Yes and no." 
The Son of God, Jesus Christ, who was announced among you by us, by me, Silvanus, and Timothy, wasn't "Yes and no," but in him is "Yes." 
However many promises God has made, in him is the "Yes." That is why we also say the "Amen" through him, to the weight of God through us. 
Now the one who makes us firm with you in Christ, and who anointed us, is God, 
who also sealed us and gave us the down payment of the Spirit in our hearts. 
But I call God as witness over my life, that to spare you I didn't come to Corinth. 
We don't rule over your trust, but we work with you for your joy. You stand firm in trust.
