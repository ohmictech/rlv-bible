Immediately in the morning, the chief priests, with the elders, the Bible teachers, and the whole council, held a consultation, tied Jesus up, carried him away, and handed him over to Pilate. 
Pilate asked him, "Are you the King of the Jews?" He answered, "So you say." 
The chief priests accused him of many things. 
Pilate again asked him, "Have you no answer? Look how many things they're testifying against you!" 
But Jesus made no further answer, which amazed Pilate. 
Now at the feast he used to release to them one prisoner, whomever they asked for. 
There was one called Barabbas, tied up with his fellow rebels, men who had committed murder in the uprising. 
The crowd came up and began to ask him to do as he always did for them. 
Pilate answered them, saying, "Do you want me to release to you the King of the Jews?" 
He knew that the chief priests had handed him over out of envy. 
But the chief priests stirred up the crowd to have him release Barabbas instead. 
Pilate again asked them, "What then should I do with the one you call the King of the Jews?" 
They cried out again, "Crucify him!" 
Pilate said to them, "Why? What bad thing has he done?" But they shouted all the louder, "Crucify him!" 
Pilate, wanting to please the crowd, released Barabbas to them, and after having Jesus flogged, handed him over to be crucified. 
The soldiers led him away inside the courtyard, which is the governor's headquarters, and they called together the whole cohort. 
They dressed him in purple, and braiding a crown of thorns, they put it on him. 
They began to salute him, "Hail, King of the Jews!" 
They struck his head with a reed and spat on him, and bowing their knees, they bowed down to him. 
When they had mocked him, they took the purple cloak off him, and put his own garments on him. They led him out to crucify him. 
They forced a man passing by, Simon of Cyrene, who was coming in from the countryside, the father of Alexander and Rufus, to carry his cross. 
They brought him to the place called Golgotha, which means "The place of a skull." 
They offered him wine mixed with myrrh to drink, but he didn't take it. 
They crucified him and divided his clothes among them, casting lots for them, to decide what each would take. 
It was mid-morning when they crucified him. 
The notice of his accusation was written above him: "THE KING OF THE JEWS." 
With him they crucified two robbers, one on his right hand, and one on his left. 
The Scripture was fulfilled which says, "He was counted with those who break the law." 
Those who passed by slandered him, shaking their heads and saying, "Ha! You who would tear down the temple and build it in three days, 
rescue yourself, and come down from the cross!" 
In the same way the chief priests also, mocking him among themselves with the Bible teachers, said, "He rescued others. He can't rescue himself. 
Let the Christ, the King of Israel, come down from the cross now, so that we can see and trust him." And those who were crucified with him also insulted him. 
When noon came, there was darkness over the whole land until mid-afternoon. 
At mid-afternoon Jesus cried with a loud voice, saying, "Eloi, Eloi, lama sabachthani?" which means "My God, my God, why have you abandoned me?" 
Some of those standing by, when they heard it, said, "Look, he's calling Elijah." 
One ran, filled a sponge with sour wine, put it on a reed, and gave it to him to drink, saying, "Leave him alone. Let's see whether Elijah comes to take him down." 
Jesus cried out with a loud voice, and gave up the spirit. 
The curtain of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom. 
When the centurion, who stood facing him, saw that he cried out like this and breathed his last, he said, "Truly this man was the Son of God!" 
There were also women watching from a distance, among them Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James the younger and of Joses, and Salome, 
who, when he was in Galilee, had followed him and served him, and many other women who had come up with him to Jerusalem. 
When evening had come, since it was the Preparation Day, that is, the day before the Sabbath, 
Joseph of Arimathaea, a prominent council member who was himself looking for God's reign, came. He boldly went in to Pilate and asked for Jesus' body. 
Pilate was surprised to hear that he was already dead, and summoning the centurion, he asked him whether he had been dead for long. 
When he found out from the centurion, he gave the body to Joseph. 
He bought a linen cloth, and taking him down, wrapped him in the linen cloth and laid him in a tomb that had been cut out of rock. Then he rolled a stone against the entrance of the tomb. 
Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses saw where he was laid.
