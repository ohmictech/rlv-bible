If I speak with the languages of people and of messengers, but don't have love, I have become a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal. 
If I have the gift of speaking for God, and know all secrets and all knowledge, and if I have all trust, so as to move mountains, but don't have love, I'm nothing. 
If I give away everything I own to feed the poor, and if I hand over my body to be burned, but don't have love, it does me no good. 
Love is patient and kind. Love doesn't envy. Love doesn't brag, and isn't proud, 
doesn't behave badly, doesn't push for its own way, isn't easily provoked, keeps no record of wrongs; 
doesn't celebrate over wrong, but celebrates with the truth; 
puts up with all things, trusts all things, expects all things, and sticks with all things. 
Love never fails. But where there's speaking for God, it'll be put out of work. Where there are languages, they'll stop. Where there's knowledge, it'll be put out of work. 
We know in part and we speak for God in part; 
but when that which is complete has come, then what's partial will be put out of work. 
When I was a child, I spoke as a child, I felt as a child, I thought as a child. Now that I have become a man, I have put away childish things. 
Now we see in a mirror, dimly, but then face to face. Now I know in part, but then I'll know fully, just as I was fully known. 
But now trust, expectation, and love remain, these three. The greatest of these is love.
