So Abram went up from Egypt into the south, with his wife and everything he had, and Lot went with him. 
Abram was very rich in livestock, in silver, and in gold. 
He journeyed from the south as far as Bethel, to the place where his tent had been at first, between Bethel and Ai, 
to the place of the altar he had built there at first. There Abram called on God's name. 
Lot also, who went with Abram, had flocks, herds, and tents. 
The land couldn't support them both, because their possessions were so great that they couldn't stay together. 
There was quarreling between Abram's herdsmen and Lot's herdsmen. The Canaanites and the Perizzites lived in the land at that time. 
Abram said to Lot, "Please, let there be no quarreling between you and me, or between your herdsmen and mine, because we're relatives. 
Isn't the whole land open before you? Please separate from me. If you go to the left, I'll go to the right. If you go to the right, I'll go to the left." 
Lot looked up and saw that the whole plain of the Jordan was well watered everywhere, like the garden of God, like the land of Egypt, as far as Zoar. This was before God destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah. 
So Lot chose the whole plain of the Jordan for himself and traveled east, and they separated from each other. 
Abram lived in the land of Canaan, and Lot lived in the cities of the plain, and moved his tent as far as Sodom. 
Now the men of Sodom were deeply guilty and did great wrong against God. 
God said to Abram, after Lot had separated from him, "Now look up from where you're, and look north, south, east, and west, 
for I'll give all the land which you see to you and to your offspring for the eras. 
I'll make your offspring as the dust of the earth, so that if a man can count the dust of the earth, then your offspring may also be counted. 
Get up and walk through the land, its length and its width, because I'll give it to you." 
Abram moved his tent and came to live by the great trees of Mamre at Hebron, and he built an altar there to God.
