The Israelites set out and camped on the plains of Moab across the Jordan from Jericho. 
Balak son of Zippor saw everything Israel had done to the Amorites. 
Moab was terrified of the people because there were so many, and Moab was sick with dread because of the Israelites. 
Moab said to the elders of Midian, "This horde is going to lick up everything around us the way an ox licks up the grass of the field." Balak son of Zippor was king of Moab at that time. 
He sent messengers to Balaam son of Beor at Pethor, which is by the River, in the land of his people, to summon him, saying, "Look, a people has come out of Egypt, and they cover the face of the land, and they are camped opposite me. 
Please come and curse these people for me, because they are too strong for me. Maybe then I can defeat them and drive them out of the land, because I know that whoever you bless is blessed, and whoever you curse is cursed." 
The elders of Moab and Midian left with the payment for divination in their hands, and they came to Balaam and gave him Balak's message. 
He said to them, "Stay here tonight and I will bring you back word, whatever God says to me." So the leaders of Moab stayed with Balaam. 
God came to Balaam and said, "Who are these men with you?" 
Balaam said to God, "Balak son of Zippor, king of Moab, sent me this message: 
'Look, the people that came out of Egypt cover the face of the land. Come now and curse them for me. Maybe then I can fight them and drive them out.'" 
God said to Balaam, "Don't go with them. Don't curse the people, because they are blessed." 
Balaam got up in the morning and said to Balak's leaders, "Go back to your land, because God won't let me go with you." 
The leaders of Moab got up and went to Balak and said, "Balaam refuses to come with us." 
Balak sent more leaders, more important than the first ones. 
They came to Balaam and said, "This is what Balak son of Zippor says: 'Please don't let anything stop you from coming to me, 
because I will honor you greatly, and I will do whatever you say. Please come and curse these people for me.'" 
Balaam answered Balak's servants, "Even if Balak gave me his house full of silver and gold, I couldn't go beyond the word of my God, either less or more. 
But please stay here tonight too, so I can find out what else God will say to me." 
God came to Balaam at night and said, "If the men have come to call you, get up and go with them, but you are to do only what I tell you." 
Balaam got up in the morning, saddled his donkey and went with the leaders of Moab. 
God's anger burned because he went, and God's messenger stood in the road to block him. Balaam was riding his donkey, with his two servants. 
The donkey saw God's messenger standing in the road with a drawn sword, and the donkey turned off the road into a field. Balaam beat her to get her back on the road. 
Then God's messenger stood in a narrow path between two vineyards, with a wall on each side. 
The donkey saw God's messenger and crushed Balaam's foot against the wall, and he beat her again. 
God's messenger went further and stood in a narrow place where there was no room to turn either right or left. 
The donkey saw God's messenger and lay down under Balaam, and Balaam's anger burned and he beat her with his staff. 
Then God opened the donkey's mouth, and she said to Balaam, "What have I done to you, that you have beaten me three times?" 
Balaam said to the donkey, "Because you made a fool of me. If I had a sword in my hand, I would kill you right now." 
The donkey said to Balaam, "Aren't I your donkey, the one you have ridden all your life until today? Have I ever done this to you before?" He said, "No." 
Then God opened Balaam's eyes, and he saw God's messenger standing in the road with a drawn sword, and he bowed his head and fell face down. 
God's messenger said to him, "Why have you beaten your donkey these three times? I have come out to block you, because your way is twisted in front of me. 
The donkey saw me and turned away from me these three times. If she hadn't turned away, I would have killed you by now and let her live." 
Balaam said to God's messenger, "I have drifted, because I didn't know you were standing in the road against me. If this is wrong, I will go back." 
God's messenger said to Balaam, "Go with the men, but you are to say only what I tell you." So Balaam went with Balak's leaders. 
When Balak heard that Balaam had come, he went out to meet him at Ir Moab, on the Arnon border, at the far edge of the territory. 
Balak said to Balaam, "Didn't I send for you urgently? Why didn't you come to me? Am I not able to honor you?" 
Balaam said to Balak, "Look, I have come to you. Do I have any power to say anything at all? I will say only what God puts in my mouth." 
Balaam went with Balak, and they came to Kiriath Huzoth. 
Balak sacrificed cattle and sheep, and sent some to Balaam and the leaders with him. 
In the morning Balak took Balaam up to the high places of Baal, and from there he could see part of the people.
