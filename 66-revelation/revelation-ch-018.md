After this I saw another messenger coming down out of the sky with great authority, and the land was lit up by his weight. 
He called out in a mighty voice, saying, "Babylon the great has fallen! She has become a home for unclean powers, a prison for every unclean spirit, and a cage for every unclean and hated bird! 
For all the nations have drunk the wine of the heat of her sleeping around, the kings of the land slept around with her, and the merchants of the land grew rich from her great luxury." 
Then I heard another voice from the sky saying, "Come out of her, my people, so that you have no part in her mistakes and do not share in her plagues, 
because her mistakes pile up as high as the sky, and God has remembered her wrongs. 
Pay her back as she has paid others. Pay her back double for what she has done, and by her own deeds. In the cup she mixed, mix her a double portion. 
As much as she lived it up and gave herself to luxury, give her that much anguish and mourning. She says in her heart, 'I sit here a queen. I am no widow, and I will never see mourning.' 
So in a single day her plagues will come: death, mourning, and famine, and she will be burned up with fire, because the Lord God who passed a verdict on her is strong. 
The kings of the land who slept around with her and lived in luxury with her will weep and wail over her when they see the smoke of her burning. 
They will stand far off because they are afraid of her anguish, saying, 'How terrible, how terrible for the great city, Babylon, the strong city! Your verdict came in a single hour.' 
The merchants of the land weep and mourn over her, because no one buys their goods any more: 
goods of gold, silver, precious stones, pearls, fine linen, purple, silk, scarlet, all kinds of scented wood, every article of ivory, every article made of the finest wood, and of bronze, iron, and marble; 
and cinnamon, incense, perfume, frankincense, wine, olive oil, fine flour, wheat, cattle, sheep, horses, chariots, and the bodies and lives of people. 
The ripe fruit your heart longed for is gone from you. Everything rich and splendid has vanished from you, and you will never find any of it again. 
The merchants who sold these things, who grew rich from her, will stand far off because they are afraid of her anguish, weeping and mourning, 
saying, 'How terrible, how terrible for the great city, dressed in fine linen, purple, and scarlet, covered with gold, precious stones, and pearls! 
In a single hour such great wealth has been wiped out!' Every ship captain and everyone who sails anywhere, and the sailors, and everyone who makes their living on the sea, stood far off 
and cried out as they watched the smoke of her burning, saying, 'What city was ever like this great city?' 
They threw dust on their heads and cried out, weeping and mourning, saying, 'How terrible, how terrible for the great city, where everyone who had ships on the sea grew rich from her great wealth! In a single hour she has been wiped out!' 
"Celebrate over her, you sky, and you set-apart ones, sent ones, and spokesmen, because God has passed your verdict on her." 
A mighty messenger picked up a stone like a great millstone and threw it into the sea, saying, "That is how Babylon, the great city, will be thrown down, and it will never be found again. 
The music of harpists and singers, of flute players and trumpeters, will never be heard in you again. No craftsman of any trade will ever be found in you again. The sound of a mill will never be heard in you again. 
The light of a lamp will never shine in you again. The voices of bride and bridegroom will never be heard in you again, because your merchants were the great ones of the land, and by your drug-magic all the nations were led astray. 
In her was found the blood of spokesmen and of set-apart ones, and of everyone who has been killed on the land."
