When all the kings west of the Jordan heard about it, in the hill country, in the lowlands and along the whole coast of the Great Sea toward Lebanon, the Hittites, Amorites, Canaanites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites, 
they gathered together with one purpose, to fight Joshua and Israel. 
But when the people of Gibeon heard what Joshua had done to Jericho and Ai, 
they came up with a plan. They pretended to be messengers, and loaded their donkeys with worn-out sacks and old wineskins that were torn and patched up. 
They put worn-out and patched sandals on their feet and wore old clothes, and all the bread they had with them was dry and crumbling. 
They went to Joshua at the camp at Gilgal and said to him and the men of Israel, "We have come from a far country. Now make a treaty with us." 
The men of Israel said to the Hivites, "Maybe you live right here among us. How could we make a treaty with you?" 
They said to Joshua, "We are your servants." Joshua asked them, "Who are you, and where do you come from?" 
They said to him, "Your servants have come from a very far country because of the name of your God. We have heard of his fame and everything he did in Egypt, 
and everything he did to the two kings of the Amorites east of the Jordan, to Sihon king of Heshbon and Og king of Bashan, who lived at Ashtaroth. 
Our elders and everyone in our country told us, 'Take supplies for the journey, go and meet them and say, "We are your servants. Now make a treaty with us."' 
This bread was still warm when we packed it at home the day we set out to come to you, and now look, it is dry and crumbling. 
These wineskins were new when we filled them, and now look, they are split. And our clothes and sandals are worn out from the very long journey." 
The men looked over their supplies but didn't ask God for advice. 
So Joshua made peace with them and made a treaty with them to let them live, and the leaders of the community swore an oath to them. 
Three days after they made the treaty, they heard that these people were their neighbors and lived right among them. 
So the Israelites set out and came to their cities on the third day. Their cities were Gibeon, Chephirah, Beeroth and Kiriath Jearim. 
But the Israelites didn't attack them, because the leaders of the community had sworn to them by the God of Israel. So the whole community grumbled against the leaders. 
But all the leaders said to the whole community, "We have sworn to them by the God of Israel, so we can't touch them now. 
This is what we will do: let them live, so that anger doesn't come on us because of the oath we swore to them." 
The leaders said, "Let them live." So they became woodcutters and water carriers for the whole community, as the leaders had said. 
Then Joshua called them in and said, "Why did you lie to us and say, 'We are very far from you,' when you live among us? 
From now on you are under a curse. You will never stop being slaves, woodcutters and water carriers for the house of my God." 
They answered Joshua, "Your servants were told plainly how your God commanded his servant Moses to give you all the land and to destroy everyone living in it ahead of you. So we were terrified for our lives because of you, and that is why we did this. 
Now look, we are in your hands. Do to us whatever seems good and right to you." 
So Joshua did that, and saved them from the Israelites, so they didn't kill them. 
That day Joshua made them woodcutters and water carriers for the community and for the altar of God, which they still are today, in the place he would choose.
