Now concerning the things about which you wrote to me: it's good for a man not to touch a woman. 
But because of so much sleeping around, let each man have his own wife, and each woman her own husband. 
Let the husband give his wife the affection owed her, and likewise also the wife her husband. 
The wife doesn't have authority over her own body, but the husband does. Likewise also the husband doesn't have authority over his own body, but the wife does. 
Don't hold back from one another, unless you both agree for a time, so that you can give yourselves to going without food and prayer, and then be together again, so that the Adversary doesn't test you because of your lack of self-control. 
But this I say by way of concession, not of commandment. 
Yet I wish that everyone was like me. But each person has his own gift from God, one of one kind, and another of another. 
But I say to the unmarried and to widows, it's good for them if they remain even as I am. 
But if they don't have self-control, let them marry. It's better to marry than to burn with passion. 
But to the married I command, not I but the Lord, that the wife not leave her husband 
(but if she does leave, let her stay unmarried, or else come back together with her husband), and that the husband not leave his wife. 
But to the rest I say, not the Lord, if any brother has a wife who doesn't trust, and she's willing to live with him, let him not leave her. 
And the woman who has a husband who doesn't trust, and he's willing to live with her, let her not leave her husband. 
The husband who doesn't trust is set apart through his wife, and the wife who doesn't trust is set apart through her husband. Otherwise your children would be unclean, but as it's they're set apart. 
Yet if the one who doesn't trust leaves, let them go. The brother or the sister isn't bound in such cases, but God has called us to wholeness. 
How do you know, wife, whether you'll make your husband whole? Or how do you know, husband, whether you'll make your wife whole? 
Only let each person walk the way the Lord has given him, and the way God has called him. So I command in all the gatherings. 
Was anyone called who was circumcised? Let him not try to undo it. Has anyone been called who wasn't circumcised? Let him not be circumcised. 
Being circumcised is nothing, and not being circumcised is nothing, but keeping God's commands is what matters. 
Let each person stay in the calling in which he was called. 
Were you called as a slave? Don't let that bother you, but if you can become free, take it. 
The one who was called in the Lord as a slave is the Lord's free person. Likewise the one who was called free is Christ's slave. 
You were bought with a price. Don't become slaves of people. 
Brothers, let each person stay with God in whatever condition he was called. 
Now about virgins, I have no command from the Lord, but I give my opinion as one who has been shown mercy by the Lord and can be trusted. 
So I think that because of the distress that's on us, it's good for a man to remain as he is. 
Are you bound to a wife? Don't seek to be freed. Are you free from a wife? Don't seek a wife. 
But if you do marry, you haven't drifted. And if a virgin marries, she hasn't drifted. Yet those who marry will have pressure in the flesh, and I want to spare you. 
But I say this, brothers: the time is short. From now on, both those who have wives may be as though they had none; 
and those who weep, as though they didn't weep; and those who celebrate, as though they didn't celebrate; and those who buy, as though they didn't possess; 
and those who use the world, as though they weren't using it to the full. The shape of this world is passing away. 
But I want you to be free from worries. The unmarried man is concerned with the things of the Lord, how he can please the Lord; 
but he who's married is concerned about the things of the world, how he can please his wife. 
There's also a difference between a wife and a virgin. The unmarried woman cares about the things of the Lord, so that she can be set apart in both body and spirit. But the married woman cares about the things of the world: how she can please her husband. 
This I say for your own benefit, not that I can ensnare you, but for that which is appropriate, and that you can attend to the Lord without distraction. 
But if anyone thinks he's behaving improperly toward the woman he's pledged to, and she's past the age for marrying, and it seems necessary, let him do what he wants. He isn't drifting. Let them marry. 
But the one who stands firm in his heart, under no pressure, but has control over his own will, and has decided in his own heart to keep the woman he's pledged to, does well. 
So then the one who gives her in marriage does well, and the one who doesn't give her in marriage does better. 
A wife is bound as long as her husband lives; but if her husband dies, she's free to marry anyone she wishes, only in the Lord. 
But she's more flourishing if she stays as she is, in my opinion, and I think that I also have God's Spirit.
