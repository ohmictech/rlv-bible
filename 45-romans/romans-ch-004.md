What then will we say that Abraham, our forefather, has found according to the flesh? 
For if Abraham was made right by deeds, he has something to boast about, but not toward God. 
For what does the Scripture say? "Abraham trusted God, and it was credited to him as rightness." 
Now to him who works, the earned result is not counted as a gift, but as something owed. 
But to him who doesn't work, but trusts in him who makes the one with no regard for God right, his trust is credited as rightness. 
Even as David also pronounces flourishing on the one to whom God credits rightness apart from deeds: 
"Flourishing are they whose wrongs are released, whose mistakes are covered. 
Flourishing is the one whom the Lord will by no means charge with the drift." 
Is this flourishing then pronounced only on the circumcised, or on the uncircumcised also? For we say that trust was credited to Abraham as rightness. 
How then was it counted? When he was circumcised, or when he was not? Not when he was circumcised, but when he was not. 
He received the sign of circumcision, a seal of the rightness of the trust he had while he was not yet circumcised, so that he would be the father of everyone who trusts, though they are not circumcised, so that rightness would also be credited to them. 
and he is the father of the circumcised, for those who are not only circumcised, but who also walk in the steps of that trust of our father Abraham, which he had while he was not yet circumcised. 
For the promise to Abraham and to his offspring that he would be heir of the world wasn't through the law, but through the rightness of trust. 
For if those who are of the law are heirs, trust is made void, and the promise is made of no effect. 
For the law produces heat; for where there is no law, neither is there disobedience. 
For this cause it is of trust, that it may be according to favor, to the end that the promise may be sure to all the offspring, not to that only which is of the law, but to that also which is of the trust of Abraham, who is the father of us all. 
As it is written, "I have made you a father of many nations." This is in the presence of him whom he trusted: God, who gives life to the dead, and calls the things that are not, as though they were. 
Against expectation, Abraham in expectation trusted, so that he would become a father of many nations, according to that which had been spoken, "So will your offspring be." 
Without being weakened in trust, he didn't consider his own body, already having been worn out, (he being about a hundred years old), and the deadness of Sarah's womb. 
Yet, looking to the promise of God, he didn't waver through lack of trust, but grew strong through trust, giving honor to God, 
and being fully assured that what he had promised, he was also able to perform. 
Therefore it also was "credited to him for rightness." 
Now it was not written that it was accounted to him for his sake alone, 
but for our sake also, to whom it will be accounted, who trust in him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead, 
who was handed over for our false steps, and was raised for our being made right.
