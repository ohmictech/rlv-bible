God spoke to Moses: 
"Speak to the Israelites and tell them, 'When someone makes a special vow to God setting apart a person, the value is to be set by your valuation. 
Your valuation for a male from twenty to sixty years old is fifty shekels of silver, by the sanctuary standard. 
If it is a female, your valuation is thirty shekels. 
From five to twenty years old, your valuation is twenty shekels for a male and ten shekels for a female. 
From one month to five years old, your valuation is five shekels of silver for a male and three shekels of silver for a female. 
From sixty years old and up, your valuation is fifteen shekels for a male and ten shekels for a female. 
But if the person is too poor for your valuation, he is to be brought to the priest, and the priest will set the value according to what the person can afford. 
"'If it is an animal that people may bring as an offering to God, anything given to God from such animals becomes set apart. 
He is not to swap it or exchange it, a good one for a bad one or a bad one for a good one. If he does exchange one animal for another, both of them become set apart. 
If it is an unclean animal that may not be brought as an offering to God, he is to bring the animal to the priest, 
and the priest will set its value, whether it is good or bad. It stands at whatever the priest values it at. 
If the owner wants it back, he is to add a fifth to its value. 
"'When someone sets apart his house as holy to God, the priest is to set its value, whether it is good or bad. It stands at whatever the priest values it at. 
If the one who set it apart wants his house back, he is to add a fifth to the money of its valuation, and it will be his. 
"'If someone sets apart part of a field he owns to God, its value is to be set by how much seed it takes: a homer of barley seed is worth fifty shekels of silver. 
If he sets the field apart starting from the Year of Jubilee, it stands at your valuation. 
But if he sets the field apart after the Jubilee, the priest is to figure out the money by the years left until the Year of Jubilee, and the value is to be reduced. 
If the one who set the field apart wants it back, he is to add a fifth to the money of its valuation, and it will stay his. 
If he doesn't buy the field back, or if he has sold it to someone else, it can't be bought back any more. 
When it is released in the Jubilee, the field becomes holy to God, a field given over to him, and it becomes the priests' property. 
"'If someone sets apart to God a field he has bought, which is not part of his family's land, 
the priest is to work out its value up to the Year of Jubilee, and he is to pay that amount on that day as something set apart to God. 
In the Year of Jubilee the field returns to the man he bought it from, to the one whose family land it is. 
All your values are to be set by the sanctuary standard, twenty gerahs to the shekel. 
"'But no one may set apart the firstborn of an animal, since the firstborn already belongs to God, whether an ox or a sheep. It is God's. 
If it is an unclean animal, he may buy it back at your valuation plus a fifth of it, and if it isn't bought back, it is to be sold at your valuation. 
"'But nothing that someone has given over to God from what he owns, whether a person, an animal or family land, may be sold or bought back. Everything given over to God is most holy to God. 
"'No one who has been given over to destruction from among people can be bought back. He is to be put to death. 
"'All the tithe of the land, whether from the seed of the land or the fruit of the trees, belongs to God. It is set apart to God. 
If someone wants to buy back part of his tithe, he is to add a fifth to it. 
Every tenth animal from the herds and flocks, whichever passes under the shepherd's rod, is set apart to God. 
He is not to check whether it is good or bad or exchange it. If he does exchange it, both it and the exchange become set apart. It can't be bought back.'" 
These are the commands God gave Moses for the Israelites on Mount Sinai.
