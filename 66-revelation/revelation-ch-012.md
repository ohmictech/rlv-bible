A great sign appeared in the sky: a woman dressed with the sun, with the moon under her feet and a wreath of twelve stars on her head. 
She was pregnant, and she cried out in pain as she labored to give birth. 
Then another sign appeared in the sky: a huge red dragon with seven heads and ten horns, and seven crowns on its heads. 
Its tail swept away a third of the stars of the sky and threw them to the land. The dragon stood in front of the woman who was about to give birth, so that it could swallow her child the moment it was born. 
She gave birth to a son, a male child, who is going to shepherd all the nations with an iron rod. Her child was caught up to God and to his throne. 
The woman fled into the desert, where God had prepared a place for her, so that she could be taken care of there for one thousand two hundred sixty days. 
Then war broke out in the sky. Michael and his messengers fought against the dragon, and the dragon and his messengers fought back, 
but they did not win, and there was no longer any place for them in the sky. 
The great dragon was thrown down, that ancient snake, the one called the Slanderer and the Adversary, who deceives the whole world. He was thrown down to the land, and his messengers were thrown down with him. 
Then I heard a loud voice in the sky saying, "Now rescue, power, and the reign of our God, and the authority of his Christ, have come, because the one who accuses our brothers and sisters has been thrown down, the one who accused them in front of our God day and night. 
They won the victory over him by the Lamb's blood and by the witness they bore. They did not love their life so much as to shrink from death. 
So celebrate, you skies and everyone who lives in them! But how terrible for the land and the sea, because the Slanderer has come down to you in great heat, knowing that he has only a short time." 
When the dragon saw that it had been thrown down to the land, it hunted down the woman who had given birth to the male child. 
The woman was given the two wings of a great eagle, so that she could fly to her place in the desert, where she would be taken care of for a time, times, and half a time, out of the snake's reach. 
The snake poured water out of its mouth like a river after the woman, to sweep her away with the flood. 
But the land helped the woman: it opened its mouth and swallowed up the river the dragon had poured out of its mouth. 
Then the dragon was furious with the woman and went off to make war on the rest of her children, who keep God's commands and hold to the witness Jesus gave.
