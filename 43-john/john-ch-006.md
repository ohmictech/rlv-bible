After these things, Jesus went away to the other side of the lake of Galilee, which is also called the lake of Tiberias. 
A great crowd followed him, because they saw his signs which he did on those who were sick. 
Jesus went up into the mountain, and he sat there with his students. 
Now the Jewish Passover was near. 
Jesus, lifting up his eyes and seeing that a great crowd was coming to him, said to Philip, "Where are we to buy bread, that these may eat?" 
He said this to test him, for he himself knew what he'd do. 
Philip answered him, "Two hundred silver coins worth of bread isn't sufficient for them, that every one of them may receive a little." 
One of his students, Andrew, Simon Peter's brother, said to him, 
"There's a boy here who has five barley loaves and two fish, but what are these among so many?" 
Jesus said, "Have the people sit down." Now there was much grass in that place. So the men sat down, in number about five thousand. 
Jesus took the loaves, and having given thanks, he distributed to those who were sitting down, likewise also of the fish as much as they desired. 
When they were filled, he said to his students, "Gather up the broken pieces which are left over, that nothing be lost." 
So they gathered them up, and filled twelve baskets with broken pieces from the five barley loaves, which were left over by those who had eaten. 
When the people saw the sign which Jesus did, they said, "This is truly the spokesman who comes into the world." 
Jesus, perceiving that they were about to come and take him by force to make him king, withdrew again to the mountain by himself. 
When evening came, his students went down to the lake. 
They entered into the boat, and were going over the lake to Capernaum. It was now dark, and Jesus hadn't come to them. 
A strong wind was blowing and the lake was rough. 
When they had rowed about three or four miles, they saw Jesus walking on the lake and drawing near to the boat; and they were afraid. 
But he said to them, "It's I. Don't be afraid." 
They were glad to receive him into the boat. Immediately the boat was at the land where they were going. 
On the next day, the crowd that stood on the other side of the lake saw that there was no other boat there, except the one in which his students had embarked, and that Jesus hadn't entered with his students into the boat, but his students had gone away alone. 
However, boats from Tiberias came near to the place where they ate the bread after the Lord had given thanks. 
When the crowd saw that Jesus wasn't there, nor his students, they themselves got into the boats and came to Capernaum, seeking Jesus. 
When they found him on the other side of the sea, they asked him, "Rabbi, when did you come here?" 
Jesus answered them, "Truly I tell you, you seek me, not because you saw signs, but because you ate of the loaves and were filled. 
Don't work for the food which goes bad, but for the food which remains to the real life of the era, which the Human One will give to you. God the Father has sealed him." 
They said to him, "What must we do, that we may do the deeds of God?" 
Jesus answered them, "This is the deed of God, that you trust in him whom he has sent." 
They said to him, "What then do you do for a sign, that we may see and trust you? What deed do you do? 
Our fathers ate the manna in the wilderness. As it's written, 'He gave them bread out of the sky to eat.'" 
Jesus said to them, "Truly I tell you, it wasn't Moses who gave you the bread out of the sky, but my Father gives you the true bread out of the sky. 
The bread of God is that which comes down out of the sky and gives life to the world." 
They said to him, "Lord, always give us this bread." 
Jesus said to them, "I am the bread of life. Whoever comes to me won't be hungry, and whoever trusts in me will never be thirsty. 
But I told you that you have seen me, and yet you don't trust. 
All those whom the Father gives me will come to me. Whoever comes to me I'll in no way throw out. 
I have come down from the sky, not to do my own will, but the will of him who sent me. 
This is the will of my Father who sent me, that of all he has given to me I should lose nothing, but should raise it up at the last day. 
This is the will of the one who sent me, that everyone who sees the Son and trusts in him should have the real life of the era; and I'll raise them up at the last day." 
The Jewish crowd grumbled concerning him, because he said, "I am the bread which came down out of the sky." 
They said, "Isn't this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know? How then does he say, 'I have come down out of the sky'?" 
So Jesus answered them, "Don't grumble among yourselves. 
No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws them; and I'll raise them up at the last day. 
It's written in the spokesmen, 'They'll all be taught by God.' Therefore everyone who hears from the Father and has learned, comes to me. 
Not that anyone has seen the Father, except he who's from God. He has seen the Father. 
Truly I tell you, whoever trusts in me has the real life of the era. 
I am the bread of life. 
Your fathers ate the manna in the wilderness and they died. 
This is the bread which comes down out of the sky, that anyone may eat of it and not die. 
I am the living bread which came down out of the sky. If anyone eats of this bread, they'll live forever. Yes, the bread which I'll give for the life of the world is my flesh." 
The Jewish crowd contended with one another, saying, "How can this man give us his flesh to eat?" 
Jesus said to them, "Truly I tell you, unless you eat the flesh of the Human One and drink his blood, you don't have life in yourselves. 
Whoever eats my flesh and drinks my blood has the real life of the era, and I'll raise them up at the last day. 
My flesh is food indeed, and my blood is drink indeed. 
Whoever eats my flesh and drinks my blood makes their home in me, and I in them. 
As the living Father sent me, and I truly live because of the Father, so whoever feeds on me will also truly live because of me. 
This is the bread which came down out of the sky, not as our fathers ate the manna and died. Whoever eats this bread will live forever." 
He said these things in the gathering place, as he taught in Capernaum. 
So many of his students, when they heard this, said, "This is a hard saying! Who can listen to it?" 
But Jesus knowing in himself that his students grumbled at this, said to them, "Does this trap you? 
Then what if you'd see the Human One ascending to where he was before? 
It's the spirit who gives life. The flesh profits nothing. The words that I speak to you're spirit, and are life. 
But there are some of you who don't trust." For Jesus knew from the beginning who they were who didn't trust, and who it was who would hand him over. 
He said, "That's why I have said to you that no one can come to me, unless it's given to them by my Father." 
At this, many of his students went back and walked no more with him. 
Jesus said to the twelve, "You don't also want to go away, do you?" 
Simon Peter answered him, "Lord, to whom would we go? You have the words of the real life of the era. 
We have come to trust and know that you're the Christ, the Son of the living God." 
Jesus answered them, "Didn't I choose you, the twelve, and one of you is a slanderer?" 
Now he spoke of Judas, the son of Simon Iscariot, for it was he who would hand him over, being one of the twelve.
