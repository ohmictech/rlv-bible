Don't speak harshly to an older man; urge him on as you would a father. Treat younger men as brothers, 
older women as mothers, and younger women as sisters, with all purity. 
Honor widows who really are widows. 
But if a widow has children or grandchildren, those younger ones should learn first to show devotion to their own family and to give back to their parents, because this is what pleases God. 
The widow who really is one, and has been left all alone, has set her expectation on God and keeps on with requests and prayers night and day. 
But the one who lives for pleasure is dead even while she is alive. 
Give these instructions too, so that no one can be blamed. 
But if anyone doesn't provide for his own people, and especially for his own household, he has turned his back on the trust and is worse than someone who has no trust. 
No one should be put on the list of widows unless she is at least sixty, and has been faithful to one husband, 
and has a good name for good deeds, if she has raised children, if she has welcomed strangers, if she has washed the feet of the set-apart ones, if she has helped people in hard times, and if she has gone after every good work. 
But don't put younger widows on the list; when their desires turn away from Christ, they want to marry, 
and so they come under a verdict, because they have set aside the first promise they made. 
And along with that they learn to be idle, going from house to house. And not only idle, but also tattlers and people sticking their noses into everything, saying things they shouldn't. 
So I want younger widows to marry, to have children, to run their households, and to give the one who opposes us nothing to say against them. 
Some have already turned away to follow the Adversary. 
If anyone who trusts has widows in the family, they should help them, and not let the gathering be weighed down, so that it can help the widows who really are widows. 
The elders who lead well should be counted worthy of double honor, especially those who work hard at speaking and teaching. 
The Scripture says, "You shall not muzzle the ox while it treads out the grain," and, "The worker deserves his pay." 
Don't take up an accusation against an elder unless two or three witnesses bring it. 
Show those who go on drifting their fault in front of everyone, so that the rest may be afraid too. 
Before God and Christ Jesus and the chosen messengers, I command you to keep these things without prejudging anyone, and to do nothing out of favoritism. 
Don't lay hands on anyone in a hurry, and don't share in other people's mistakes. Keep yourself pure. 
Stop drinking only water; use a little wine for your stomach and for the illnesses you keep having. 
Some people's mistakes are plain to see and go ahead of them to the verdict; other people's catch up afterward. 
The same is true of good deeds: some are obvious, and even the ones that aren't can't stay hidden.
