When all the kings of the Amorites west of the Jordan and all the kings of the Canaanites along the coast heard how God had dried up the water of the Jordan ahead of the Israelites until we had crossed over, their hearts melted and there was no courage left in them because of the Israelites. 
At that time God said to Joshua, "Make flint knives and circumcise the Israelites a second time." 
So Joshua made flint knives and circumcised the Israelites at the hill of the foreskins. 
This is why Joshua circumcised them: all the men who came out of Egypt who were old enough to fight had died in the wilderness on the way after they left Egypt. 
Everyone who came out had been circumcised, but none of those born in the wilderness on the way out of Egypt had been. 
The Israelites walked in the wilderness forty years, until the whole generation of fighting men who came out of Egypt had died out, because they didn't listen to the voice of God. God had sworn to them that he wouldn't let them see the land he swore to their fathers to give us, a land flowing with milk and honey. 
So he raised up their children in their place, and Joshua circumcised them, because they were uncircumcised, since nobody had circumcised them on the way. 
When the whole nation had been circumcised, they stayed where they were in the camp until they were healed. 
God said to Joshua, "Today I have rolled away the shame of Egypt from you." That is why the place is called Gilgal to this day. 
The Israelites camped at Gilgal and kept the Passover on the evening of the fourteenth day of the month in the plains of Jericho. 
The day after the Passover they ate bread made without yeast and roasted grain from the crops of the land. 
The manna stopped the next day, after they ate the crops of the land. The Israelites had no more manna, and that year they ate the produce of the land of Canaan. 
When Joshua was near Jericho, he looked up and saw a man standing in front of him with his sword drawn in his hand. Joshua went to him and asked, "Are you for us or for our enemies?" 
He said, "Neither. I have come now as commander of the army of God." Joshua fell face down to the ground and worshiped, and asked him, "What does my lord want to say to his servant?" 
The commander of the army of God said to Joshua, "Take off your sandals, because the place where you are standing is holy." And Joshua did.
