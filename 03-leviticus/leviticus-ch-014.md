God spoke to Moses, saying, 
"This will be the law of the leper in the day of his cleansing: He will be brought to the priest, 
and the priest will go out of the camp. The priest will examine him. Look, if the plague of leprosy is healed in the leper, 
then the priest will command them to take for him who's to be cleansed two living clean birds, cedar wood, scarlet, and hyssop. 
The priest will command them to kill one of the birds in an earthen vessel over running water. 
As for the living bird, he will take it, the cedar wood, the scarlet, and the hyssop, and will dip them and the living bird in the blood of the bird that was killed over the running water. 
He will sprinkle on him who's to be cleansed from the leprosy seven times, and will pronounce him clean, and will let the living bird go into the open field. 
"He who's to be cleansed will wash his clothes, and shave off all his hair, and bathe himself in water; and he will be clean. After that he will come into the camp, but will dwell outside his tent seven days. 
It will be on the seventh day, that he will shave all his hair off his head and his beard and his eyebrows. He will shave off all his hair. He will wash his clothes, and he will bathe his body in water. Then he will be clean. 
"On the eighth day he will take two male lambs without defect, one ewe lamb a year old without defect, three tenths of an ephah of fine flour for a meal offering, mixed with oil, and one log of oil. 
The priest who cleanses him will set the man who's to be cleansed, and those things, before God, at the door of the Tent of Meeting. 
"The priest will take one of the male lambs, and offer him for a trespass offering, with the log of oil, and wave them for a wave offering before God. 
He will kill the male lamb in the place where they kill the offering for wrongdoing and the burnt offering, in the place of the sanctuary; for as the offering for wrongdoing is the priest's, so is the trespass offering. It's most holy. 
The priest will take some of the blood of the trespass offering, and the priest will put it on the tip of the right ear of him who's to be cleansed, and on the thumb of his right hand, and on the big toe of his right foot. 
The priest will take some of the log of oil, and pour it into the palm of his own left hand. 
The priest will dip his right finger in the oil that's in his left hand, and will sprinkle some of the oil with his finger seven times before God. 
The priest will put some of the rest of the oil that's in his hand on the tip of the right ear of him who's to be cleansed, and on the thumb of his right hand, and on the big toe of his right foot, upon the blood of the trespass offering. 
The rest of the oil that's in the priest's hand he will put on the head of him who's to be cleansed, and the priest will make things right for him before God. 
"The priest will offer the offering for wrongdoing, and make things right for him who's to be cleansed because of his uncleanness. Afterward he will kill the burnt offering; 
then the priest will offer the burnt offering and the meal offering on the altar. The priest will make things right for him, and he will be clean. 
"If he's poor, and can't afford so much, then he will take one male lamb for a trespass offering to be waved, to make things right for him, and one tenth of an ephah of fine flour mixed with oil for a meal offering, and a log of oil; 
and two turtledoves, or two young pigeons, such as he's able to afford; and the one will be an offering for wrongdoing, and the other a burnt offering. 
"On the eighth day he will bring them for his cleansing to the priest, to the door of the Tent of Meeting, before God. 
The priest will take the lamb of the trespass offering, and the log of oil, and the priest will wave them for a wave offering before God. 
He will kill the lamb of the trespass offering. The priest will take some of the blood of the trespass offering and put it on the tip of the right ear of him who's to be cleansed, and on the thumb of his right hand, and on the big toe of his right foot. 
The priest will pour some of the oil into the palm of his own left hand; 
and the priest will sprinkle with his right finger some of the oil that's in his left hand seven times before God. 
Then the priest will put some of the oil that's in his hand on the tip of the right ear of him who's to be cleansed, and on the thumb of his right hand, and on the big toe of his right foot, on the place of the blood of the trespass offering. 
The rest of the oil that's in the priest's hand he will put on the head of him who's to be cleansed, to make things right for him before God. 
He will offer one of the turtledoves, or of the young pigeons, which ever he's able to afford, 
of the kind he's able to afford, the one for an offering for wrongdoing, and the other for a burnt offering, with the meal offering. The priest will make things right for him who's to be cleansed before God." 
This is the law for him in whom is the plague of leprosy, who isn't able to afford the sacrifice for his cleansing. 
God spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying, 
"When you have come into the land of Canaan, which I give to you for a possession, and I put a spreading mildew in a house in the land of your possession, 
then he who owns the house will come and tell the priest, saying, 'There seems to me to be some sort of plague in the house.' 
The priest will command that they empty the house, before the priest goes in to examine the plague, that all that's in the house not be made unclean. Afterward the priest will go in to inspect the house. 
He will examine the plague; and look, if the plague is in the walls of the house with hollow streaks, greenish or reddish, and it appears to be deeper than the wall, 
then the priest will go out of the house to the door of the house, and shut up the house seven days. 
The priest will come again on the seventh day, and look. If the plague has spread in the walls of the house, 
then the priest will command that they take out the stones in which is the plague, and cast them into an unclean place outside of the city. 
He will cause the inside of the house to be scraped all over. They will pour out the mortar that they scraped off outside of the city into an unclean place. 
They will take other stones, and put them in the place of those stones; and he will take other mortar, and will plaster the house. 
"If the plague comes again, and breaks out in the house after he has taken out the stones, and after he has scraped the house, and after it was plastered, 
then the priest will come in and look; and look, if the plague has spread in the house, it's a destructive mildew in the house. It's unclean. 
He will break down the house, its stones, and its timber, and all the house's mortar. He will carry them out of the city into an unclean place. 
"Moreover he who goes into the house while it's shut up will be unclean until the evening. 
He who lies down in the house will wash his clothes; and he who eats in the house will wash his clothes. 
"If the priest will come in, and examine it, and look, the plague hasn't spread in the house, after the house was plastered, then the priest will pronounce the house clean, because the plague is healed. 
To cleanse the house he will take two birds, cedar wood, scarlet, and hyssop. 
He will kill one of the birds in an earthen vessel over running water. 
He will take the cedar wood, the hyssop, the scarlet, and the living bird, and dip them in the blood of the slain bird, and in the running water, and sprinkle the house seven times. 
He will cleanse the house with the blood of the bird, and with the running water, with the living bird, with the cedar wood, with the hyssop, and with the scarlet; 
but he will let the living bird go out of the city into the open field. So will he make things right for the house; and it will be clean." 
This is the law for any plague of leprosy, and for an itch, 
and for the destructive mildew of a garment, and for a house, 
and for a swelling, and for a scab, and for a bright spot; 
to teach when it's unclean, and when it's clean. This is the law of leprosy.
