The children of Israel again did that which was bad in God's sight; and God delivered them into the hand of the Philistines forty years. 
There was a certain man of Zorah, of the family of the Danites, whose name was Manoah; and his wife was barren, and childless. 
God's messenger appeared to the woman, and said to her, "See now, you're barren and childless; but you will conceive and bear a son. 
Now please beware and drink no wine nor strong drink, and don't eat any unclean thing; 
for, look, you will conceive and give birth to a son. No razor will come on his head, for the child will be a Nazirite to God from the womb. He will begin to save Israel out of the hand of the Philistines." 
Then the woman came and told her husband, saying, "A man of God came to me, and his face was like the face of the messenger of God, very awesome. I didn't ask him where he was from, neither did he tell me his name; 
but he said to me, 'Look, you will conceive and bear a son; and now drink no wine nor strong drink. Don't eat any unclean thing, for the child will be a Nazirite to God from the womb to the day of his death.'" 
Then Manoah entreated God, and said, "Oh, Lord, please let the man of God whom you sent come again to us, and teach us what we should do to the child who will be born." 
God listened to the voice of Manoah, and the messenger of God came again to the woman as she sat in the field; but Manoah, her husband, wasn't with her. 
The woman hurried and ran, and told her husband, saying to him, "Look, the man who came to me that day has appeared to me." 
Manoah arose and followed his wife, and came to the man, and said to him, "Are you the man who spoke to my wife?" He said, "I am." 
Manoah said, "Now let your words happen. What will the child's way of life and mission be?" 
God's messenger said to Manoah, "Of all that I said to the woman let her beware. 
She can't eat of anything that comes of the vine, neither let her drink wine or strong drink, nor eat any unclean thing. Let her observe all that I commanded her." 
Manoah said to God's messenger, "Please stay with us, that we can make a young goat ready for you." 
God's messenger said to Manoah, "Though you detain me, I won't eat your bread. If you'll prepare a burnt offering, you must offer it to God." Manoah didn't know that he was God's messenger. 
Manoah said to God's messenger, "What's your name, that when your words happen, we can honor you?" 
God's messenger said to him, "Why do you ask about my name, since it's incomprehensible?" 
So Manoah took the young goat with the meal offering, and offered it on the rock to God. Then the messenger did an amazing thing as Manoah and his wife watched. 
When the flame went up toward the sky from off the altar, God's messenger ascended in the flame of the altar. Manoah and his wife watched; and they fell on their faces to the ground. 
But God's messenger didn't appear to Manoah or to his wife any more. Then Manoah knew that he was God's messenger. 
Manoah said to his wife, "We will surely die, because we have seen God." 
But his wife said to him, "If God were pleased to kill us, he wouldn't have received a burnt offering and a meal offering at our hand, and he wouldn't have shown us all these things, nor would he have told us such things as these at this time." 
The woman bore a son and named him Samson. The child grew, and God blessed him. 
God's Spirit began to move him in Mahaneh Dan, between Zorah and Eshtaol.
