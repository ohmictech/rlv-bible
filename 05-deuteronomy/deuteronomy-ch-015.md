At the end of every seven years, you will cancel debts. 
This is the way it will be done: every creditor will release that which he has lent to his neighbor. He won't require payment from his neighbor and his brother, because God's release has been proclaimed. 
Of a foreigner you can require it; but whatever of yours is with your brother, your hand will release. 
However there will be no poor with you (for God will surely bless you in the land which your God gives you for an inheritance to possess) 
if only you diligently listen to your God's voice, to observe to do all this commandment which I command you today. 
Your God will bless you, as he promised you. You'll lend to many nations, but you won't borrow. You'll rule over many nations, but they won't rule over you. 
If a poor man, one of your brothers, is with you within any of your gates in your land which your God gives you, you won't harden your heart, nor shut your hand from your poor brother; 
but you will surely open your hand to him, and will surely lend him sufficient for his need, which he lacks. 
Beware that there not be a guilty thought in your heart, saying, "The seventh year, the year of release, is at hand," and your eye be bad against your poor brother and you give him nothing; and he cry to God against you, and it be guilt on you. 
You will surely give, and your heart won't be grieved when you give to him, because it's for this thing your God will bless you in all your work and in all that you put your hand to. 
The poor will never cease out of the land. So I command you to surely open your hand to your brother, to your needy, and to your poor, in your land. 
If your brother, a Hebrew man, or a Hebrew woman, is sold to you and serves you six years, then in the seventh year you will let him go free from you. 
When you let him go free from you, you won't let him go empty. 
You will furnish him liberally out of your flock, out of your threshing floor, and out of your wine press. As your God has blessed you, you will give to him. 
You will remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt, and your God bought back you. So I command you this thing today. 
It will be, if he tells you, "I won't go out from you," because he loves you and your house, because he's well with you, 
then you will take an awl, and thrust it through his ear to the door, and he will be your servant for the eras. Also to your female servant you will do likewise. 
It won't seem hard to you when you let him go free from you, for he has been double the value of a hired hand as he served you six years. Your God will bless you in all that you do. 
You will dedicate all the firstborn males that are born of your herd and of your flock to your God. You will do no work with the firstborn of your herd, nor shear the firstborn of your flock. 
You will eat it before your God year by year in the place which God will choose, you and your household. 
If it has any defect, is lame or blind, or has any defect whatever, you won't sacrifice it to your God. 
You will eat it within your gates. The unclean and the clean will eat it alike, as the gazelle and as the deer. 
Only you won't eat its blood. You will pour it out on the ground like water.
