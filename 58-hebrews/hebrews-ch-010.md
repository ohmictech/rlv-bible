The law is only a shadow of the good things to come, not the real shape of them. It can never, with the same sacrifices offered year after year without stopping, make those who come near complete. 
If it could, would those sacrifices not have stopped being offered? The worshipers would have been made clean once and for all, and would no longer have felt guilty for their mistakes. 
But those sacrifices are a yearly reminder of mistakes. 
It's impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take mistakes away. 
That's why, when he comes into the world, he says, "You didn't want sacrifice and offering, but you prepared a body for me. 
You took no pleasure in whole burnt offerings and offerings for wrongdoing. 
Then I said, 'Here I am, it's written about me in the scroll of the book, I have come to do your will, O God.'" 
First he says, "You didn't want and took no pleasure in sacrifices and offerings, whole burnt offerings and offerings for wrongdoing" (the ones offered according to the law), 
Then he says, "Here I am, I have come to do your will." He does away with the first to set up the second. 
And by that will we have been set apart, once and for all, through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ. 
Every priest stands day after day serving and offering the same sacrifices over and over, and those can never take mistakes away. 
But this one, after he had offered a single sacrifice for wrongdoing for all time, sat down at the right hand of God, 
and from then on he waits until his enemies are made a footstool for his feet. 
By one offering he has made complete for all time those who are being set apart. 
The Holy Spirit also bears witness to us. First he says, 
"This is the binding arrangement I'll make with them after those days," says the Lord, "I'll put my laws on their hearts, I'll also write them on their minds;" then he says, 
"I'll remember their mistakes and their living as if there were no law no more." 
And where these have been released, there's no longer any offering for wrongdoing. 
So then, brothers and sisters, we have boldness to enter the Holy Place by the blood of Jesus, 
by a new and living way he opened for us through the curtain, that is, through his flesh. 
And we have a great priest over God's house. 
So let's come near with a sincere heart, in the full assurance that comes from trust, with our hearts sprinkled clean from a guilty conscience and our bodies washed with pure water. 
Let's hold tightly to the expectation we declare, without wavering, because the one who promised is trustworthy. 
Let's think about how to stir one another up to love and good deeds, 
not staying away from meeting together, as some have gotten into the habit of doing, but encouraging one another, and all the more as you see the Day coming closer. 
If we keep on drifting, knowing what we're doing, after we have received the knowledge of the truth, there's no longer any sacrifice for wrongdoing, 
only a terrifying expectation of the verdict, and a raging fire that will consume those who are against God. 
Anyone who set aside Moses' law died without mercy on the word of two or three witnesses. 
How much worse a penalty do you think that person deserves, who has trampled the Son of God underfoot, who has treated the blood of the arrangement as something ordinary even though it set them apart, and who has insulted the Spirit of favor? 
We know the one who said, "Payback belongs to me. I'll repay," says the Lord. And again, "The Lord will pass a verdict on his people." 
It's a terrifying thing to fall into the hands of the living God. 
But remember the earlier days, when, after you were given light, you went through a hard struggle with suffering: 
partly by being publicly shamed and treated badly, and partly by standing alongside those who were treated that way. 
You had compassion on me in my chains, and you joyfully accepted the plundering of your possessions, knowing that you have for yourselves a better possession and one that lasts, in the skies. 
So don't throw away your boldness, which brings a great reward. 
You need staying power, so that after you have done the will of God, you can receive what was promised. 
"In just a little while, the one who's coming will come and won't be late. 
But the one who's right will live by trust. And if he draws back, I take no pleasure in him." 
But we aren't among those who draw back and are ruined; we're among those who trust, and so our life is rescued.
