Of the children of Ammon. God says: "Has Israel no sons? Has he no heir? Why then does Malcam possess Gad, and his people dwell in its cities? 
Look, the days come," says God, "that I'll cause an alarm of war to be heard against Rabbah of the children of Ammon, and it'll become a desolate heap, and her daughters will be burned with fire; then Israel will possess those who possessed him," says God. 
"Wail, Heshbon, for Ai is laid waste! Cry, you daughters of Rabbah! Clothe yourself in sackcloth. Lament, and run back and forth among the fences; for Malcam will go into captivity, his priests and his princes together. 
Why do you boast in the valleys, your flowing valley, backsliding daughter? You trusted in her treasures, saying, 'Who will come to me?' 
Look, I'll bring a terror on you," says the Lord, GOD of Armies, "from everyone who's around you. All of you'll be driven completely out, and there will be no one to gather together the fugitives. 
"But afterward I'll reverse the captivity of the children of Ammon," says God. 
Of Edom, the God of Armies says: "Is wisdom no more in Teman? Has counsel perished from the prudent? Has their wisdom vanished? 
Flee! Turn back! Dwell in the depths, inhabitants of Dedan; for I'll bring the calamity of Esau on him when I visit him. 
If grape gatherers came to you, would they not leave some gleaning grapes? If thieves came by night, wouldn't they steal until they had enough? 
But I have made Esau bare, I have uncovered his secret places, and he won't be able to hide himself. His offspring is destroyed, with his brothers and his neighbors; and he's no more. 
Leave your fatherless children. I'll preserve them alive. Let your widows trust in me." 
God says: "Look, they to whom it didn't pertain to drink of the cup will certainly drink; and are you he who will altogether go unpunished? You won't go unpunished, but you'll surely drink. 
I have sworn by myself," says God, "that Bozrah will become an astonishment, a reproach, a waste, and a curse. All its cities will be perpetual wastes." 
I have heard news from God, and an ambassador is sent among the nations, saying, "Gather yourselves together! Come against her! Rise up to the battle!" 
"For, look, I have made you small among the nations, and despised among men. 
As for your terror, the pride of your heart has deceived you, O you who dwell in the clefts of the rock, who hold the height of the hill, though you should make your nest as high as the eagle, I'll bring you down from there," says God. 
"Edom will become an astonishment. Everyone who passes by it'll be astonished, and will hiss at all its plagues. 
As in the overthrow of Sodom and Gomorrah and its neighbor cities," says God, "no man will dwell there, neither will any son of man live therein. 
"Look, he'll come up like a lion from the pride of the Jordan against the strong habitation; for I'll suddenly make them run away from it, and whoever is chosen, I'll appoint him over it. Who's like me? Who will appoint me a time? Who's the shepherd who will stand before me?" 
So hear the counsel of God, that he has taken against Edom, and his purposes that he has purposed against the inhabitants of Teman: Surely they'll drag them away, the little ones of the flock. Surely he'll make their habitation desolate over them. 
The earth trembles at the noise of their fall; there's a cry, the noise which is heard in the Red Sea. 
Look, he'll come up and fly as the eagle, and spread out his wings against Bozrah. The heart of the mighty men of Edom at that day will be as the heart of a woman in her pangs. 
Of Damascus: "Hamath and Arpad are confounded, for they have heard bad news. They have melted away. There's sorrow on the sea. It can't be quiet. 
Damascus has grown feeble, she turns herself to flee, and trembling has seized her. Anguish and sorrows have taken hold of her, as of a woman in travail. 
How is the city of praise not forsaken, the city of my joy? 
So her young men will fall in her streets, and all the men of war will be brought to silence in that day," says the God of Armies. 
"I'll kindle a fire in the wall of Damascus, and it'll devour the palaces of Ben Hadad." 
Of Kedar, and of the kingdoms of Hazor, which Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon struck, God says: "Arise, go up to Kedar, and destroy the children of the east. 
They'll take their tents and their flocks. they'll carry away for themselves their curtains, all their vessels, and their camels; and they'll cry to them, 'Terror on every side!' 
Flee! Wander far off! Dwell in the depths, you inhabitants of Hazor," says God; "for Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon has taken counsel against you, and has conceived a purpose against you. 
Arise! Go up to a nation that's at ease, that dwells without care," says God; "that has neither gates nor bars, that dwells alone. 
Their camels will be a booty, and the multitude of their livestock a plunder. I'll scatter to all winds those who have the corners of their beards cut off; and I'll bring their calamity from every side of them," says God. 
Hazor will be a dwelling place of jackals, a desolation for the eras. No man will dwell there, neither will any son of man live therein." 
God's word that came to Jeremiah the spokesman concerning Elam, in the beginning of the reign of Zedekiah king of Judah, saying, 
"The God of Armies says: 'Look, I'll break the bow of Elam, the chief of their might. 
I'll bring on Elam the four winds from the four quarters of the sky, and will scatter them toward all those winds. There will be no nation where the outcasts of Elam won't come. 
I'll cause Elam to be dismayed before their enemies, and before those who seek their life. I'll bring bad on them, even my fierce anger,' says God; 'and I'll send the sword after them, until I have consumed them. 
I'll set my throne in Elam, and will destroy from there king and princes,' says God. 
'But it'll happen in the latter days that I'll reverse the captivity of Elam,' says God."
