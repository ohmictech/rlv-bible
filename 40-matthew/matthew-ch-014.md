At that time, Herod the district ruler heard the report about Jesus, 
and said to his servants, "This is John the Baptist. He has been raised from the dead. That's why these powerful works are happening through him." 
Herod had arrested John, tied him up, and put him in prison because of Herodias, his brother Philip's wife. 
John said to him, "It isn't allowed for you to have her." 
When he wanted to put him to death, he feared the crowd, because they counted him as a spokesman. 
But when Herod's birthday came, the daughter of Herodias danced among them and pleased Herod. 
So he swore to give her whatever she would ask for. 
She, being prompted by her mother, said, "Give me here on a platter the head of John the Baptist." 
The king was sorry, but because of his oaths and of those who sat at the table with him, he commanded it to be given, 
and he sent and beheaded John in prison. 
His head was brought on a platter and given to the girl, and she brought it to her mother. 
His students came, took the body, and buried it. Then they went and told Jesus. 
Now when Jesus heard this, he withdrew from there in a boat to an empty place apart. When the crowds heard it, they followed him on foot from the cities. 
Jesus went out, and he saw a great crowd. He was moved with deep compassion for them and healed their sick. 
When evening had come, his students came to him, saying, "This place is empty, and it's already late. Send the crowds away, so that they may go into the villages and buy themselves food." 
But Jesus said to them, "They don't need to go away. You give them something to eat." 
They told him, "We only have here five loaves and two fish." 
He said, "Bring them here to me." 
He commanded the crowds to sit down on the grass, and he took the five loaves and the two fish, and looking up to the sky, he blessed them, broke them and gave the loaves to the students, and the students gave to the crowds. 
They all ate and were filled. They took up twelve baskets full of the pieces that were left over. 
Those who ate were about five thousand men, in addition to women and children. 
Immediately Jesus made the students get into the boat and go ahead of him to the other side, while he sent the crowds away. 
After he had sent the crowds away, he went up into the mountain by himself to pray. When evening had come, he was there alone. 
But the boat was already far out on the sea, being battered by the waves, because the wind was against them. 
Just before dawn, Jesus came to them, walking on the sea. 
When the students saw him walking on the sea, they were shaken, saying, "It's a ghost!" and they cried out in fear. 
But immediately Jesus spoke to them, saying, "Cheer up! It's I! Don't be afraid." 
Peter answered him and said, "Lord, if it's you, command me to come to you on the waters." 
He said, "Come!" Peter stepped down from the boat and walked on the waters to come to Jesus. 
But when he saw that the wind was strong, he was afraid, and beginning to sink, he cried out, saying, "Lord, rescue me!" 
Immediately Jesus stretched out his hand, took hold of him, and said to him, "You who don't trust, why did you doubt?" 
When they got up into the boat, the wind ceased. 
Those who were in the boat came and worshiped him, saying, "You're truly the Son of God!" 
When they had crossed over, they came to the land of Gennesaret. 
When the people of that place recognized him, they sent into all that surrounding region and brought to him everyone who was sick. 
And they begged him to let them just touch the fringe of his garment. As many as touched it were healed and made whole.
