Joseph threw himself on his father's face, wept over him, and kissed him. 
Then Joseph ordered the doctors who served him to embalm his father, and they did. 
It took forty days, because that's how long embalming takes, and the Egyptians mourned for Israel seventy days. 
When the mourning was over, Joseph spoke to Pharaoh's household: "If you're pleased with me, please say this to Pharaoh for me: 
'My father made me swear an oath. He said, "I'm dying. Bury me in the grave I dug for myself in Canaan." So let me go up and bury my father, and then I'll come back.'" 
Pharaoh said, "Go and bury your father, as he made you swear." 
So Joseph went up to bury his father, and with him went all Pharaoh's servants, the elders of his household and all the elders of Egypt, 
along with Joseph's whole household, his brothers and his father's household. Only their children, their flocks and their herds were left behind in Goshen. 
Chariots and horsemen went up with him too. It was a huge company. 
When they reached the threshing floor of Atad, beyond the Jordan, they held a very great and bitter lamentation there, and Joseph mourned for his father seven days. 
When the Canaanites living in the land saw the mourning at the threshing floor of Atad, they said, "This is a bitter mourning for the Egyptians." That's why the place is called Abel Mizraim, which is beyond the Jordan. 
So his sons did for him exactly what he had ordered. 
They carried him into Canaan and buried him in the cave in the field of Machpelah, near Mamre, the field Abraham bought from Ephron the Hittite as a burial place. 
After the burial, Joseph went back to Egypt with his brothers and everyone who had gone up with him. 
When Joseph's brothers saw that their father was dead, they said, "Joseph may hate us now and pay us back for all the wrong we did to him." 
So they sent word to Joseph: "Your father gave this order before he died: 
'Tell Joseph, "Please forgive your brothers' disobedience and their wrongdoing, because they did you wrong."' Now please forgive the disobedience of your father's God's servants." Joseph wept when they said this. 
His brothers came and threw themselves down in front of him. "We're your servants," they said. 
Joseph said to them, "Don't be afraid. Am I in God's place? 
You meant it for evil against me, but God meant it for good, to keep many people alive, as you see happening today. 
So don't be afraid. I'll take care of you and your children." He comforted them and spoke kindly to them. 
Joseph stayed in Egypt with his father's household, and he lived a hundred and ten years. 
Joseph lived to see Ephraim's children to the third generation, and the children of Machir son of Manasseh were born on his knees. 
Joseph said to his brothers, "I'm dying, but God will surely come to you and bring you up out of this land to the land he promised to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob." 
And Joseph made the sons of Israel swear an oath: "When God comes to you, you must carry my bones up from here." 
So Joseph died at a hundred and ten, and they embalmed him and put him in a coffin in Egypt.
