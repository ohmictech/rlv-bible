There was a famine in the land, besides the first one that had come in Abraham's time, so Isaac went to Gerar, to Abimelech king of the Philistines. 
God appeared to him and said, "Don't go down to Egypt. Stay in the land I'm about to tell you about. 
Live here in this land, and I'll be with you and bless you. I'll give all these lands to you and your offspring, and I'll keep the oath I swore to your father Abraham. 
I'll make your offspring as many as the stars of the sky and give all these lands to them, and all the nations of the earth will be blessed through your offspring, 
because Abraham obeyed me and did what I required, keeping my commands, my rules, and my laws." 
So Isaac stayed in Gerar. 
When the men of the place asked about his wife, he said, "She's my sister," because he was afraid to say she was his wife. He thought, "The men here might kill me because of Rebekah, since she's beautiful." 
After he had been there a long time, Abimelech king of the Philistines looked out of a window and saw Isaac holding his wife Rebekah close. 
Abimelech called Isaac in and said, "She's obviously your wife. Why did you say, 'She's my sister'?" Isaac answered, "Because I thought I might be killed because of her." 
Abimelech said, "What have you done to us? One of my people could easily have slept with your wife, and you'd have brought guilt on us." 
So Abimelech gave orders to everyone: "Anyone who touches this man or his wife will be put to death." 
Isaac planted crops in that land and that same year harvested a hundred times what he had sown, because God blessed him. 
He grew richer and richer until he was very wealthy. 
He had flocks and herds and a large household, and the Philistines envied him. 
The Philistines had stopped up all the wells his father's servants had dug in Abraham's time, filling them with dirt. 
So Abimelech said to Isaac, "Leave us, because you've become much stronger than we are." 
Isaac left and camped in the valley of Gerar, and settled there. 
Isaac reopened the wells that had been dug in his father Abraham's time, the ones the Philistines had stopped up after Abraham died, and he gave them the same names his father had used. 
Isaac's servants dug in the valley and found a well of fresh water. 
But the herdsmen of Gerar argued with Isaac's herdsmen, saying, "The water is ours." So Isaac named the well Esek, because they quarreled with him. 
They dug another well, and the herdsmen argued over that one too, so he named it Sitnah. 
He moved on from there and dug another well, and nobody argued over that one. So he named it Rehoboth, saying, "Now God has made room for us, and we'll flourish in this land." 
From there he went up to Beersheba. 
That night God appeared to him and said, "I'm the God of your father Abraham. Don't be afraid, because I'm with you. I'll bless you and give you many offspring, for the sake of my servant Abraham." 
Isaac built an altar there and called on the name of God, and he set up his tent there. And there his servants dug a well. 
Then Abimelech came to him from Gerar, with his advisor Ahuzzath and Phicol the commander of his army. 
Isaac said to them, "Why have you come to me, when you hate me and sent me away?" 
They said, "We could see plainly that God is with you, so we said: let there be a solemn agreement between us, between you and us, and let us make a treaty with you, 
that you won't harm us, just as we haven't touched you and have done nothing but good to you, and sent you away in peace. And now you are blessed by God." 
So Isaac made them a feast, and they ate and drank. 
Early the next morning they swore an oath to each other, and Isaac sent them on their way, and they left in peace. 
That same day Isaac's servants came and told him about the well they had dug. They said, "We've found water." 
He named it Shibah, and that's why the city is called Beersheba to this day. 
When Esau was forty, he married Judith daughter of Beeri the Hittite, and Basemath daughter of Elon the Hittite. 
And they made life bitter for Isaac and Rebekah.
