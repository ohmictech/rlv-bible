I wish that you would bear with me in a little foolishness, but indeed you do bear with me. 
For I am jealous over you with a godly jealousy. For I promised you in marriage to one husband, that I might present you as a pure virgin to Christ. 
But I am afraid that somehow, just as the snake tricked Eve with his craftiness, your minds might be led away from a simple and pure devotion to Christ. 
For if someone comes and announces another Jesus we didn't announce, or if you receive a different spirit you didn't receive, or a different "good news" you didn't accept, you put up with it well enough. 
For I don't think I am at all behind those super-sent ones. 
But though I am unskilled in speech, yet I am not unskilled in knowledge. No, in every way we have been revealed to you in all things. 
Or did I make a mistake by humbling myself so that you might be lifted up, because I announced God's Good News to you free of charge? 
I robbed other gatherings, taking pay from them so that I could serve you. 
When I was present with you and was in need, I wasn't a burden on anyone, for the brothers, when they came from Macedonia, supplied the measure of my need. In everything I kept myself from being burdensome to you, and I will continue to do so. 
As the truth of Christ is in me, no one will stop me from this pride in the regions of Achaia. 
Why? Because I don't love you? God knows. 
But what I do, that I will continue to do, that I may cut off opportunity from those who desire an opportunity, that in which they boast, they may be recognized just like us. 
For such people are false sent ones, dishonest workers, dressing up as Christ's sent ones. 
And no wonder, for even the Adversary dresses up as a messenger of light. 
So it is no great thing if his servants also dress up as servants of rightness, and their end will be what their deeds deserve. 
I say again, let no one think me foolish. But if so, yet receive me as foolish, that I also may boast a little. 
That which I speak, I don't speak according to the Lord, but as in foolishness, in this confidence of boasting. 
Seeing that many boast after the flesh, I will also boast. 
For you bear with the foolish gladly, being wise. 
For you put up with it if someone makes you a slave, if someone eats you out, if someone takes you in, if someone puts on airs, if someone slaps you in the face. 
To my shame, I speak as though we had been weak. Yet in whatever way anyone is bold (I speak in foolishness), I am bold also. 
Are they Hebrews? So am I. Are they Israelites? So am I. Are they the offspring of Abraham? So am I. 
Are they servants of Christ? (I am talking like a madman.) I am more: in harder toil, in more prisons, in beatings beyond counting, and often near death. 
Five times I received forty lashes minus one from the Jewish people. 
Three times I was beaten with rods. Once I was stoned. Three times I suffered shipwreck. I have been a night and a day in the deep. 
I have been on many journeys, in danger from rivers, in danger from robbers, in danger from my own people, in danger from the nations, in danger in the city, in danger in the empty land, in danger at sea, in danger from false brothers; 
in toil and hardship, in many nights without sleep, in hunger and thirst, in many days without food, in cold and with nothing to wear. 
Besides everything else, there is what presses on me daily: worry for all the gatherings. 
Who is weak, and I am not weak? Who is caused to stumble, and I don't burn with indignation? 
If I must take pride, I will take pride in the things that show my weakness. 
The God and Father of the Lord Jesus Christ, who is praised for the eras, knows that I am not lying. 
In Damascus the governor under King Aretas guarded the Damascenes' city, desiring to arrest me. 
I was let down in a basket through a window by the wall, and escaped his hands.
