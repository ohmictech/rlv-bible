God spoke to Moses: 
"Speak to Aaron, his sons and all the Israelites and tell them, 'This is what God has commanded. 
Any Israelite who kills an ox, a lamb or a goat in the camp, or kills it outside the camp, 
and doesn't bring it to the entrance of the Tent of Meeting to present it as an offering to God in front of God's tent, is guilty of bloodshed. He has shed blood, and he is to be cut off from among his people. 
This is so the Israelites will bring the sacrifices they are making out in the open field to God, to the priest at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting, and sacrifice them there as wholeness offerings to God. 
The priest is to splash the blood against God's altar at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting and burn the fat as a soothing aroma to God. 
They are never again to sacrifice to the goat idols they chase after. This is to be a perpetual rule for them throughout their generations.' 
"Say to them, 'Any Israelite, or any foreigner living among them, who brings a burnt offering or a sacrifice 
and doesn't bring it to the entrance of the Tent of Meeting to sacrifice it to God, is to be cut off from his people. 
"'Any Israelite, or any foreigner living among them, who eats any blood, I will set my face against that person and cut him off from among his people, 
because the life of the flesh is in the blood, and I have given it to you on the altar to make things right for your lives, because it is the blood, with the life in it, that makes things right. 
That is why I said to the Israelites, "No one among you is to eat blood, and no foreigner living among you is to eat blood." 
"'Any Israelite, or any foreigner living among them, who hunts and takes an animal or bird that may be eaten is to pour out its blood and cover it with dust. 
The life of every creature is its blood, because its life is in it. That is why I said to the Israelites, "You are not to eat the blood of any creature, because the life of every creature is its blood. Whoever eats it is to be cut off." 
"'Anyone who eats an animal that died on its own or was torn by wild animals, whether native born or a foreigner, is to wash his clothes and wash himself in water, and will be unclean until evening. Then he will be clean. 
But if he doesn't wash them or wash himself, he will carry his guilt.'"
