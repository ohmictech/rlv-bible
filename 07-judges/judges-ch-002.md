God's messenger went up from Gilgal to Bochim and said, "I brought you out of Egypt and brought you to the land I swore to give your fathers. I said, 'I will never break my covenant with you. 
You must make no covenant with the people of this land. You must tear down their altars.' But you haven't listened to my voice. Why have you done this? 
So I also said, 'I won't drive them out ahead of you. They will be thorns in your sides, and their gods will be a trap to you.'" 
When God's messenger spoke these words to all the Israelites, the people cried out and wept. 
They named that place Bochim, and they sacrificed there to God. 
When Joshua sent the people away, the Israelites each went to his own inheritance to take it. 
The people served God all the days of Joshua and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua and had seen everything God had done for Israel. 
Joshua son of Nun, the servant of God, died at a hundred and ten years old. 
They buried him in his own land at Timnath Heres in the hill country of Ephraim, north of Mount Gaash. 
After that whole generation had joined their ancestors, another generation grew up who didn't know God or what he had done for Israel. 
The Israelites did what was bad in God's sight and served the Baals. 
They left the God of their fathers, who had brought them out of Egypt, and followed other gods, the gods of the peoples around them, and bowed down to them, and they made God angry. 
They left God and served Baal and the Ashtaroth. 
God's anger burned against Israel, and he handed them over to raiders who plundered them. He sold them into the hands of their enemies all around, and they could no longer stand against their enemies. 
Everywhere they went, the hand of God was against them and brought them trouble, just as God had said and sworn to them, and they were in great distress. 
Then God raised up judges who saved them from those who plundered them. 
But they didn't listen to their judges either. They went sleeping around after other gods and bowed down to them. They quickly turned off the road their fathers had walked, the road of obeying God's commands. They didn't do it. 
When God raised up a judge for them, God was with the judge and saved them from their enemies as long as the judge lived, because God was moved to pity by their groaning under those who oppressed and crushed them. 
But when the judge died, they went back and became even more corrupt than their fathers, following other gods, serving them and bowing down to them. They wouldn't give up what they were doing or their stubborn ways. 
God's anger burned against Israel, and he said, "Because this nation has broken my covenant that I commanded their fathers and hasn't listened to my voice, 
I will no longer drive out ahead of them any of the nations Joshua left when he died. 
I will use them to test Israel, to see whether they will keep the way of God and walk in it as their fathers did or not." 
So God left those nations there and didn't drive them out quickly, and he didn't hand them over to Joshua.
