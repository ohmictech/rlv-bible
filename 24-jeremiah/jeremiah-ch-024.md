God showed me, and look, two baskets of figs were set before God's temple, after Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had carried away captive Jeconiah the son of Jehoiakim, king of Judah, and the princes of Judah, with the craftsmen and smiths, from Jerusalem, and had brought them to Babylon. 
One basket had very good figs, like the figs that are first-ripe; and the other basket had very bad figs, which couldn't be eaten, they were so bad. 
Then God asked me, "What do you see, Jeremiah?" I said, "Figs. The good figs are very good, and the bad are very bad, so bad that they can't be eaten." 
God's word came to me, saying, 
"The God of Israel says: 'Like these good figs, so I'll regard the captives of Judah, whom I have sent out of this place into the land of the Chaldeans, as good. 
I'll set my eyes on them for good, and I'll bring them again to this land. I'll build them, and not pull them down. I'll plant them, and not pluck them up. 
I'll give them a heart to know me, that I'm God. They'll be my people, and I'll be their God; for they'll return to me with their whole heart. 
"'As the bad figs, which can't be eaten, they're so bad,' surely God says, 'So I'll give up Zedekiah the king of Judah, and his princes, and the remnant of Jerusalem who remain in this land, and those who dwell in the land of Egypt. 
I'll even give them up to be tossed back and forth among all the kingdoms of the earth for bad, to be a reproach and a proverb, a taunt and a curse, in all places where I'll drive them. 
I'll send the sword, the famine, and the pestilence among them, until they're consumed from off the land that I gave to them and to their fathers.'"
