God spoke to Moses and Aaron in Egypt: 
"This month will mark the start of your calendar. It will be the first month of your year. 
Tell the whole community of Israel that on the tenth day of this month each man is to take a lamb for his family, one lamb per household. 
If a household is too small for a whole lamb, he and his nearest neighbor can share one, dividing the lamb by how much each person eats. 
The lamb must be without defect, a year old male, taken from the sheep or the goats. 
Keep it until the fourteenth day of this month, and then the whole community of Israel will slaughter it at twilight. 
They are to take some of the blood and smear it on the doorposts and the beam above the door of the houses where they eat it. 
That night they will eat the meat roasted over fire, with bread made without yeast and with bitter herbs. 
Don't eat it raw or boiled in water. Roast it over fire, head, legs and inner parts. 
Don't leave any of it until morning. Whatever is left in the morning, burn. 
This is how you are to eat it: dressed and ready to travel, with sandals on your feet and a staff in your hand. Eat it in a hurry. It is God's Passover. 
That night I will pass through Egypt and strike every firstborn in the land, both people and animals, and I will bring judgment on all the gods of Egypt. I am God. 
The blood on the houses where you live will be a sign for you. When I see the blood, I will pass over you, and no plague will destroy you when I strike Egypt. 
This day will be a memorial for you. You will celebrate it as a festival to God, a festival kept for the eras, throughout your generations. 
For seven days you will eat bread made without yeast. On the first day clear the yeast out of your houses, because anyone who eats bread with yeast from the first day to the seventh will be cut off from Israel. 
On the first day hold a holy assembly, and another on the seventh day. Do no work on those days except preparing food, which you may do. 
Keep the festival of bread without yeast, because on this very day I brought your people out of Egypt like an army. Keep this day for the eras, throughout your generations. 
In the first month, from the evening of the fourteenth day to the evening of the twenty-first day, you will eat bread without yeast. 
For seven days no yeast is to be found in your houses, because anyone who eats anything with yeast will be cut off from the community of Israel, whether he is a foreigner or born in the land. 
Eat nothing with yeast. Wherever you live, eat bread without yeast.'" 
Then Moses called all the elders of Israel and said, "Go and get a lamb for each family, and slaughter the Passover lamb. 
Take a bunch of hyssop, dip it in the blood in the basin, and smear some on the beam above the door and on the two doorposts. None of you may go out of his door until morning. 
God will pass through to strike the Egyptians, and when he sees the blood on the beam and the doorposts, God will pass over that door and will not let the destroyer come into your houses to strike you. 
You must keep this as a rule for you and your children for the eras. 
When you come to the land God will give you, as he promised, you will keep this ceremony. 
And when your children ask you, 'What does this ceremony mean?' 
you will say, 'It is the Passover sacrifice to God, who passed over the houses of the Israelites in Egypt when he struck the Egyptians and spared our homes.'" Then the people bowed down and worshiped. 
The Israelites went and did exactly as God had commanded Moses and Aaron. 
At midnight God struck every firstborn in Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh on his throne to the firstborn of the prisoner in the dungeon, and every firstborn of the livestock. 
Pharaoh got up in the night, he and all his officials and all the Egyptians, and there was loud crying in Egypt, because there was no house without someone dead. 
He called for Moses and Aaron during the night and said, "Get up and get out from among my people, you and the Israelites. Go and serve God as you asked. 
Take your flocks and herds as you said, and go. And bless me too." 
The Egyptians pushed the people to leave the country quickly, saying, "We're all dead men." 
So the people took their dough before the yeast was added, carrying their kneading bowls wrapped in their clothes on their shoulders. 
The Israelites did as Moses said, and asked the Egyptians for silver and gold jewelry and clothing. 
God made the Egyptians look kindly on the people, so they gave them what they asked for. So they carried off the wealth of the Egyptians. 
The Israelites traveled from Rameses to Succoth, about six hundred thousand men on foot, not counting the children. 
A mixed crowd went up with them too, along with flocks and herds and a great deal of livestock. 
They baked the dough they had brought out of Egypt into flatbread without yeast, because it hadn't risen yet. They had been driven out of Egypt so fast they couldn't wait, and they had no food prepared. 
The Israelites had lived in Egypt four hundred and thirty years. 
At the end of four hundred and thirty years, on that very day, all the armies of God marched out of Egypt. 
It is a night to be kept for God for bringing them out of Egypt. This is that night of God, to be kept by every generation of Israelites. 
God said to Moses and Aaron, "This is the rule for the Passover. No foreigner may eat it, 
but a slave bought with money may eat it once you have circumcised him. 
A temporary resident or a hired hand may not eat it. 
It must be eaten in one house. Don't carry any of the meat outside, and don't break any of its bones. 
The whole community of Israel must keep it. 
If a foreigner living among you wants to keep the Passover to God, all the males in his household must be circumcised first, and then he may come and keep it, and he will be like someone born in the land. But no uncircumcised person may eat it. 
The same law applies to the native and to the foreigner living among you." 
All the Israelites did as God had commanded Moses and Aaron. 
That same day God brought the Israelites out of Egypt like an army.
