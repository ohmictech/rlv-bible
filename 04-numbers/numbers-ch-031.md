God spoke to Moses: 
"Pay back the Midianites for what they did to the Israelites. After that you will be gathered to your people." 
Moses spoke to the people: "Arm some of your men for war and send them against Midian, to carry out God's payback on Midian. 
Send a thousand men from each tribe, from all the tribes of Israel." 
So a thousand were handed over from each tribe, twelve thousand men armed for war. 
Moses sent them out, a thousand from each tribe, with Phinehas son of Eleazar the priest, who carried the utensils of the Holy Place and the trumpets for sounding the alarm. 
They fought against Midian, just as God commanded Moses, and they killed every male. 
They killed the kings of Midian along with the rest: Evi, Rekem, Zur, Hur and Reba, the five kings of Midian. They also killed Balaam son of Beor with the sword. 
The Israelites took the Midianite women and children captive, and took all their livestock, all their herds and all their goods as plunder. 
They burned every city where they lived and every camp with fire. 
They took all the captives and all the plunder, both people and animals, 
and brought the captives, the plunder and the goods to Moses, to Eleazar the priest and to the community of Israel, at the camp on the plains of Moab by the Jordan across from Jericho. 
Moses and Eleazar the priest and all the leaders of the community went out to meet them outside the camp. 
Moses was angry with the officers of the army, the commanders of thousands and the commanders of hundreds, who had come back from the battle. 
Moses said to them, "Have you let all the women live? 
Look, these are the ones who, on Balaam's advice, led the Israelites to break faith with God in the matter of Peor, and the plague came on the community of God. 
So now kill every male among the children, and kill every woman who has slept with a man. 
But keep alive for yourselves every girl who has never slept with a man. 
"Stay outside the camp for seven days. Anyone who has killed someone, or touched anyone who was killed, is to make himself clean on the third day and on the seventh day, you and your captives. 
Clean every garment, everything made of leather, everything made of goats' hair and everything made of wood." 
Eleazar the priest said to the men of war who had gone to battle, "This is the rule of the law God commanded Moses. 
The gold, the silver, the bronze, the iron, the tin and the lead, 
everything that can stand fire, you are to pass through the fire, and it will be clean, though it must also be cleaned with the water that makes things clean. Everything that can't stand fire you are to pass through the water. 
Wash your clothes on the seventh day, and you will be clean. After that you may come into the camp." 
God spoke to Moses: 
"Count the plunder that was taken, both people and animals, you and Eleazar the priest and the heads of the families of the community, 
and divide the plunder in two, between the men who went out to battle and the rest of the community. 
From the men of war who went out, set aside a share for God: one out of every five hundred, of the people, the cattle, the donkeys and the flocks. 
Take it from their half and give it to Eleazar the priest as God's wave offering. 
From the half given to the Israelites, take one out of every fifty, of the people, the cattle, the donkeys and the flocks, from all the livestock, and give them to the Levites, who do the work of God's tent." 
Moses and Eleazar the priest did as God commanded Moses. 
The plunder left over from what the soldiers had taken was six hundred seventy-five thousand sheep, 
seventy-two thousand cattle, 
sixty-one thousand donkeys, 
and thirty-two thousand people, all of them girls who had never slept with a man. 
The half belonging to those who went out to war was three hundred thirty-seven thousand five hundred sheep, 
and God's share of the sheep was six hundred seventy-five. 
There were thirty-six thousand cattle, and God's share was seventy-two. 
There were thirty thousand five hundred donkeys, and God's share was sixty-one. 
There were sixteen thousand people, and God's share was thirty-two. 
Moses gave the share that belonged to God to Eleazar the priest, as God commanded Moses. 
From the half given to the Israelites, which Moses had separated from the soldiers' half 
(the community's half was three hundred thirty-seven thousand five hundred sheep, 
thirty-six thousand cattle, 
thirty thousand five hundred donkeys, 
and sixteen thousand people), 
from the Israelites' half Moses took one out of every fifty, of people and animals, and gave them to the Levites, who did the work of God's tent, just as God commanded Moses. 
The officers in charge of the army, the commanders of thousands and of hundreds, came to Moses 
and said, "Your servants have counted the men under our command, and not one of them is missing. 
So we have brought what each man found as an offering to God: gold ornaments, armbands, bracelets, signet rings, earrings and necklaces, to make things right for our lives before God." 
Moses and Eleazar the priest took the gold from them, all the worked jewelry. 
All the gold of the wave offering that they gave to God, from the commanders of thousands and of hundreds, was sixteen thousand seven hundred fifty shekels. 
The soldiers had each kept their own plunder. 
Moses and Eleazar the priest took the gold from the commanders of thousands and of hundreds and brought it into the Tent of Meeting, as a reminder for the Israelites before God.
