On that day Jesus went out of the house and sat by the sea. 
Great crowds gathered to him, so that he got into a boat and sat down, and all the crowd stood on the shore. 
He spoke to them many things in stories, saying, "Look, a farmer went out to sow. 
As he sowed, some seeds fell by the roadside, and the birds came and ate them up. 
Others fell on rocky ground, where they didn't have much soil, and immediately they sprang up, because they had no depth of soil. 
When the sun had risen, they were scorched. Because they had no root, they withered away. 
Others fell among thorns. The thorns grew up and choked them. 
Others fell on good soil and yielded fruit: some one hundred times as much, some sixty, and some thirty. 
He who has ears to hear, let him hear." 
The students came, and said to him, "Why do you speak to them in stories?" 
He answered them, "To you it's given to know the secrets of the reign of the sky, but it isn't given to them. 
Whoever has, to him will be given, and he'll have more than enough, but whoever doesn't have, from him will be taken away even that which he has. 
So I speak to them in stories, because seeing they don't see, and hearing, they don't hear, neither do they understand. 
What Isaiah spoke for God is coming true in them. He said, 'By hearing you'll hear, and will in no way understand. Seeing you'll see, and will in no way perceive. 
This people's heart has grown callous, their ears are hard of hearing, and they have closed their eyes, or else they might perceive with their eyes, hear with their ears, understand with their heart, and turn back, and I'd heal them.' 
"But flourishing are your eyes, for they see, and your ears, for they hear. 
Truly I tell you, many spokesmen and upright people wanted to see what you see and didn't see it, and to hear what you hear and didn't hear it. 
"Hear, then, the story of the farmer. 
When anyone hears the word of the reign and doesn't understand it, the Slanderer comes and snatches away that which has been sown in his heart. This is what was sown by the roadside. 
What was sown on the rocky places, this is the one who hears the word and immediately receives it with joy. 
Yet he has no root in himself, but lasts only a while. When pressure or being hunted down arises because of the word, immediately he's trapped. 
What was sown among the thorns, this is the one who hears the word, but the worries of this era and the deceitfulness of riches choke the word, and he becomes unfruitful. 
What was sown on the good ground, this is the one who hears the word and understands it, who indeed bears fruit and produces, some one hundred times as much, some sixty, and some thirty." 
He set another story before them, saying, "The reign of the sky is like a man who sowed good seed in his field, 
but while people slept, his enemy came and sowed weeds also among the wheat, and went away. 
But when the blade sprang up and produced grain, then the weeds appeared also. 
The servants of the head of the house came and said to him, 'Sir, didn't you sow good seed in your field? Where did these weeds come from?' 
"He said to them, 'An enemy has done this.' "The servants asked him, 'Do you want us to go and gather them up?' 
"But he said, 'No, or else while you gather up the weeds, you might root up the wheat with them. 
Let both grow together until the harvest, and at harvest time I'll tell the reapers, "First, gather up the weeds, and tie them in bundles to burn them, but gather the wheat into my barn."'" 
He set another story before them, saying, "The reign of the sky is like a grain of mustard seed which a man took and sowed in his field, 
which indeed is smaller than all seeds. But when it's grown, it's greater than the herbs and becomes a tree, so that the birds of the sky come and nest in its branches." 
He spoke another story to them. "The reign of the sky is like yeast which a woman took and hid in three measures of flour, until it was all leavened." 
Jesus spoke all these things in stories to the crowds, and without a story he didn't speak to them, 
so that what was spoken through the spokesman would come true: "I'll open my mouth in stories. I'll utter things hidden from the foundation of the world." 
Then Jesus sent the crowds away, and went into the house. His students came to him, saying, "Explain to us the story of the weeds of the field." 
He answered them, "The one who sows the good seed is the Human One, 
the field is the world, the good seeds are the children of the reign, and the weeds are the children of the Slanderer. 
The enemy who sowed them is the Slanderer. The harvest is the completion of the era, and the reapers are messengers. 
So just as the weeds are gathered up and burned with fire, that's how it'll be at the completion of this era. 
The Human One will send out his messengers, and they'll gather out of his reign everything that traps and those who live as if there were no law, 
and will throw them into the furnace of fire. There will be crying and grinding of teeth. 
Then the upright will shine like the sun in the reign of their Father. He who has ears to hear, let him hear. 
"Again, the reign of the sky is like treasure hidden in the field, which a man found and hid. In his joy, he goes and sells all that he has and buys that field. 
"Again, the reign of the sky is like a man who's a merchant seeking fine pearls, 
who found one pearl worth a great deal, and went and sold all that he had and bought it. 
"Again, the reign of the sky is like a fishing net that was thrown into the sea and gathered fish of every kind, 
which, when it was filled, fishermen dragged up onto the shore. They sat down and gathered the good into containers, but the rotten ones they threw away. 
So it'll be at the completion of the era. The messengers will come and separate the malicious from among the upright, 
and will throw them into the furnace of fire. There will be crying and grinding of teeth." 
Jesus said to them, "Have you understood all these things?" They answered him, "Yes, Lord." 
He said to them, "So every Bible teacher who has become a student of the reign of the sky is like the head of a house who brings out of his treasure both new things and old." 
When Jesus had finished these stories, he departed from there. 
Coming to his hometown, he taught them in their gathering place, so that they were astonished and said, "Where did this man get this wisdom and these powerful works? 
Isn't this the carpenter's son? Isn't his mother called Mary, and his brothers James, Joses, Simon, and Judas? 
Aren't all of his sisters with us? Where then did this man get all of these things?" 
They were trapped by him. But Jesus said to them, "A spokesman isn't without honor, except in his own country and in his own house." 
He didn't do many powerful works there because of their lack of trust.
