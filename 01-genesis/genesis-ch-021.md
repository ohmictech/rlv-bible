God did what he had promised for Sarah, exactly as he had said. 
Sarah became pregnant and gave Abraham a son in his old age, at the exact time God had said. 
Abraham named the son Sarah bore him Isaac. 
Abraham circumcised his son Isaac when he was eight days old, just as God had commanded. 
Abraham was a hundred years old when his son Isaac was born. 
Sarah said, "God has given me a laugh, and everyone who hears about it will laugh with me." 
She said, "Who would have told Abraham that Sarah would nurse children? And here I have given him a son in his old age." 
The boy grew and was weaned, and Abraham threw a big party the day Isaac was weaned. 
Sarah saw the son Hagar the Egyptian had borne to Abraham making fun of him. 
So she said to Abraham, "Get rid of that slave woman and her son! That slave woman's son won't share the inheritance with my son Isaac." 
This upset Abraham a great deal, because it was about his son. 
But God said to Abraham, "Don't be upset about the boy and your slave woman. Do whatever Sarah tells you, because your family line will come through Isaac. 
I'll make a nation out of the slave woman's son too, because he's your child." 
Abraham got up early the next morning, took bread and a skin of water, and put them on Hagar's shoulder. Then he sent her off with the boy. She left and wandered in the wilderness of Beersheba. 
When the water in the skin ran out, she put the boy under one of the bushes. 
She went off and sat down a good distance away, about a bowshot, and said, "I can't watch the boy die." She sat there opposite him and cried out loud. 
God heard the boy crying, and the messenger of God called to Hagar from the sky, "What's wrong, Hagar? Don't be afraid. God has heard the boy crying where he is. 
Get up, pick the boy up, and hold on to him. I'll make him a great nation." 
Then God opened her eyes and she saw a well. She went and filled the skin with water and gave the boy a drink. 
God was with the boy as he grew. He lived in the wilderness and became a skilled archer. 
He lived in the wilderness of Paran, and his mother found him a wife from Egypt. 
About that time, Abimelech and Phicol, the commander of his army, said to Abraham, "God is with you in everything you do. 
Now swear to me here by God that you won't lie to me, or to my children, or to my grandchildren. Show me and the land you've been living in the same kindness I've shown you." 
Abraham said, "I swear it." 
Then Abraham complained to Abimelech about a well that Abimelech's servants had taken by force. 
Abimelech said, "I don't know who did that. You never told me, and I only heard about it today." 
So Abraham took sheep and cattle and gave them to Abimelech, and the two of them made a treaty. 
Abraham set seven female lambs apart from the rest of the flock. 
Abimelech asked Abraham, "What are these seven lambs you've set apart for?" 
Abraham said, "Take these seven lambs from me as your witness that I dug this well." 
That's why the place is called Beersheba, because the two of them swore an oath there. 
After they had made the treaty at Beersheba, Abimelech and Phicol, the commander of his army, went back to the land of the Philistines. 
Abraham planted a tamarisk tree at Beersheba, and there he called on the name of God, the God of the eras. 
Abraham lived in the land of the Philistines as a foreigner for a long time.
