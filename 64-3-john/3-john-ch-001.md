From the elder, to my dear Gaius, whom I truly love. 
My truly loved friend, I pray that everything goes well with you and that you stay healthy, just as your life is going well. 
I celebrated greatly when brothers and sisters came and told me the truth about you, about how you are walking in the truth. 
I have no greater joy than this: to hear that my children are walking in the truth. 
My truly loved friend, you are doing a trustworthy work in whatever you do for the brothers and sisters, even though they are strangers to you. 
They have told the gathering about your love. You will do well to send them on their way in a way that honors God, 
because they went out for the sake of the Name, taking nothing from the nations. 
So we should welcome people like that, so that we may work alongside them for the truth. 
I wrote to the gathering, but Diotrephes, who loves to be the most important among them, doesn't accept what we say. 
So if I come, I will bring up what he is doing, spreading wicked rumors about us. And he isn't satisfied with that: he refuses to welcome the brothers and sisters himself, and he stops those who would, and throws them out of the gathering. 
My truly loved friend, don't imitate what is bad; imitate what is good. Whoever does good belongs to God. Whoever does bad hasn't seen God. 
Everyone bears witness to Demetrius, and so does the truth itself. We also bear witness, and you know that our witness is true. 
I had many things to write to you, but I am unwilling to write to you with ink and pen; 
but I hope to see you soon, and then we will speak face to face. Wholeness be with you. The friends send you greetings. Greet each friend by name.
