He began to speak to them in stories. "A man planted a vineyard, put a hedge around it, dug a winepress pit, built a tower, rented it out to some farmers, and went to another country. 
When it was time, he sent a slave to the farmers to get from them his share of the fruit of the vineyard. 
They took him, beat him, and sent him away empty. 
Again he sent another slave to them, and they hit him on the head and sent him away humiliated. 
Again he sent another, and they killed him, and many others, beating some and killing some. 
He still had one left, his son whom he truly loved, and he sent him last to them, saying, 'They will respect my son.' 
But those farmers said among themselves, 'This is the heir. Come, let's kill him, and the inheritance will be ours.' 
They took him, killed him, and cast him out of the vineyard. 
So what will the lord of the vineyard do? He will come and ruin those farmers and give the vineyard to others. 
Haven't you even read this Scripture: 'The stone the builders rejected has become the cornerstone. 
This was from the Lord. It is marvelous in our eyes'?" 
They tried to seize him, but they feared the crowd, for they knew that he had spoken the story against them. They left him and went away. 
They sent some of the strict religious ones and the Herodians to him, to trap him in his words. 
When they had come, they asked him, "Teacher, we know that you are honest and don't defer to anyone, for you aren't partial to anyone, but truly teach the way of God. Is it allowed to pay taxes to Caesar, or not? 
Should we pay, or shouldn't we?" But he knew their pretense and said to them, "Why are you testing me? Bring me a silver coin, that I may look at it." 
They brought it. He said to them, "Whose is this image and inscription?" They said to him, "Caesar's." 
Jesus answered them, "Give to Caesar what belongs to Caesar, and to God what belongs to God." And they were amazed at him. 
Some of the religious elite, who say there is no rising from the dead, came to him and asked him, 
"Teacher, Moses wrote for us, 'If a man's brother dies and leaves a wife behind but leaves no children, his brother should take the widow and raise up children for his brother.' 
There were seven brothers. The first took a wife, and dying left no offspring. 
The second took her, and died, leaving no children behind him. The third likewise; 
and the seven took her and left no children. Last of all, the woman also died. 
In the rising from the dead, when they rise, whose wife will she be of them? All seven had her as a wife." 
Jesus answered them, "Isn't this why you are mistaken: you don't know the Scriptures or the power of God? 
When they rise from the dead, they neither marry nor are given in marriage, but are like messengers in the sky. 
But about the dead, that they are raised, haven't you read in the book of Moses, in the account of the burning bush, how God spoke to him, saying, 'I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob'? 
He isn't the God of dead bodies, but of the living. You are badly mistaken." 
One of the Bible teachers came and heard them arguing, and knowing that he had answered them well, asked him, "Which commandment is the greatest of all?" 
Jesus answered, "The greatest is: 'Hear, Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is one. 
You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your life, with all your mind, and with all your strength.' This is the first commandment. 
The second is this: 'You shall love your neighbor as yourself.' There is no other commandment greater than these." 
The Bible teacher said to him, "Teacher, you have spoken well in saying that he is one, and there is no other but he; 
and to love him with all the heart, with all the understanding, with all the life, and with all the strength, and to love one's neighbor as oneself, is more important than all whole burnt offerings and sacrifices." 
When Jesus saw that he had answered wisely, he said to him, "You aren't far from God's reign." And no one dared ask him any more questions after that. 
Jesus responded, as he taught in the temple courts, "How is it that the Bible teachers say the Christ is the son of David? 
David himself, in the Holy Spirit, said, 'The Lord said to my Lord, "Sit at my right hand until I put your enemies under your feet."' 
David himself calls him Lord, so how can he be his son?" And the great crowd heard him gladly. 
In his teaching he said to them, "Watch out for the Bible teachers, who like to walk around in long robes and to be greeted in the marketplaces, 
and to have the best seats in the gathering places and the places of honor at dinners, 
who eat up widows' houses and, for a pretense, make long prayers. These will receive a heavier verdict." 
Jesus sat down opposite the treasury and watched how the crowd put money into the treasury. Many who were rich put in a great deal. 
A poor widow came and put in two small copper coins, less than an hour's pay. 
He called his students to himself and said to them, "Truly I tell you, this poor widow put in more than everyone who is putting money into the treasury, 
for they all gave out of what they had to spare, but she, out of her poverty, gave everything she had to live on."
