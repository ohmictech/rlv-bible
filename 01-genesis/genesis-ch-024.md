Abraham was old, well on in years, and God had blessed him in every way. 
Abraham said to the senior servant in his household, the one in charge of everything he owned, "Put your hand under my thigh 
and swear by the God of the sky and the God of the earth that you won't get a wife for my son from the Canaanite women I live among. 
Instead, go to my homeland and my relatives and find a wife there for my son Isaac." 
The servant asked, "What if the woman won't come back with me to this land? Should I take your son back to the country you came from?" 
Abraham said, "Make sure you don't take my son back there. 
The God of the sky took me from my father's house and the land where I was born. He spoke to me and swore to me, 'I'll give this land to your offspring.' He'll send his messenger ahead of you, and you'll get a wife for my son from there. 
If the woman won't come back with you, then you're free from this oath to me. Just don't take my son back there." 
So the servant put his hand under the thigh of his master Abraham and swore to him about this matter. 
Then the servant took ten of his master's camels and left, carrying all kinds of good things from his master. He set out and went to Mesopotamia, to the city of Nahor. 
He made the camels kneel down outside the city beside the well, in the evening, the time when women come out to draw water. 
He said, "God of my master Abraham, please give me success today, and show kindness to my master Abraham. 
Look, I'm standing here by the spring, and the young women of the city are coming out to draw water. 
Let it work out this way: the young woman I say to, 'Please lower your jar so I can drink,' and who answers, 'Drink, and I'll water your camels too,' let her be the one you've chosen for your servant Isaac. That's how I'll know you've shown kindness to my master." 
Before he had finished praying, Rebekah came out with her jar on her shoulder. She was the daughter of Bethuel, son of Milcah, the wife of Abraham's brother Nahor. 
She was very beautiful, a young woman who had never been with a man. She went down to the spring, filled her jar, and came back up. 
The servant ran to meet her and said, "Please give me a little water from your jar." 
She said, "Drink, my lord." She quickly lowered her jar to her hand and gave him a drink. 
When he had finished drinking, she said, "I'll draw water for your camels too, until they've had enough." 
She quickly emptied her jar into the trough and ran back to the well to draw more, and she drew water for all his camels. 
The man watched her closely and said nothing, wondering whether God had made his journey succeed. 
When the camels had finished drinking, the man took out a gold nose ring weighing half a shekel and two gold bracelets weighing ten shekels, 
and said, "Whose daughter are you? Please tell me. Is there room in your father's house for us to spend the night?" 
She said to him, "I'm the daughter of Bethuel, the son Milcah had by Nahor." 
She added, "We have plenty of straw and feed, and room for you to stay." 
Then the man bowed low and worshiped God. 
He said, "Praise the God of my master Abraham, who hasn't stopped being kind and faithful to my master. As for me, God has led me straight to the house of my master's relatives." 
The young woman ran and told her mother's household what had happened. 
Rebekah had a brother named Laban, and he ran out to the spring to find the man. 
When he saw the ring and the bracelets on his sister's arms, and heard what Rebekah said the man had told her, he went to him. He was standing by the camels at the spring. 
He said, "Come in, you who are blessed by God. Why are you standing out here? I've made the house ready, and there's room for the camels." 
So the man went into the house, and Laban unloaded the camels. He brought straw and feed for the camels, and water to wash the man's feet and the feet of the men with him. 
Food was put in front of him, but he said, "I won't eat until I've told you why I came." Laban said, "Go ahead." 
He said, "I'm Abraham's servant. 
God has blessed my master richly, and he's become a great man. God has given him flocks and herds, silver and gold, male and female servants, camels and donkeys. 
My master's wife Sarah had a son for him in her old age, and he's given everything he owns to him. 
My master made me swear, 'You won't get a wife for my son from the Canaanite women, in whose land I live, 
but go to my father's family and my own relatives and find a wife for my son.' 
I asked my master, 'What if the woman won't come back with me?' 
He said to me, 'The God I serve will send his messenger with you and make your journey succeed, and you'll get a wife for my son from my family, from my father's house. 
Then you'll be free from my oath once you've been to my relatives. Even if they refuse to give her to you, you'll still be free from my oath.' 
So I came to the spring today and said, 'God of my master Abraham, if you're going to make my journey succeed, 
look, I'm standing here by the spring. Let it work out that the young woman who comes out to draw water, the one I say to, "Please give me a little water from your jar to drink," 
the one who answers, "Drink, and I'll draw water for your camels too," let her be the woman God has chosen for my master's son.' 
Before I had finished praying silently, Rebekah came out with her jar on her shoulder. She went down to the spring and drew water, and I said to her, 'Please let me have a drink.' 
She quickly lowered her jar from her shoulder and said, 'Drink, and I'll water your camels too.' So I drank, and she watered the camels. 
I asked her, 'Whose daughter are you?' She said, 'The daughter of Bethuel, the son Milcah had by Nahor.' So I put the ring in her nose and the bracelets on her arms. 
Then I bowed low and worshiped God, and praised the God of my master Abraham, who had led me on the right road to get my master's niece for his son. 
Now tell me whether you'll be kind and faithful to my master. If not, say so, and I'll know which way to turn." 
Laban and Bethuel answered, "This comes from God. We can't say yes or no to you. 
Rebekah is right here in front of you. Take her and go, and let her be your master's son's wife, just as God has said." 
When Abraham's servant heard this, he bowed down to the ground before God. 
Then the servant brought out silver jewelry, gold jewelry, and clothes, and gave them to Rebekah. He also gave fine gifts to her brother and her mother. 
Then he and the men with him ate and drank and spent the night there. When they got up in the morning, he said, "Send me back to my master." 
Her brother and her mother said, "Let the young woman stay with us a few days, say ten, and then she can go." 
He said to them, "Don't hold me back, since God has made my journey succeed. Send me off so I can go to my master." 
They said, "We'll call the young woman and ask her." 
So they called Rebekah and asked her, "Will you go with this man?" She said, "I will." 
So they sent their sister Rebekah on her way, along with her nurse, Abraham's servant, and his men. 
They blessed Rebekah and said to her, "Our sister, may you become the mother of millions, and may your offspring take over the cities of those who hate them." 
Then Rebekah and her servant girls got up and mounted the camels and followed the man. So the servant took Rebekah and set off. 
Isaac had come in from Beer Lahai Roi, because he was living in the southern country. 
One evening Isaac went out to the field to think things over. He looked up and saw camels coming. 
Rebekah looked up, and when she saw Isaac, she slid down off her camel. 
She said to the servant, "Who's that man walking across the field toward us?" The servant said, "That's my master." So she took her veil and covered herself. 
Then the servant told Isaac everything that had happened. 
Isaac brought her into his mother Sarah's tent, and married Rebekah, and she became his wife. He loved her, and Isaac was comforted after his mother died.
