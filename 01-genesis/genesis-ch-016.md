Now Sarai, Abram's wife, bore him no children. She had an Egyptian servant named Hagar. 
Sarai said to Abram, "Look, God has kept me from having children. Please sleep with my servant. Perhaps I can build a family through her." Abram listened to Sarai. 
Sarai, Abram's wife, took Hagar the Egyptian, her servant, after Abram had lived ten years in the land of Canaan, and gave her to Abram her husband to be his wife. 
He slept with Hagar, and she became pregnant. When she saw that she was pregnant, she looked down on her mistress. 
Sarai said to Abram, "This wrong is your fault. I put my servant in your arms, and now that she is pregnant she looks down on me. May God judge between you and me." 
But Abram said to Sarai, "Look, your servant is in your hands. Do with her whatever you think best." Then Sarai treated her so harshly that she ran away from her. 
God's messenger found her by a spring of water in the wilderness, the spring on the road to Shur. 
He said, "Hagar, Sarai's servant, where did you come from? Where are you going?" She said, "I am running away from my mistress Sarai." 
God's messenger said to her, "Go back to your mistress and submit to her." 
God's messenger said to her, "I will greatly multiply your offspring, so that they will be too many to count." 
God's messenger said to her, "Look, you are pregnant and will have a son. You must name him Ishmael, because God has heard about your suffering. 
He will be like a wild donkey among men. His hand will be against every man, and every man's hand against him. He will live in conflict with all his brothers." 
She named God, who had spoken to her, "You are the God who sees me," because she said, "Have I really seen God and stayed alive?" 
That is why the well was called Beer Lahai Roi. Look, it is between Kadesh and Bered. 
Hagar bore a son for Abram, and Abram named his son, whom Hagar bore, Ishmael. 
Abram was eighty-six years old when Hagar bore Ishmael to Abram.
