# RLV Bible

**The Bible: Real Life Version**

A rendering of the World English Bible into plain modern English, made by people who
wanted to read it without translating it in their heads first.

---

## What this is

Most English Bibles are not written in English. They are written in a dialect that only
exists inside churches, a dialect that sounds like English, borrows English words, and
means something different by them. You can read a chapter of it and come away with the
impression that you have understood, when what you have actually done is recognize the
shape of sentences you have heard before.

This version is an attempt to remove that. It takes the World English Bible, which is a
faithful and public-domain rendering of the original languages, and re-renders it so that
the words do the work instead of the associations.

It is not a new translation out of Greek and Hebrew. It is a re-rendering of an existing
one, which means the scholarship that put the text into English in the first place is
already in place, and this project's work is the layer above it: taking what is accurate
and making it sayable.

## The conviction underneath it

The words should speak what they mean. That is the whole project in one line.

Every choice below is a consequence of it. If a word has to be explained after it is read,
it has failed. If a word arrives carrying a picture nobody in the room is actually seeing,
it has failed. If a sentence has to be untangled before it can be understood, it has
failed, however correct it is.

Three things follow from that conviction.

**Christianese has to go.** Not because the old words are wrong, but because they have
worn into sound. *Repentance* no longer means turning around; it means a church word.
*Justification* no longer means being put in the right; it means a doctrine. *Sin* no
longer means the condition everyone is in; it means rule breaking. A word that has to be
looked up has stopped carrying anything. Where the old word still has force for someone
who has never been inside a church, it stays. *Christ*, *Lord*, *the gathering*,
*spokesman*, these still land. *Heresy*, *baptism*, and *glory* imagined as a shining
substance, these do not.

**The mental images have to go.** This is the part that is easy to miss. A translation can
be plain and still be full of pictures nobody meant to draw. English *soul* summons a
detachable ghost. *Flesh* in the old versions summons a body that is bad. *Glory* summons
light. *Hell* summons a place and a temperature. *Hell* is not in the text at all; the
Greek has Hades and Gehenna, two different words with two different histories, and this
version keeps them apart instead of melting them together. When a picture is not in the
original, it is not in here.

**The small words have to be read up, and the big ones read down.** Tradition tends to
flatten the words that describe what God actually does, and inflate the words that
describe the machinery around it. *Blessed* becomes a religious mood; the Greek says
flourishing. *Saved* becomes a status; the Greek says healed and made whole. *Eternal*
becomes a philosophical property; the Greek says of the era. *Church* becomes a building
and an institution; the Greek says the gathering, the people actually assembled.
Meanwhile *glory* gets inflated into a shining substance when the Hebrew is talking about
weight, and *the kingdom of God* gets inflated into a place when the Greek is talking
about a reign, something happening, a king actually reigning.

So this version reads both directions at once. Ordinary words get their force back.
Inflated words get put back on the ground.

## The stance

This is written from a biblical universalist position: the conviction that the scriptures
teach restorative justice and the rescue of all, each in his own order (1 Cor 15:23).

That conviction is not smuggled into the text. The rule is the opposite of smuggling: no
verse gets stretched toward the conclusion, and no verse gets softened away from it either.
Where the text says everyone, it says everyone (1 Tim 2:6, "who gave himself as a ransom
for everyone"). Where the text says aionios, it says of the era, because that is the word.
Where the text describes judgment, the judgment is left as severe as it is.

The stance shapes what the version refuses to do. It refuses to import later doctrinal
furniture into first-century sentences. It refuses the habit of reading every hard passage
through the lens of endless punishment and then mistranslating to protect the lens. And it
refuses the opposite error of turning every warning into a mist. What is left is the text
doing its own work, which turns out to be both more hopeful and more serious than either
flattening.

## How the English actually reads

The test for every sentence is a person of about twenty in 2026 with no church background.
If they would not say it, it does not go in. Not "would they understand it," which is a
much lower bar. Would they say it.

That produces prose that is spoken rather than composed. Contractions, because people use
them. Short sentences where the Greek is short. *Will* instead of *shall*, except in a
quotation of the law, which keeps its own grammar and is never swept. Semicolons broken
into full stops most of the time. Two-beat pairings kept, because Hebrew and Greek both
like them and they still work in English. Roughly the same sentence order as the source, a
little looser in the letters, because the letters were dictated fast and read like it.

And no dashes. None. Every dash in the source is resolved by asking what it was doing:
apposition becomes a comma, two independent clauses become a full stop, a list becomes a
colon, a parenthesis becomes parentheses. A dash is a shortcut, and a shortcut in a
sentence is usually a place where the writer has not decided what the relation is.

## What it looks like

| Reference | The usual English | Here |
|---|---|---|
| Rom 3:23 | all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God | all have drifted, and fall short of the weight of God |
| Matt 4:17 | Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand | Have a change of heart, because the reign of the sky is at hand |
| John 3:16 | should not perish but have eternal life | shouldn't be lost, but have the real life of the era |
| Rom 6:23 | the wages of sin is death | What the drift owes is death |
| 1 John 2:2 | he is the propitiation for our sins | He's the sacrifice that makes things right for our mistakes |
| Eph 1:10 | to gather together in one all things in Christ | to gather up all things in Christ |
| 1 Tim 2:6 | who gave himself a ransom for all | who gave himself as a ransom for everyone |
| Matt 5:3 | Blessed are the poor in spirit | Flourishing are the ones who know they have nothing to offer God |

Each of those is a decision recorded in a rule, with the reasoning and the precedent behind
it. None of them is a preference.

## How it is made

There is a constitution. It is a working document, and it is law: a routing table with one
line per word in the original languages, the English it is rendered as, and one sentence on
why. Nothing gets changed without a row. Nothing gets a row without an argument. When a row
is not enough to settle a dispute, there is a longer companion document with the full
reasoning, the precedents, and the history of every ruling that got overturned.

The method, in order:

1. **Read the chapter cold**, in the current text, before touching it. Note what is accurate
   and what is unreadable. They are different problems.
2. **Check every rendering against the routing table, lemma by lemma.** Not against a
   hand-written list of English words to swap, which is how a version drifts. Against the
   Greek and Hebrew words themselves, using the source's own tagging.
3. **Rewrite for speech.** Same meaning, said the way a person says it. Keep the two-beat
   pairings and the repetitions that are doing work. Cut the ones that are not.
4. **Sweep for residue.** Every chapter gets scanned afterward for the debris that
   mechanical passes leave: doubled articles, orphaned commas, a contraction that broke a
   formula, a lowercase start after a full stop.
5. **Read it three more times.** Once for sense, once out loud, once for anything that
   sounds like a translation.
6. **Verify the format**, then commit that chapter and only that chapter.

The single hardest discipline is the one that sounds trivial: never assume a rule is
applied just because it is written down. A search is case-sensitive or it is not, a word
boundary is there or it is not, and the gap between those two facts and the intention is
where most of the real work of this project has gone.

## Format, for anyone building on it

One folder per book, numbered in canonical order so the tree sorts correctly:

```
01-genesis/genesis-ch-001.md
01-genesis/genesis-ch-002.md
...
66-revelation/revelation-ch-022.md
```

Each file holds one chapter and verses only: no book title line, no chapter heading. Verse
*V* sits on line *V*, so the verse number is the line number, which makes every file
trivially addressable by any tool that can read lines.

Every file is UTF-8 with BOM, LF line endings only, ends with a newline, carries exactly one
trailing space on every line, uses straight quotes only, and contains no em or en dashes.

## Source

Rendered from the **World English Bible (WEB)**, which is in the public domain. The WEB is
itself a modern-English update of the American Standard Version, which is why it is a good
starting point: the underlying text is careful, and the English is only one layer behind the
original rather than three.

## Status

- **New Testament: 27 books, 260 chapters, 7,953 verses.** Complete, with the readability
  pass finished through the Gospels.
- **Old Testament: 39 books, 929 chapters, 23,145 verses.** Complete, readability pass in
  progress, running chapter by chapter from Genesis forward.
- **Total: 1,189 chapters, 31,098 verses.**

The work is done a chapter at a time and committed one chapter at a time, so the history is
the record of the sweep.

## Why bother

Because the text is better than the way it is usually said. Because a person who has never
been inside a church should be able to open it and meet the thing itself, not a dialect
about it. And because the habit of explaining a sentence to yourself while reading it is a
habit that hides the sentence.

If the words speak what they mean, there is nothing left to translate.
