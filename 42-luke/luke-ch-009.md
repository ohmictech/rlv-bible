He called the twelve together and gave them power and authority over all unclean spirits, and to heal diseases. 
He sent them out to preach God's reign and to heal the sick. 
He said to them, "Take nothing for your journey: no staff, nor bag, nor bread, nor money. Don't have two tunics each. 
Into whatever house you enter, stay there, and depart from there. 
As many as don't receive you, when you depart from that city, shake off even the dust from your feet as a witness against them." 
They departed and went throughout the villages, preaching the Good News and healing everywhere. 
Now Herod the district ruler heard of all that was done by him; and he was very perplexed, because it was said by some that John had been raised from the dead, 
and by some that Elijah had appeared, and by others that one of the old spokesmen had risen again. 
Herod said, "I beheaded John, but who's this about whom I hear such things?" He tried to see him. 
The ones sent out, when they had returned, told him what things they had done. He took them and withdrew by themselves to a deserted region near a city called Bethsaida. 
But the crowds, perceiving it, followed him. He welcomed them, spoke to them of God's reign, and he healed those who needed healing. 
Late in the day the twelve came and said to him, "Send the crowd away, that they can go into the surrounding villages and farms and lodge and get food, for we're here in an empty place." 
But he said to them, "You give them something to eat." They said, "We have no more than five loaves and two fish, unless we should go and buy food for all these people." 
They were about five thousand men. He said to his students, "Make them sit down in groups of about fifty each." 
They did so, and made them all sit down. 
He took the five loaves and the two fish, and looking up to the sky, he blessed them, broke them, and gave them to the students to set before the crowd. 
They ate and were all filled. They gathered up twelve baskets of broken pieces that were left over. 
As he was praying alone, the students were near him, and he asked them, "Who do the crowds say that I am?" 
They answered, "'John the Baptist,' but others say, 'Elijah,' and others, that one of the old spokesmen has risen again." 
He said to them, "But who do you say that I am?" Peter answered, "The Christ of God." 
But he warned them and commanded them to tell this to no one, 
saying, "The Human One must suffer many things, and be rejected by the elders, chief priests, and Bible teachers, and be killed, and the third day be raised up." 
He said to all, "If anyone desires to come after me, let him disown himself, take up his cross, and follow me. 
Whoever desires to rescue his life will lose it, but whoever will lose his life for my sake will rescue it. 
What does it profit a man if he gains the whole world, and loses or forfeits his own self? 
Whoever will be ashamed of me and of my words, of him will the Human One be ashamed when he comes in his weight, and the weight of the Father, and of the set-apart messengers. 
But I tell you the truth: There are some standing here who will in no way taste death until they see God's reign." 
About eight days after these sayings, he took with him Peter, John, and James, and went up onto the mountain to pray. 
As he was praying, the appearance of his face was altered, and his clothing became white and dazzling. 
Look, two men were talking with him, Moses and Elijah, 
who appeared in weight and spoke of his departure, which he was about to accomplish at Jerusalem. 
Now Peter and those who were with him were heavy with sleep, but when they were fully awake, they saw his weight, and the two men who stood with him. 
As they were leaving him, Peter said to Jesus, "Master, it's good for us to be here. Let's make three tents: one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah," not knowing what he said. 
While he said these things, a cloud came and overshadowed them, and they were afraid as they entered into the cloud. 
A voice came out of the cloud, saying, "This is my Son, whom I truly love. Listen to him!" 
When the voice came, Jesus was found alone. They were silent, and told no one in those days any of the things which they had seen. 
On the next day, when they had come down from the mountain, a great crowd met him. 
Look, a man from the crowd called out, saying, "Teacher, I beg you to look at my son, for he's my only child. 
Look, a spirit takes him, he suddenly cries out, and it throws him into convulsions so that he foams at the mouth; and it hardly ever leaves him, bruising him badly. 
I begged your students to drive it out, and they couldn't." 
Jesus answered, "You generation with no trust! You twisted generation! How long must I be with you and put up with you? Bring your son here." 
While he was still coming, the unclean spirit threw him down and convulsed him violently. But Jesus rebuked the unclean spirit, healed the boy, and gave him back to his father. 
They were all astonished at the majesty of God. But while everyone was amazed at all the things Jesus was doing, he said to his students, 
"Let these words sink into your ears, for the Human One will be handed over into the hands of people." 
But they didn't understand this saying. It was concealed from them, that they shouldn't perceive it, and they were afraid to ask him about this saying. 
An argument arose among them about which of them was the greatest. 
Jesus, perceiving the reasoning of their hearts, took a little child, and set him by his side, 
and said to them, "Whoever welcomes this little child in my name welcomes me. Whoever welcomes me welcomes him who sent me. The one who's least among you all, this one will be great." 
John answered, "Master, we saw someone driving out unclean spirits in your name, and we told him to stop, because he doesn't follow along with us." 
Jesus said to him, "Don't stop him, for he who isn't against us is for us." 
When the days were near for him to be taken up, he set his face firmly to go to Jerusalem 
and sent messengers ahead of him. They went and entered into a village of the Samaritans, so as to prepare for him. 
They didn't receive him, because he was traveling with his face set toward Jerusalem. 
When his students, James and John, saw this, they said, "Lord, do you want us to command fire to come down from the sky and burn them up, just as Elijah did?" 
But he turned and rebuked them, "You don't know of what kind of spirit you are. 
The Human One didn't come to break up people's lives, but to rescue them." They went to another village. 
As they went on the way, a certain man said to him, "I want to follow you wherever you go, Lord." 
Jesus said to him, "The foxes have holes and the birds of the sky have nests, but the Human One has no place to lay his head." 
He said to another, "Follow me!" But he said, "Lord, allow me first to go and bury my father." 
But Jesus said to him, "Leave the dead to bury their own dead, but you go and announce God's reign." 
Another also said, "I want to follow you, Lord, but first allow me to say good-bye to those who are at my house." 
But Jesus said to him, "No one, having put his hand to the plow and looking back, is fit for God's reign."
