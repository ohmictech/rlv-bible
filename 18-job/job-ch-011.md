Then Zophar, the Naamathite, answered, 
"Shouldn't the multitude of words be answered? Should a man full of talk be justified? 
Should your boastings make men hold their peace? When you mock, will no man make you ashamed? 
You say, 'My doctrine is pure. I'm clean in your eyes.' 
But oh that God would speak, and open his lips against you, 
that he'd show you the secrets of wisdom! True wisdom has two sides. Know that God exacts of you less than your guilt deserves. 
"Can you fathom the mystery of God? Or can you probe the limits of the Almighty? 
They're high as sky. What can you do? They're deeper than Hades. What can you know? 
Its measure is longer than the earth, and broader than the sea. 
If he passes by, or confines, or convenes a court, then who can oppose him? 
He knows false men. He sees guilt also, even though he doesn't consider it. 
An empty-headed man becomes wise when a man is born as a wild donkey's colt. 
"If you set your heart aright, stretch out your hands toward him. 
If guilt is in your hand, put it far away. Don't let unrighteousness dwell in your tents. 
Surely then you'll lift up your face without spot. Yes, you'll be steadfast, and won't fear, 
for you'll forget your misery. You'll remember it like waters that have passed away. 
Life will be clearer than the noonday. Though there's darkness, it'll be as the morning. 
You'll be secure, because there's hope. Yes, you'll search, and will take your rest in safety. 
Also you'll lie down, and no one will make you afraid. Yes, many will court your favor. 
But the eyes of the guilty will fail. They'll have no way to flee. Their hope will be the giving up of the spirit."
