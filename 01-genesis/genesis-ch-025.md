Abraham married another wife, whose name was Keturah. 
She had Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian, Ishbak, and Shuah by him. 
Jokshan was the father of Sheba and Dedan. Dedan's sons were Asshurim, Letushim, and Leummim. 
Midian's sons were Ephah, Epher, Hanoch, Abida, and Eldaah. All of these were Keturah's children. 
Abraham gave everything he owned to Isaac, 
but he gave gifts to the sons of his concubines, and while he was still alive he sent them away from his son Isaac, eastward to the land of the east. 
Abraham lived a hundred and seventy-five years. 
He breathed his last and died at a good old age, an old man who had lived his full span, and he was gathered to his people. 
His sons Isaac and Ishmael buried him in the cave of Machpelah, in the field of Ephron son of Zohar the Hittite, near Mamre, 
the field Abraham had bought from the Hittites. He was buried there with his wife Sarah. 
After Abraham died, God blessed his son Isaac, and Isaac lived near Beer Lahai Roi. 
This is the family record of Ishmael, Abraham's son, whom Hagar the Egyptian, Sarah's servant, had for Abraham. 
These are the names of Ishmael's sons, in the order they were born: his firstborn Nebaioth, then Kedar, Adbeel, Mibsam, 
Mishma, Dumah, Massa, 
Hadad, Tema, Jetur, Naphish, and Kedemah. 
These were Ishmael's sons, and these are their names by their villages and camps: twelve chiefs, each over his own people. 
Ishmael lived a hundred and thirty-seven years. He breathed his last, died, and was gathered to his people. 
His descendants lived from Havilah to Shur, which is near Egypt, as you head toward Assyria. He settled there, within sight of all his relatives. 
This is the family record of Isaac, Abraham's son. Abraham was Isaac's father. 
Isaac was forty years old when he married Rebekah, daughter of Bethuel the Aramean from Paddan Aram, and sister of Laban the Aramean. 
Isaac begged God on behalf of his wife, because she couldn't have children. God answered his prayer, and his wife Rebekah became pregnant. 
The babies fought each other inside her, and she said, "If it's like this, why go on living?" So she went to ask God about it. 
God said to her, "Two nations are in your womb, and two peoples will come out of you and split apart. One will be stronger than the other, and the older will serve the younger." 
When her time came, there were twins in her womb. 
The first one came out reddish, covered with hair like a fur coat, and they named him Esau. 
Then his brother came out, with his hand gripping Esau's heel, so he was named Jacob. Isaac was sixty years old when she had them. 
The boys grew up. Esau became a skilled hunter, a man of the outdoors, while Jacob was a quiet man who stayed among the tents. 
Isaac loved Esau because he brought home meat to eat, but Rebekah loved Jacob. 
One day Jacob was cooking stew, and Esau came in from the field, exhausted. 
Esau said to Jacob, "Give me some of that red stuff, please. I'm starving." That's why he was called Edom. 
Jacob said, "First sell me your birthright." 
Esau said, "Look, I'm dying here. What good is a birthright to me?" 
Jacob said, "Swear it to me first." So Esau swore to him, and sold him his birthright. 
Then Jacob gave Esau bread and lentil stew. He ate and drank, got up, and walked off. That's how little Esau thought of his birthright.
