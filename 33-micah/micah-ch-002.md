Woe to those who devise guilt and work bad on their beds! When the morning is light, they practice it, because it's in the power of their hand. 
They covet fields and seize them, and houses, then take them away. They oppress a man and his house, even a man and his heritage. 
So God says: "Look, I'm planning against these people a disaster, from which you won't remove your necks, neither will you walk haughtily, for it's an bad time. 
In that day they'll take up a parable against you, and lament with a doleful lamentation, saying, 'We're utterly ruined! My people's possession is divided up. Indeed he takes it from me and assigns our fields to traitors!'" 
So you'll have no one who divides the land by lot in God's assembly. 
"Don't prophesy!", they prophesy, "Don't prophesy about these things. Disgrace won't overtake us." 
Shall it be said, O house of Jacob, "Is God's Spirit angry? Are these his doings? Don't my words do good to him who walks blamelessly?" 
But lately my people have risen up as an enemy. You strip the robe and clothing from those who pass by without a care, returning from battle. 
You drive the women of my people out from their pleasant houses; from their young children you take away my blessing for the eras. 
Arise, and depart! This isn't your resting place, because of uncleanness that destroys, even with a grievous destruction. 
If a man walking in a spirit of falsehood lies, saying, "I'll prophesy to you of wine and of strong drink," he'd be the spokesman of this people. 
I'll surely assemble all of you, Jacob. I'll surely gather the remnant of Israel. I'll put them together as the sheep of Bozrah, as a flock in the middle of their pasture. They'll swarm with people. 
He who breaks open the way goes up before them. They break through the gate, and go out. Their king passes on before them, with God at their head.
