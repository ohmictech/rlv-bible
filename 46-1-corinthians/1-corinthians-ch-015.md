Now I want you to know, brothers, the Good News I announced to you, which you also received, and in which you also stand, 
by which you are also made whole, if you hold firmly to the word I announced to you, unless you trusted for nothing. 
I delivered to you first of all that which I also received: that Christ died for our mistakes according to the Scriptures, 
that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures, 
and that he appeared to Cephas, then to the twelve. 
Then he appeared to over five hundred brothers at once, most of whom remain until now, but some have also fallen asleep. 
Then he appeared to James, then to all the sent ones, 
and last of all, as to the child born at the wrong time, he appeared to me also. 
I am the least of the sent ones, and I'm not fit to be called a sent one, because I hunted down the gathering of God. 
But by the favor of God I am what I am. His favor to me wasn't wasted, but I worked harder than everyone; yet not I, but the favor of God that was with me. 
Whether then it is I or they, so we announce, and so you trusted. 
Now if Christ is announced as raised from the dead, how can some of you say that there is no rising again of the dead? 
But if there is no rising again of the dead, then Christ hasn't been raised either. 
If Christ hasn't been raised, then our announcing is empty and your trust is empty too. 
Yes, and we are found to be false witnesses about God, because we testified that God raised up Christ, whom he didn't raise up if the dead aren't raised. 
If the dead aren't raised, neither has Christ been raised. 
If Christ hasn't been raised, your trust is empty; you are still in your mistakes. 
Then those who have fallen asleep in Christ have also been lost. 
If our expectation in Christ is only for this life, we are of everyone the most to be pitied. 
But now Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep. 
Since death came by a human being, the rising again of the dead also comes by a human being. 
As in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive. 
But each in his own order: Christ the firstfruits, then those who belong to Christ at his arrival. 
Then the end comes, when he hands the reign over to God the Father, when he has put out of work all rule and all authority and power. 
He must reign until he has put all his enemies under his feet. 
The last enemy to be put out of work is death. 
For, "He put all things in subjection under his feet." But when he says, "All things are put in subjection", it is evident that he is excepted who subjected all things to him. 
When all things have been subjected to him, then the Son will also himself be subjected to him who subjected all things to him, that God may be all in all. 
Or else what will those do who are immersed for the dead? If the dead aren't raised at all, why then are they immersed for them? 
Why do we also stand in jeopardy every hour? 
I affirm, by my pride in you which I have in Christ Jesus our Lord, I die daily. 
If I fought with animals at Ephesus for human purposes, what does it profit me? If the dead aren't raised, then "let's eat and drink, for tomorrow we die." 
Don't be led astray! "Bad company spoils good habits." 
Wake up as you should, and don't drift, for some have no knowledge of God. I say this to your shame. 
But someone will say, "How are the dead raised?" and, "With what kind of body do they come?" 
You foolish one, that which you yourself sow isn't made alive unless it dies. 
That which you sow, you don't sow the body that will be, but a bare grain, maybe of wheat, or of some other kind. 
But God gives it a body even as it pleased him, and to each seed a body of its own. 
All flesh isn't the same flesh, but there is one flesh of men, another flesh of animals, another of fish, and another of birds. 
There are also bodies in the sky and bodies on the land; but the weight of the one in the sky differs from the weight of the one on the land. 
There is one weight of the sun, another weight of the moon, and another weight of the stars; for one star differs from another star in weight. 
So also is the rising again of the dead. The body is sown decaying; it is raised undecaying. 
It is sown in dishonor; it is raised in weight. It is sown in weakness; it is raised in power. 
It is sown a natural body; it is raised a spiritual body. There is a natural body and there is also a spiritual body. 
So also it is written, "The first man Adam became a living being." The last Adam became a spirit that makes alive. 
However, that which is spiritual isn't first, but that which is natural, then that which is spiritual. 
The first man is from the land, made of dust. The second man is the Lord from the sky. 
As the one made of dust was, so are those made of dust; and as the one from the sky is, so are those who are from the sky. 
And as we have borne the image of the one made of dust, let's also bear the image of the one from the sky. 
Now I say this, brothers, that flesh and blood can't be given charge of God's reign; neither does what decays take charge of what doesn't decay. 
Look, I tell you a secret. We won't all fall asleep, but we will all be changed, 
in a moment, in the blink of an eye, at the last trumpet. The trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised undecaying, and we will be changed. 
This decaying body must become undecaying, and this dying body must put on undying life. 
But when this decaying body has become undecaying, and this dying body has put on undying life, then what is written will happen: "Death is swallowed up in victory." 
"Death, where is your sting? Hades, where is your victory?" 
The sting of death is the drift, and the power of the drift is the law. 
But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ. 
So, my truly loved brothers, stand firm, don't be moved, always doing more in the Lord's work, because you know that your toil isn't empty in the Lord.
