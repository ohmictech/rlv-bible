About that time Judah left his brothers and went to stay with a man from Adullam named Hirah. 
There Judah met the daughter of a Canaanite man named Shua, and he married her and slept with her. 
She became pregnant and had a son, and he named him Er. 
She became pregnant again and had a son, and she named him Onan. 
She had another son and named him Shelah. She was at Chezib when he was born. 
Judah found a wife for his firstborn Er, and her name was Tamar. 
But Er, Judah's firstborn, did what was bad in God's sight, so God put him to death. 
Then Judah said to Onan, "Sleep with your brother's wife and do your duty as her brother-in-law, so your brother will have children through her." 
But Onan knew the children wouldn't count as his, so whenever he slept with his brother's wife, he spilled his semen on the ground so he wouldn't give his brother any children. 
What he did was bad in God's sight, so God put him to death too. 
Then Judah said to his daughter-in-law Tamar, "Go back and live as a widow in your father's house until my son Shelah is grown." He was thinking, "I don't want him to die too, like his brothers." So Tamar went and lived in her father's house. 
After a long time, Judah's wife, the daughter of Shua, died. When Judah had finished mourning, he went up to Timnah to his sheep shearers, along with his friend Hirah from Adullam. 
Tamar was told, "Your father-in-law is on his way up to Timnah to shear his sheep." 
So she took off her widow's clothes, covered herself with a veil, and wrapped up in it, and sat down at the entrance to Enaim, on the road to Timnah. She could see that Shelah had grown up, and she still hadn't been given to him as his wife. 
When Judah saw her, he thought she was a prostitute, because she had covered her face. 
He went over to her by the roadside and said, "Let me sleep with you," not knowing she was his daughter-in-law. She asked, "What will you give me if you do?" 
He said, "I'll send you a young goat from the flock." She said, "Will you leave me something as a pledge until you send it?" 
He said, "What should I give you?" She said, "Your seal and its cord, and that walking stick in your hand." So he gave them to her, slept with her, and she became pregnant by him. 
Then she got up and left, took off her veil, and put her widow's clothes back on. 
Judah sent his friend from Adullam to deliver the young goat and get the pledge back from the woman, but he couldn't find her. 
So he asked the men of that place, "Where's the prostitute who was by the road at Enaim?" They said, "There's never been a prostitute here." 
He went back to Judah and said, "I couldn't find her, and the men there told me, 'There's never been a prostitute here.'" 
Judah said, "Let her keep the things, or we'll be a laughingstock. I did send the goat, and you couldn't find her." 
About three months later Judah was told, "Your daughter-in-law Tamar has been sleeping around, and now she's pregnant." Judah said, "Bring her out, and burn her." 
As she was being brought out, she sent a message to her father-in-law: "I'm pregnant by the man who owns these." And she added, "Take a look. Whose seal and cord and walking stick are these?" 
Judah recognized them and said, "She's more in the right than I am, because I didn't give her to my son Shelah." And he didn't sleep with her again. 
When her time came to give birth, there were twins in her womb. 
While she was in labor, one of them put out a hand, and the midwife tied a scarlet thread around his wrist, saying, "This one came out first." 
But he pulled his hand back, and his brother came out first. She said, "So you've broken your way out!" And he was named Perez. 
Then his brother came out, the one with the scarlet thread on his wrist, and he was named Zerah.
