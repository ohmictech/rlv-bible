In Iconium, they entered together into the gathering place of the Jewish people, and so spoke that a great crowd both of Jewish people and of Greeks trusted. 
But the Jewish people who wouldn't trust stirred up and embittered the lives of the nations against the brothers. 
So they stayed there a long time, speaking boldly in the Lord, who testified to the word of his favor, granting signs and wonders to be done by their hands. 
But the crowd of the city was divided. Part sided with the Jewish people and part with the sent ones. 
When some of both the nations and the Jewish people, with their rulers, made a violent attempt to mistreat and stone them, 
they became aware of it and fled to the cities of Lycaonia, Lystra, Derbe, and the surrounding region. 
There they announced the Good News. 
At Lystra a certain man sat, powerless in his feet, a cripple from his mother's womb, who never had walked. 
He was listening to Paul speaking, who, fastening eyes on him and seeing that he had trust to be made whole, 
said with a loud voice, "Stand upright on your feet!" He leaped up and walked. 
When the crowd saw what Paul had done, they lifted up their voice, saying in the language of Lycaonia, "The gods have come down to us looking like people!" 
They called Barnabas "Jupiter", and Paul "Mercury", because he was the chief speaker. 
The priest of Jupiter, whose temple was in front of their city, brought oxen and garlands to the gates, and would have made a sacrifice along with the crowd. 
But when the sent ones, Barnabas and Paul, heard of it, they tore their clothes and sprang into the crowd, crying out, 
"Men, why are you doing these things? We also are men of the same nature as you, and bring you good news, that you should turn from these empty things to the living God, who made the sky, the land, the sea, and all that is in them; 
who in the generations gone by allowed all the nations to walk in their own ways. 
Yet he didn't leave himself without witness, in that he did good and gave you rains from the sky and fruitful seasons, filling our hearts with food and gladness." 
Even saying these things, they hardly stopped the crowd from making a sacrifice to them. 
But some Jewish people from Antioch and Iconium came there, and having persuaded the crowd, they stoned Paul and dragged him out of the city, supposing that he was dead. 
But as the students stood around him, he rose up, and entered into the city. On the next day he went out with Barnabas to Derbe. 
When they had announced the Good News to that city and had made many students, they returned to Lystra, Iconium, and Antioch, 
strengthening the lives of the students, exhorting them to continue in the trust, and that through many hard times we must enter into God's reign. 
When they had appointed elders for them in every gathering, and had prayed with fasting, they commended them to the Lord on whom they had trusted. 
They passed through Pisidia and came to Pamphylia. 
When they had spoken the word in Perga, they went down to Attalia. 
From there they sailed to Antioch, from where they had been committed to the favor of God for the work which they had fulfilled. 
When they had arrived and had called the gathering together, they reported all the things that God had done with them, and that he had opened a door of trust to the nations. 
They stayed there with the students for a long time.
