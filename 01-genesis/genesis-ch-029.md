Jacob kept traveling and came to the land of the eastern peoples. 
He looked and saw a well in the field, with three flocks of sheep lying beside it, because the flocks were watered from that well. A large stone covered the mouth of the well. 
When all the flocks were gathered there, the shepherds would roll the stone away from the well and water the sheep, and then put it back in place. 
Jacob said to them, "Friends, where are you from?" They said, "We're from Haran." 
He asked, "Do you know Laban son of Nahor?" They said, "We do." 
He said, "How is he doing?" They said, "He's fine. Look, here comes his daughter Rachel with the sheep." 
He said, "It's still the middle of the day, not time to pen the animals. Water the sheep and take them back to pasture." 
They said, "We can't, until all the flocks are gathered and the stone is rolled away. Then we'll water the sheep." 
He was still talking with them when Rachel arrived with her father's sheep, because she was the one who looked after them. 
When Jacob saw Rachel, his uncle Laban's daughter, and Laban's sheep with her, he went over, rolled the stone away from the mouth of the well, and watered his uncle's flock. 
Then Jacob kissed Rachel and burst into tears. 
Jacob told Rachel that he was her father's relative and that he was Rebekah's son, and she ran to tell her father. 
When Laban heard the news about Jacob, his sister's son, he ran to meet him, hugged and kissed him, and brought him home. Then Jacob told Laban the whole story. 
Laban said, "You really are my own flesh and blood." And Jacob stayed with him for a month. 
Then Laban said to Jacob, "Just because you're my relative, should you work for me for nothing? Tell me what your wages should be." 
Laban had two daughters. The older one was named Leah, and the younger one Rachel. 
Leah had gentle eyes, but Rachel was beautiful, with a lovely face and figure. 
Jacob was in love with Rachel, so he said, "I'll work for you seven years for your younger daughter Rachel." 
Laban said, "It's better to give her to you than to some other man. Stay with me." 
So Jacob worked seven years for Rachel, and to him it felt like only a few days, because he loved her so much. 
Then Jacob said to Laban, "Give me my wife. My time is up, and I want to be with her." 
So Laban gathered all the men of the place and held a feast. 
But that evening he took his daughter Leah and brought her to Jacob, and Jacob slept with her. 
Laban gave his servant girl Zilpah to his daughter Leah as her maid. 
When morning came, it was Leah! Jacob said to Laban, "What have you done to me? Didn't I work for you for Rachel? Why did you trick me?" 
Laban said, "That's not how we do things here. We don't give the younger daughter before the older one. 
Finish this daughter's wedding week, and then we'll give you the other one too, in return for another seven years of work." 
Jacob did that and finished her week, and then Laban gave him his daughter Rachel as his wife. 
And Laban gave his servant girl Bilhah to his daughter Rachel as her maid. 
Jacob also slept with Rachel, and he loved her more than Leah, and he worked for Laban another seven years. 
When God saw that Leah was unloved, he gave her children, while Rachel had none. 
Leah became pregnant and had a son, and she named him Reuben, saying, "God has seen how miserable I am. Now my husband will love me." 
She became pregnant again and had a son, and said, "God heard that I'm unloved, so he gave me this one too." And she named him Simeon. 
She became pregnant again and had a son, and said, "Now at last my husband will be attached to me, because I've given him three sons." So she named him Levi. 
She became pregnant again and had a son, and said, "This time I'll praise God." So she named him Judah, and then she stopped having children.
