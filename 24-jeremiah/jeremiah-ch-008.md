"At that time," says God, "they'll bring the bones of the kings of Judah, the bones of his princes, the bones of the priests, the bones of the spokesmen, and the bones of the inhabitants of Jerusalem, out of their graves. 
They'll spread them before the sun, the moon, and all the army of the sky, which they have loved, which they have served, after which they have walked, which they have sought, and which they have worshiped. They won't be gathered or be buried. They'll be like dung on the surface of the earth. 
Death will be chosen rather than life by all the residue that remain of this bad family, that remain in all the places where I have driven them," says the God of Armies. 
"Moreover you shall tell them, 'God says: "'Do men fall, and not rise up again? Does one turn away, and not return? 
Why then have the people of Jerusalem fallen back by a perpetual backsliding? They cling to deceit. They refuse to return. 
I listened and heard, but they didn't say what's right. No one repents of his guilt, saying, "What have I done?" Everyone turns to his course, as a horse that rushes headlong in the battle. 
Yes, the stork in the sky knows her appointed times. The turtledove, the swallow, and the crane observe the time of their coming; but my people don't know God's law. 
"'How do you say, "We're wise, and God's law is with us"? But, look, the false pen of the Bible teachers has made that a lie. 
The wise men are disappointed. They're dismayed and trapped. Look, they have rejected God's word. What kind of wisdom is in them? 
So I'll give their wives to others and their fields to those who will possess them. Everyone from the least even to the greatest is given to covetousness; from the spokesman even to the priest everyone deals falsely. 
They have healed the hurt of the daughter of my people slightly, saying, "Peace, peace," when there's no peace. 
Were they ashamed when they had committed abomination? No, they weren't at all ashamed. They couldn't blush. So they'll fall among those who fall. In the time of their visitation they'll be cast down, says God. 
"'I'll utterly consume them, says God. No grapes will be on the vine, no figs on the fig tree, and the leaf will fade. The things that I have given them will pass away from them.'" 
"Why do we sit still? Assemble yourselves! Let's enter into the fortified cities, and let's be silent there; for our God has put us to silence, and given us poisoned water to drink, because we have wronged God. 
We looked for peace, but no good came; and for a time of healing, and look, dismay! 
The snorting of his horses is heard from Dan. The whole land trembles at the sound of the neighing of his strong ones; for they have come, and have devoured the land and all that's in it, the city and those who dwell therein." 
"For, look, I'll send serpents, adders among you, which won't be charmed; and they'll bite you," says God. 
Oh that I could comfort myself against sorrow! My heart is faint within me. 
Look, the voice of the cry of the daughter of my people from a land that's very far off: "Isn't God in Zion? Isn't her King in her?" "Why have they provoked me to anger with their engraved images, and with foreign idols?" 
"The harvest is past. The summer has ended, and we aren't saved." 
The hurt of the daughter of my people, I'm hurt. I mourn. Dismay has taken hold of me. 
Is there no balm in Gilead? Is there no physician there? Why then isn't the health of the daughter of my people recovered?
