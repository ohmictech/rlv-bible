The people complained to God, and God heard it. His anger burned, and God's fire burned among them and consumed some of the outskirts of the camp. 
The people cried out to Moses, and Moses prayed to God, and the fire died down. 
That place was named Taberah, because God's fire burned among them. 
The mixed crowd among them had a fierce craving, and the Israelites started weeping again and said, "Who will give us meat to eat? 
We remember the fish we ate in Egypt for nothing, the cucumbers, the melons, the leeks, the onions and the garlic, 
but now we have no appetite at all. There's nothing to look at except this manna." 
The manna was like coriander seed and looked like resin. 
The people went around gathering it, and ground it in mills or crushed it in mortars, and boiled it in pots and made cakes of it. It tasted like something made with fresh oil. 
When the dew fell on the camp at night, the manna fell on it. 
Moses heard the people weeping, family by family, each man at the entrance of his tent, and God's anger burned fiercely, and Moses was upset. 
Moses said to God, "Why have you treated your servant so badly? Why haven't I found favor with you, that you put the burden of all these people on me? 
Did I conceive all these people? Did I give birth to them, that you should tell me, 'Carry them in your arms, the way a nurse carries a nursing baby, to the land you swore to their fathers'? 
Where am I going to get meat for all these people? They keep weeping at me, saying, 'Give us meat to eat.' 
I can't carry all these people by myself. It's too heavy for me. 
If this is how you're going to treat me, please kill me now, if I have found favor with you, and don't let me see my own ruin." 
God said to Moses, "Gather seventy of Israel's elders for me, men you know to be the elders of the people and their officers, and bring them to the Tent of Meeting to stand there with you. 
I will come down and talk with you there. I will take some of the Spirit that is on you and put it on them, and they will carry the burden of the people with you, so you don't carry it alone. 
"Say to the people, 'Set yourselves apart for tomorrow, and you will eat meat, because you have wept in God's hearing and said, "Who will give us meat to eat? It was better for us in Egypt." So God will give you meat, and you will eat it. 
Not for one day, or two days, or five days, or ten days, or twenty days, 
but for a whole month, until it comes out of your nostrils and you are sick of it, because you rejected God, who is among you, and wept before him, saying, "Why did we ever leave Egypt?"'" 
Moses said, "There are six hundred thousand men on foot here with me, and you say, 'I will give them meat, and they will eat it for a whole month.' 
Will flocks and herds be slaughtered for them, to be enough? Or will all the fish of the sea be caught for them, to be enough?" 
God said to Moses, "Has God's hand grown short? Now you will see whether what I say happens or not." 
Moses went out and told the people God's words, and he gathered seventy of the elders of the people and set them around the Tent. 
God came down in the cloud and spoke to him, and took some of the Spirit that was on him and put it on the seventy elders. When the Spirit rested on them, they spoke for God, but they never did it again. 
Two men had stayed in the camp. One was named Eldad and the other Medad, and the Spirit rested on them. They were among those listed, but they hadn't gone out to the Tent, and they spoke for God in the camp. 
A young man ran and told Moses, "Eldad and Medad are speaking for God in the camp!" 
Joshua son of Nun, Moses' servant from his youth, said, "My lord Moses, stop them!" 
Moses said to him, "Are you jealous for me? I wish all God's people were spokesmen, and that God would put his Spirit on them!" 
Then Moses went back into the camp, he and the elders of Israel. 
A wind from God went out and brought quail in from the sea and let them fall around the camp, about a day's walk on this side and a day's walk on that side, all around the camp, about three feet deep on the ground. 
The people got up all that day, all night and all the next day and gathered the quail. The one who gathered least gathered ten homers, and they spread them out all around the camp. 
While the meat was still between their teeth, before it was chewed, God's anger burned against the people, and God struck them with a very great plague. 
That place was named Kibroth Hattaavah, because there they buried the people who had craved. 
From Kibroth Hattaavah the people traveled to Hazeroth and stayed there.
