"'This is the law of the offering for guilt. It is most holy. 
In the place where they kill the burnt offering, they are to kill the offering for guilt, and its blood is to be splashed against all sides of the altar. 
He is to offer all its fat: the fat tail, the fat that covers the inner organs, 
the two kidneys with the fat on them by the loins, and the covering over the liver, which he is to take out with the kidneys, 
and the priest is to burn them on the altar as an offering made by fire to God. It is an offering for guilt. 
Every male among the priests may eat it. It is to be eaten in a holy place. It is most holy. 
"'The same law applies to the offering for wrongdoing and the offering for guilt. They belong to the priest who makes things right with them. 
The priest who offers someone's burnt offering keeps the skin of the burnt offering he has offered. 
Every gift offering baked in an oven, and everything cooked in a pan or on a griddle, belongs to the priest who offers it. 
Every gift offering, whether mixed with oil or dry, belongs to all the sons of Aaron, one as much as another. 
"'This is the law of the sacrifice of the wholeness offering that someone brings to God. 
If he brings it as a thanksgiving, then along with the thanksgiving sacrifice he is to bring cakes made without yeast mixed with oil, wafers made without yeast with oil poured over them, and cakes of fine flour mixed with oil. 
With the sacrifice of thanksgiving for his wholeness offering, he is to bring cakes of bread made with yeast. 
From it he is to offer one of each kind as a contribution to God, and it belongs to the priest who splashes the blood of the wholeness offerings. 
The meat of the sacrifice of his thanksgiving wholeness offering is to be eaten on the day he offers it. None of it is to be left until morning. 
"'But if the sacrifice he offers is a vow or a freewill offering, it is to be eaten on the day he offers the sacrifice, and what is left of it may be eaten the next day, 
but whatever meat of the sacrifice is left on the third day must be burned with fire. 
If any of the meat of the sacrifice of his wholeness offering is eaten on the third day, it will not be accepted and it will not count for the one who offered it. It is a disgusting thing, and the person who eats any of it will carry his guilt. 
"'Meat that touches anything unclean must not be eaten. It must be burned with fire. As for the rest of the meat, anyone who is clean may eat it, 
but the person who eats meat from the wholeness offering that belongs to God while his uncleanness is on him is to be cut off from his people. 
When anyone touches anything unclean, whether human uncleanness, or an unclean animal, or any unclean crawling thing, and then eats some of the meat of the wholeness offering that belongs to God, that person is to be cut off from his people.'" 
God spoke to Moses: 
"Speak to the Israelites and tell them, 'You are never to eat fat from an ox, a sheep or a goat. 
The fat of an animal found dead or torn by wild animals may be used for any other purpose, but you must never eat it. 
Whoever eats the fat of an animal that people bring as an offering made by fire to God is to be cut off from his people. 
Wherever you live, you are not to eat any blood, whether from a bird or an animal. 
Anyone who eats any blood is to be cut off from his people.'" 
God spoke to Moses: 
"Speak to the Israelites and tell them, 'Whoever offers the sacrifice of his wholeness offering to God is to bring his offering to God from that sacrifice. 
He is to bring God's offerings made by fire with his own hands. He is to bring the fat with the breast, so the breast can be waved as a wave offering before God. 
The priest is to burn the fat on the altar, but the breast belongs to Aaron and his sons. 
You are to give the right thigh to the priest as a contribution from the sacrifices of your wholeness offerings. 
The son of Aaron who offers the blood and the fat of the wholeness offerings is to have the right thigh as his share. 
I have taken the breast that is waved and the thigh that is contributed from the Israelites out of the sacrifices of their wholeness offerings, and given them to Aaron the priest and his sons as their share for the eras from the Israelites.'" 
This is the share set apart for Aaron and his sons from God's offerings made by fire, on the day he presented them to serve God as priests, 
which God commanded to be given to them by the Israelites on the day he anointed them. It is their share for the eras throughout their generations. 
This is the law of the burnt offering, the gift offering, the offering for wrongdoing, the offering for guilt, the ordination, and the sacrifice of the wholeness offering, 
which God commanded Moses on Mount Sinai on the day he commanded the Israelites to bring their offerings to God, in the Sinai wilderness.
