It was two days before the Passover and the festival of bread without yeast, and the chief priests and the Bible teachers were looking for a way to seize him by deception and kill him. 
They said, "Not during the feast, or there might be a riot among the people." 
While he was at Bethany, in the house of Simon the leper, as he sat at the table, a woman came with an alabaster jar of very costly pure nard perfume. She broke the jar and poured it over his head. 
But some were indignant among themselves, saying, "Why has this perfume been wasted? 
This could have been sold for more than three hundred silver coins and given to the poor." And they grumbled at her. 
But Jesus said, "Leave her alone. Why are you bothering her? She has done a good deed for me. 
You always have the poor with you, and whenever you want to, you can do them good, but you won't always have me. 
She has done what she could. She has anointed my body beforehand for burial. 
Truly I tell you, wherever this Good News is preached throughout the whole world, what this woman has done will also be spoken of in memory of her." 
Judas Iscariot, who was one of the twelve, went to the chief priests to hand him over to them. 
When they heard it, they were glad and promised to give him money. And he looked for a way to hand him over at the right time. 
On the first day of the festival of bread without yeast, when they sacrificed the Passover lamb, his students asked him, "Where do you want us to go and prepare for you to eat the Passover?" 
He sent two of his students and said to them, "Go into the city, and a man carrying a pitcher of water will meet you. Follow him, 
and wherever he goes in, tell the master of the house, 'The Teacher says, "Where is the guest room, where I may eat the Passover with my students?"' 
He'll show you a large upper room, furnished and ready. Get it ready for us there." 
His students went out, came into the city, and found everything as he had said to them, and they prepared the Passover. 
When it was evening he came with the twelve. 
As they sat and were eating, Jesus said, "Truly I tell you, one of you'll hand me over, one who's eating with me." 
They began to be grieved and to ask him one by one, "Surely not I?" 
He answered them, "It's one of the twelve, he who dips with me in the dish. 
The Human One goes as it's written about him, but how terrible for that man who hands over the Human One! It would be better for that man if he hadn't been born." 
As they were eating, Jesus took bread, and when he had blessed it, he broke it and gave it to them, and said, "Take it. This is my body." 
He took the cup, and when he had given thanks, he gave it to them, and they all drank from it. 
He said to them, "This is my blood of the new binding arrangement, which is poured out for everyone. 
Truly I tell you, I'll never again drink from the fruit of the vine until that day when I drink it new in God's reign." 
When they had sung a hymn, they went out to the Mount of Olives. 
Jesus said to them, "All of you'll be trapped because of me tonight, for it's written, 'I'll strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered.' 
However, after I'm raised up, I'll go before you into Galilee." 
But Peter said to him, "Even if all are trapped, I won't be." 
Jesus said to him, "Truly I tell you, today, even this night, before the rooster crows twice, you'll disown me three times." 
But he spoke all the more, "Even if I must die with you, I won't disown you." And they all said the same thing. 
They came to a place named Gethsemane. He said to his students, "Sit here while I pray." 
He took Peter, James, and John with him, and began to be greatly troubled and distressed. 
He said to them, "My life is crushed with grief, even to death. Stay here and stay awake." 
He went a little further, fell on the ground, and prayed that, if it were possible, the hour would pass from him. 
He said, "Abba, Father, all things are possible for you. Take this cup away from me. Yet not what I want, but what you want." 
He came and found them sleeping, and said to Peter, "Simon, are you sleeping? Couldn't you stay awake one hour? 
Stay awake and pray, so that you may not come into testing. The spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak." 
Again he went away and prayed, saying the same words. 
Again he returned and found them sleeping, for their eyes were very heavy; and they didn't know what to answer him. 
He came the third time and said to them, "Sleep on now and rest. That's enough. The time has come. Look, the Human One is being handed over into the hands of those who have drifted. 
Get up! Let's go. Look, the one who hands me over is near." 
Immediately, while he was still speaking, Judas, one of the twelve, came, and with him a crowd with swords and clubs, from the chief priests, the Bible teachers, and the elders. 
Now the one handing him over had given them a sign, saying, "Whomever I kiss, that's he. Seize him and lead him away safely." 
When he had come, he went straight to him and said, "Rabbi!" and kissed him. 
They laid their hands on him and seized him. 
But one of those standing by drew his sword and struck the high priest's slave, and cut off his ear. 
Jesus answered them, "Have you come out with swords and clubs to seize me, as if I were a robber? 
I was with you every day in the temple courts teaching, and you didn't arrest me. But this is so that the Scriptures would be fulfilled." 
They all left him, and fled. 
A young man followed him, with only a linen cloth wrapped around his naked body, and they grabbed him, 
but he left the linen cloth and fled from them naked. 
They led Jesus away to the high priest, and all the chief priests, the elders, and the Bible teachers came together with him. 
Peter had followed him at a distance, right into the courtyard of the high priest. He was sitting with the officers, warming himself in the light of the fire. 
Now the chief priests and the whole council were looking for witnesses against Jesus to put him to death, and they found none. 
Many gave false witness against him, but their witness didn't agree. 
Some stood up and gave false witness against him, saying, 
"We heard him say, 'I'll tear down this temple made with hands, and in three days I'll build another made without hands.'" 
Even so, their witness didn't agree. 
The high priest stood up in the middle and asked Jesus, "Have you no answer? What's this they're testifying against you?" 
But he stayed quiet and answered nothing. Again the high priest asked him, "Are you the Christ, the Son of the One who's praised?" 
Jesus said, "I'm. And you'll see the Human One sitting at the right hand of Power and coming with the clouds of the sky." 
The high priest tore his clothes and said, "Why do we need any more witnesses? 
You have heard the slander! What do you think?" And they all sentenced him as deserving death. 
Some began to spit on him, to cover his face, and to beat him with their fists, saying to him, "Speak for God!" And the officers struck him with the palms of their hands. 
As Peter was in the courtyard below, one of the servant girls of the high priest came, 
and seeing Peter warming himself, she looked at him and said, "You were also with Jesus of Nazareth!" 
But he denied it, saying, "I don't know or understand what you're saying." And he went out to the gateway, and the rooster crowed. 
The servant girl saw him and began again to say to those standing by, "This is one of them." 
But he again denied it. After a little while, those standing by said to Peter again, "You truly are one of them, for you're a Galilean, and your speech shows it." 
But he began to call down curses and to swear, "I don't know this man you're talking about!" 
The rooster crowed the second time. Peter remembered the words Jesus had said to him: "Before the rooster crows twice, you'll disown me three times." And when he thought about it, he cried.
