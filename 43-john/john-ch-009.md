As he passed by, he saw a man blind from birth. 
His students asked him, "Rabbi, who drifted, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?" 
Jesus answered, "Neither this man nor his parents drifted, but this happened so that the deeds of God would show in him. 
I must do the deeds of him who sent me while it's day. The night is coming, when no one can work. 
While I'm in the world, I am the light of the world." 
When he had said this, he spat on the ground, made mud with the saliva, put the mud on the blind man's eyes, 
and said to him, "Go, wash in the pool of Siloam" (which means "Sent"). So he went away, washed, and came back seeing. 
So the neighbors and those who saw that he was blind before said, "Isn't this he who sat and begged?" 
Others were saying, "It's he." Still others were saying, "He looks like him." He said, "I'm he." 
They were asking him, "How were your eyes opened?" 
He answered, "A man called Jesus made mud, put it on my eyes, and said to me, 'Go to the pool of Siloam and wash.' So I went away and washed, and I received sight." 
Then they asked him, "Where is he?" He said, "I don't know." 
They brought him who had been blind to the strict religious ones. 
It was a Sabbath when Jesus made the mud and opened his eyes. 
Again the strict religious ones also asked him how he received his sight. He said to them, "He put mud on my eyes, I washed, and I see." 
Some of the strict religious ones said, "This man isn't from God, because he doesn't keep the Sabbath." Others said, "How can a man who has drifted do such signs?" So they were divided. 
So they asked the blind man again, "What do you say about him, because he opened your eyes?" He said, "He's a spokesman." 
The Jewish leaders didn't trust concerning him, that he had been blind and had received his sight, until they called the parents of him who had received his sight, 
and asked them, "Is this your son, whom you say was born blind? How then does he now see?" 
His parents answered them, "We know that this is our son, and that he was born blind; 
but how he now sees, we don't know; or who opened his eyes, we don't know. He's of age. Ask him. He'll speak for himself." 
His parents said these things because they feared the Jewish leaders; for the Jewish leaders had already agreed that if any man would confess him as Christ, he'd be put out of the gathering place. 
So his parents said, "He's of age. Ask him." 
So they called the man who was blind a second time, and said to him, "Give honor to God. We know that this man has drifted." 
He answered, "I don't know if he has drifted. One thing I do know: that though I was blind, now I see." 
They said to him again, "What did he do to you? How did he open your eyes?" 
He answered them, "I told you already, and you didn't listen. Why do you want to hear it again? You don't also want to become his students, do you?" 
They insulted him and said, "You're his student, but we're students of Moses. 
We know that God has spoken to Moses. But as for this man, we don't know where he comes from." 
The man answered them, "How amazing! You don't know where he comes from, yet he opened my eyes. 
We know that God doesn't listen to people who have drifted, but if anyone worships God and does his will, he listens to them. 
Since the world began it has never been heard of that anyone opened the eyes of someone born blind. 
If this man weren't from God, he could do nothing." 
They answered him, "You were altogether born in your mistakes, and do you teach us?" Then they threw him out. 
Jesus heard that they had thrown him out, and finding him, he said, "Do you trust in the Son of God?" 
He answered, "Who's he, Lord, that I can trust in him?" 
Jesus said to him, "You have both seen him, and it's he who speaks with you." 
He said, "Lord, I trust!" and he worshiped him. 
Jesus said, "I came into this world for a verdict, that those who don't see may see; and that those who see may become blind." 
Those of the strict religious ones who were with him heard these things, and said to him, "Are we also blind?" 
Jesus said to them, "If you were blind, you'd have no mistakes; but now you say, 'We see.' Therefore your mistakes remain.
