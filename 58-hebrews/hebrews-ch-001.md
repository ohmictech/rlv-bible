God, who long ago spoke to the fathers through the spokesmen at many times and in many ways, 
has at the end of these days spoken to us by his Son, whom he appointed heir of everything, and through whom he also made the worlds. 
He's the shining of God's weight, the exact image of what God is, and he holds everything up by the word of his power. When he had made us clean from our mistakes by himself, he sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high, 
He became as far above the messengers as the name he has been given is above theirs. 
To which of the messengers did he ever say, "You're my Son. Today I have become your father"? And again, "I'll be a Father to him, and he'll be a Son to me"? 
And when he brings his firstborn into the world again, he says, "Let all God's messengers bow down to him." 
Of the messengers he says, "He makes his messengers winds, and his servants a flame of fire." 
But of the Son he says, "Your throne, O God, stands through all the eras. The scepter of uprightness is the scepter of your reign. 
You have loved rightness and hated living as if there were no law; that's why God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness above your companions." 
And, "You, Lord, laid the foundation of the earth in the beginning. The skies are the work of your hands. 
They'll wear out, but you go on. They'll all grow old like a piece of clothing. 
You'll roll them up like a coat, and they'll be changed; but you're the same. Your years will never run out." 
But to which of the messengers did he ever say, "Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies a footstool for your feet"? 
Are they not all spirits who serve, sent out to serve for the sake of those who are going to be given the charge of rescue?
