Now after these things, the Lord also appointed seventy others, and sent them two by two ahead of him into every city and place where he was about to come. 
Then he said to them, "The harvest is indeed plentiful, but the workers are few. So pray to the Lord of the harvest, that he may send out workers into his harvest. 
Go your ways. Look, I send you out as lambs among wolves. 
Carry no purse, nor wallet, nor sandals. Greet no one on the way. 
Into whatever house you enter, first say, 'Wholeness be to this house.' 
If a person of wholeness is there, your wholeness will rest on him; but if not, it'll return to you. 
Stay in that same house, eating and drinking the things they give, for the laborer is worthy of his wages. Don't go from house to house. 
Into whatever city you enter and they receive you, eat the things that are set before you. 
Heal the sick who are there and tell them, 'God's reign has come near to you.' 
But into whatever city you enter and they don't receive you, go out into its streets and say, 
'Even the dust from your city that clings to us, we wipe off against you. Nevertheless know this, that God's reign has come near to you.' 
I tell you, it'll be more bearable in that day for Sodom than for that city. 
"How terrible for you, Chorazin! How terrible for you, Bethsaida! If the powerful works had been done in Tyre and Sidon which were done in you, they'd have had a change of heart long ago, sitting in rough cloth and ashes. 
But it'll be more bearable for Tyre and Sidon at the verdict than for you. 
You, Capernaum, who are lifted up to the sky, will be brought down to Hades. 
Whoever listens to you listens to me, and whoever rejects you rejects me. Whoever rejects me rejects him who sent me." 
The seventy returned with joy, saying, "Lord, even the unclean spirits are subject to us in your name!" 
He said to them, "I saw the Adversary fall like lightning from the sky. 
Look, I give you authority to tread on snakes and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy. Nothing will in any way harm you. 
Nevertheless, don't celebrate in this, that the spirits are subject to you, but celebrate that your names are written in the sky." 
At that same time, Jesus celebrated in the Holy Spirit, and said, "I thank you, O Father, Lord of the sky and the land, that you have hidden these things from the wise and understanding, and revealed them to little children. Yes, Father, for so it was well-pleasing in your sight." 
Turning to the students, he said, "All things have been handed over to me by my Father. No one knows who the Son is, except the Father, and who the Father is, except the Son, and the one to whom the Son chooses to unveil him." 
Then he turned to his students privately and said, "Flourishing are the eyes which see the things that you see, 
for I tell you that many spokesmen and kings wanted to see what you see and didn't see it, and to hear what you hear and didn't hear it." 
Look, an expert in the law stood up and tested him, saying, "Teacher, what must I do to be given charge of the real life of the era?" 
He said to him, "What's written in the law? How do you read it?" 
He answered, "You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your life, with all your strength, and with all your mind; and your neighbor as yourself." 
He said to him, "You have answered correctly. Do this, and you'll live." 
But he, wanting to justify himself, asked Jesus, "Who's my neighbor?" 
Jesus answered, "A certain man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho, and he fell among robbers, who both stripped him and beat him, and departed, leaving him half dead. 
By chance a certain priest was going down that way. When he saw him, he passed by on the other side. 
In the same way a Levite also, when he came to the place and saw him, passed by on the other side. 
But a certain Samaritan, as he traveled, came where he was. When he saw him, he was moved with deep compassion, 
came to him, and bound up his wounds, pouring on oil and wine. He set him on his own animal, brought him to an inn, and took care of him. 
On the next day, when he departed, he took out two silver coins, gave them to the host, and said to him, 'Take care of him. Whatever you spend beyond that, I'll repay you when I return.' 
Now which of these three do you think seemed to be a neighbor to him who fell among the robbers?" 
He said, "He who showed mercy on him." Then Jesus said to him, "Go and do the same." 
As they went on their way, he entered into a certain village, and a certain woman named Martha received him into her house. 
She had a sister called Mary, who also sat at Jesus' feet and heard his word. 
But Martha was distracted with all the serving, and she came up to him and said, "Lord, don't you care that my sister left me to serve alone? Ask her to help me." 
Jesus answered her, "Martha, Martha, you're anxious and troubled about many things, 
but one thing is needed. Mary has chosen the good part, which won't be taken away from her."
