When the people saw that Moses was taking so long to come down from the mountain, they gathered around Aaron and said, "Come on, make us gods who will go ahead of us. As for this Moses, the man who brought us up out of Egypt, we don't know what has happened to him." 
Aaron said to them, "Take the gold earrings off your wives, your sons and your daughters, and bring them to me." 
So everyone took off their gold earrings and brought them to Aaron. 
He took the gold from them, shaped it with a tool and made it into a calf out of cast metal. Then they said, "These are your gods, Israel, that brought you up out of Egypt." 
When Aaron saw this, he built an altar in front of it and announced, "Tomorrow will be a festival to God." 
They got up early the next day and offered burnt offerings and brought offerings for wholeness, and the people sat down to eat and drink and then got up to play. 
God spoke to Moses: "Go down, because your people, the ones you brought up out of Egypt, have corrupted themselves. 
They have turned away so quickly from the way I commanded them. They have made themselves a calf out of cast metal, and they have worshiped it and sacrificed to it and said, 'These are your gods, Israel, that brought you up out of Egypt.'" 
God said to Moses, "I have seen these people, and they are stubborn. 
Now leave me alone, so my anger can burn against them and I can consume them, and I will make a great nation out of you." 
But Moses pleaded with God and said, "God, why does your anger burn against your people, the ones you brought out of Egypt with great power and a strong hand? 
Why should the Egyptians say, 'He brought them out to do them harm, to kill them in the mountains and wipe them off the face of the earth'? Turn from your fierce anger and turn away from this disaster for your people. 
Remember Abraham, Isaac and Israel, your servants, to whom you swore by yourself and said, 'I will multiply your descendants like the stars of the sky, and I will give all this land I spoke of to your descendants, and they will inherit it for the eras.'" 
So God turned away from the disaster he had said he would bring on his people. 
Moses turned and went down the mountain with the two tablets of the covenant in his hand, tablets written on both sides, front and back. 
The tablets were God's work, and the writing was God's writing, engraved on the tablets. 
When Joshua heard the people shouting, he said to Moses, "There's the sound of war in the camp." 
Moses said, "It isn't the sound of victory, and it isn't the sound of defeat. What I hear is singing." 
As soon as he came near the camp he saw the calf and the dancing, and Moses' anger burned, and he threw the tablets down and smashed them at the foot of the mountain. 
He took the calf they had made, burned it, ground it to powder, scattered the powder on the water and made the Israelites drink it. 
Moses said to Aaron, "What did these people do to you, that you brought such a great wrong on them?" 
Aaron said, "Don't be angry, my lord. You know these people. They're set on evil. 
They said to me, 'Make us gods who will go ahead of us. As for this Moses, the man who brought us up out of Egypt, we don't know what has happened to him.' 
I said to them, 'Whoever has gold, take it off.' So they gave it to me, and I threw it into the fire, and out came this calf." 
When Moses saw that the people were out of control, because Aaron had let them get out of control and become a mockery to their enemies, 
Moses stood at the gate of the camp and said, "Whoever is on God's side, come here!" And all the sons of Levi gathered around him. 
He said to them, "This is what the God of Israel says: 'Each man put his sword on his hip, and go back and forth through the camp from gate to gate, and each man kill his brother, his friend and his neighbor.'" 
The sons of Levi did as Moses said, and about three thousand men died that day. 
Moses said, "Set yourselves apart to God today, because each of you has been against his son and his brother, and today he has given you a blessing." 
The next day Moses said to the people, "You have done a great wrong. Now I will go up to God. Perhaps I can make things right for your wrongdoing." 
Moses went back to God and said, "Please, these people have done a great wrong and made themselves gods of gold. 
Now, if you will forgive their wrongdoing, then forgive it. If not, please blot me out of the book you have written." 
God said to Moses, "Whoever has wronged me, I will blot out of my book. 
Now go and lead the people to the place I told you about. My messenger will go ahead of you. But when the day of punishment comes, I will punish them for their wrongdoing." 
Then God struck the people for what they did with the calf Aaron had made.
