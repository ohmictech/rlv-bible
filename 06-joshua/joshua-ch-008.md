God said to Joshua, "Don't be afraid and don't lose heart. Take all the fighting men with you, and get up and go to Ai. Look, I have handed the king of Ai over to you, with his people, his city and his land. 
Do to Ai and its king what you did to Jericho and its king, except that this time you may take its goods and its livestock for yourselves. Set an ambush behind the city." 
So Joshua set out with all the fighting men to go up to Ai. He chose thirty thousand of the bravest men and sent them out at night. 
He told them, "Look, you are to hide in ambush behind the city, not very far away, and all of you be ready. 
I and everyone with me will go up to the city. When they come out against us, as they did the first time, we will run from them. 
They will chase us until we have pulled them away from the city, because they will say, 'They're running from us, just like the first time.' So we will run from them, 
and you will get up from the ambush and take the city, because your God will hand it over to you. 
When you have taken the city, set it on fire. Do this as God has said. Look, I have given you your orders." 
Joshua sent them out, and they went and set the ambush and stayed between Bethel and Ai, west of Ai. But Joshua spent that night with the people. 
Joshua got up early in the morning, gathered the people, and went up with the elders of Israel ahead of the people to Ai. 
All the fighting men who were with him went up and came near and stood in front of the city and camped on the north side of Ai, with a valley between them and the city. 
He took about five thousand men and set them in ambush between Bethel and Ai, on the west side of the city. 
So the people were placed, the whole army north of the city and the ambush west of it, and Joshua went down into the valley that night. 
When the king of Ai saw them, he and all his people hurried and got up early and went out against Israel to fight at the appointed place in front of the Arabah, but he didn't know there was an ambush behind the city. 
Joshua and all Israel pretended to be beaten by them and ran away toward the wilderness. 
Then everyone in the city was called out to chase them, and they chased Joshua and were pulled away from the city. 
Not a man was left in Ai or Bethel who didn't go out after Israel. They left the city open and chased Israel. 
God said to Joshua, "Point the spear in your hand toward Ai, because I will hand it over to you." So Joshua pointed the spear in his hand toward the city. 
The men in ambush got up quickly from their place, and as soon as he pointed his hand they ran into the city and took it, and quickly set it on fire. 
When the men of Ai looked back, they saw the smoke of the city rising up into the sky, and they had no strength to run one way or the other. Then the people who had run toward the wilderness turned back on the men chasing them. 
When Joshua and all Israel saw that the ambush had taken the city and that smoke was rising from it, they turned back and struck down the men of Ai. 
The others came out of the city against them, so the men of Ai were caught in the middle, with Israelites on both sides. They struck them down until none were left alive or got away. 
They captured the king of Ai alive and brought him to Joshua. 
When Israel had finished killing everyone in Ai out in the open country, in the wilderness where they had chased them, and they had all fallen by the sword until they were gone, all Israel came back to Ai and struck it down with the sword. 
Twelve thousand people fell that day, men and women, everyone in Ai. 
Joshua didn't pull back the hand that held the spear until he had completely destroyed everyone living in Ai. 
Israel took only the livestock and the goods of that city for themselves, as God had told Joshua. 
So Joshua burned Ai and made it a pile of ruins for the eras, a wasteland, to this day. 
He hung the king of Ai on a tree until evening. At sunset Joshua gave the order, and they took his body down from the tree and threw it at the entrance of the city gate, and piled a great heap of stones over it that is still there today. 
Then Joshua built an altar to the God of Israel on Mount Ebal, 
as Moses the servant of God had commanded the Israelites, as it is written in the scroll of the law of Moses: an altar of uncut stones that no one had used an iron tool on. They offered burnt offerings on it to God and sacrificed wholeness offerings. 
Joshua wrote on the stones a copy of the law of Moses in front of the Israelites. 
All Israel, with their elders, officials and judges, stood on both sides of the ark facing the Levite priests who carried the ark of God's covenant, the foreigner and the native alike, half of them in front of Mount Gerizim and half in front of Mount Ebal, as Moses the servant of God had first commanded, so that they would bless the people of Israel. 
After that Joshua read out all the words of the law, the blessing and the curse, everything written in the scroll of the law. 
There wasn't a word of all Moses had commanded that Joshua didn't read out in front of the whole assembly of Israel, including the women, the children and the foreigners living among them.
