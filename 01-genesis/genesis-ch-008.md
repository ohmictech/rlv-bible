God remembered Noah, and all the animals and livestock that were with him in the ship. God made a wind blow over the earth, and the waters subsided. 
The springs of the deep and the windows of the sky were closed, and the rain from the sky was held back. 
The waters kept going down from the earth. After one hundred fifty days the waters had gone down. 
The ship came to rest in the seventh month, on the seventeenth day of the month, on the mountains of Ararat. 
The waters kept going down until the tenth month. In the tenth month, on the first day, the tops of the mountains came into view. 
At the end of forty days, Noah opened the window of the ship which he had made, 
and he sent out a raven. It flew back and forth until the waters had dried up from the earth. 
Then he sent out a dove to see if the waters had gone down from the face of the land, 
but the dove found nowhere to set her foot, and she came back to him in the ship, because the waters were still over the whole earth. He reached out his hand, took her, and brought her into the ship with him. 
He waited another seven days, and again he sent the dove out of the ship. 
The dove came back to him in the evening, and there in her beak was a freshly picked olive leaf. So Noah knew that the waters had gone down from the earth. 
He waited another seven days and sent out the dove, and she didn't come back to him again. 
In the six hundred first year, in the first month, the first day of the month, the waters were dried up from the earth. Noah removed the covering of the ship and looked, and the face of the ground was dry. 
In the second month, on the twenty-seventh day of the month, the earth was dry. 
God spoke to Noah, saying, 
"Go out of the ship, you, your wife, your sons, and your sons' wives with you. 
Bring out with you every living thing that's with you of all flesh, including birds, livestock, and every small crawling thing that crawls on the earth, so they can swarm on the earth, be fruitful, and increase on the earth." 
Noah went out, with his sons, his wife, and his sons' wives with him. 
Every animal, every small crawling thing, and every bird, whatever moves on the earth, went out of the ship by their families. 
Noah built an altar to God, and took of every clean animal, and of every clean bird, and offered burnt offerings on the altar. 
God smelled the pleasant smell. God said to himself, "I'll never again curse the ground because of human beings, because the thoughts of the human heart are bad from youth. And I'll never again strike down every living thing, as I have done. 
As long as the earth lasts, planting time and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night will never stop."
