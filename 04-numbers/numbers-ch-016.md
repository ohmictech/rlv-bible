Korah son of Izhar, son of Kohath, son of Levi, along with Dathan and Abiram the sons of Eliab, and On son of Peleth, the sons of Reuben, took some men 
and rose up against Moses, along with two hundred fifty Israelite leaders, men called to the assembly and well known. 
They gathered against Moses and Aaron and said, "You've gone far enough! The whole community is set apart, every one of them, and God is among them. Why do you set yourselves above God's assembly?" 
When Moses heard it, he fell face down. 
He said to Korah and all his followers, "In the morning God will show who belongs to him and who is set apart, and he will bring that man near to him. The one he chooses he will bring near. 
Do this: take fire pans, you and all your followers, 
put fire in them and incense on them before God tomorrow. The man God chooses is the one who is set apart. You've gone far enough, you sons of Levi!" 
Moses said to Korah, "Listen, you sons of Levi! 
Is it a small thing to you that the God of Israel has set you apart from the rest of the community to bring you near to himself, to do the work of God's tent and to stand in front of the community and serve them, 
and that he has brought you near, you and all your brothers the sons of Levi? Are you after the priesthood too? 
So you and all your followers have gathered against God. Who is Aaron, that you grumble against him?" 
Moses sent for Dathan and Abiram, the sons of Eliab, and they said, "We won't come. 
Isn't it enough that you brought us out of a land flowing with milk and honey to kill us in the wilderness? Do you have to make yourself a prince over us as well? 
You haven't brought us into a land flowing with milk and honey or given us fields and vineyards. Will you gouge out the eyes of these men? We won't come." 
Moses was very angry and said to God, "Don't accept their offering. I haven't taken a single donkey from them, and I haven't harmed one of them." 
Moses said to Korah, "You and all your followers, present yourselves before God tomorrow, you, they and Aaron. 
Each man is to take his fire pan, put incense in it and bring it before God, two hundred fifty fire pans, and you and Aaron with yours." 
Each man took his fire pan, put fire in it and laid incense on it, and stood at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting with Moses and Aaron. 
Korah gathered the whole community against them at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting, and God's splendor appeared to the whole community. 
God spoke to Moses and Aaron: 
"Get away from this community so I can put an end to them in a moment." 
They fell face down and said, "God, the God of the spirits of every living person, when one man drifts, will you be angry with the whole community?" 
God spoke to Moses: 
"Speak to the community and say, 'Move away from the tents of Korah, Dathan and Abiram.'" 
Moses got up and went to Dathan and Abiram, and the elders of Israel followed him. 
He spoke to the community, saying, "Please move away from the tents of these guilty men and don't touch anything that belongs to them, or you will be swept away with all their wrongs." 
So they moved away from the tents of Korah, Dathan and Abiram on every side. Dathan and Abiram came out and stood at the entrance of their tents with their wives, their sons and their little ones. 
Moses said, "This is how you will know that God sent me to do all these things, and that they didn't come from my own mind. 
If these men die the way everyone dies, or if what happens to everyone happens to them, then God hasn't sent me. 
But if God does something new, and the ground opens its mouth and swallows them with everything that belongs to them, and they go down alive into Hades, then you will know that these men have treated God with contempt." 
As he finished saying all this, the ground under them split open. 
The earth opened its mouth and swallowed them with their households, all of Korah's men and everything they had. 
So they and everything that belonged to them went down alive into Hades, and the earth closed over them, and they were gone from the assembly. 
All the Israelites around them fled at their screams, saying, "The earth might swallow us too!" 
Fire came out from God and burned up the two hundred fifty men who had offered the incense. 
God spoke to Moses: 
"Tell Eleazar son of Aaron the priest to pick up the fire pans out of the fire and scatter the burning coals away from the camp, because they are set apart, 
the fire pans of men who wronged their own lives. Have them hammered into thin sheets to cover the altar, because they were offered before God and are set apart. They will be a sign to the Israelites." 
Eleazar the priest picked up the bronze fire pans that the men who were burned had offered, and they hammered them out to cover the altar, 
to be a reminder to the Israelites that no outsider who isn't a descendant of Aaron should come near to burn incense before God, so he doesn't end up like Korah and his followers, just as God said to him through Moses. 
But the next day the whole community of Israel grumbled against Moses and Aaron, saying, "You have killed God's people!" 
When the community gathered against Moses and Aaron, they looked toward the Tent of Meeting, and the cloud covered it and God's splendor appeared. 
Moses and Aaron came to the front of the Tent of Meeting. 
God spoke to Moses: 
"Get away from this community so I can put an end to them in a moment." They fell face down. 
Moses said to Aaron, "Take your fire pan and put fire from the altar in it, lay incense on it, and carry it quickly to the community and make things right for them, because anger has gone out from God and the plague has begun." 
Aaron did as Moses said and ran into the middle of the assembly. The plague had already begun among the people, and he put on the incense and made things right for the people. 
He stood between the dead and the living, and the plague stopped. 
Those who died from the plague were fourteen thousand seven hundred, besides those who died over the matter of Korah. 
Aaron went back to Moses at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting, and the plague was over.
