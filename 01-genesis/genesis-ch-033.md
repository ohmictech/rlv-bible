Jacob looked up and saw Esau coming, with four hundred men. So he divided the children among Leah, Rachel, and the two servant women. 
He put the servant women and their children in front, then Leah and her children, and Rachel and Joseph last. 
He went on ahead of them, bowing to the ground seven times as he came closer to his brother. 
But Esau ran to meet him, threw his arms around him, hugged him, and kissed him, and they both cried. 
Esau looked up and saw the women and children. He asked, "Who are all these with you?" Jacob said, "These are the children God has kindly given your servant." 
Then the servant women and their children came forward and bowed down. 
Leah and her children also came forward and bowed, and last of all Joseph and Rachel came forward and bowed. 
Esau asked, "What did you mean by all those herds I met on the way?" Jacob said, "To win your favor, my lord." 
Esau said, "I have plenty, brother. Keep what's yours." 
Jacob said, "No, please. If I've found favor with you, accept this gift from me, because seeing your face is like seeing the face of God, now that you've welcomed me. 
Please accept the gift I've brought you, because God has been good to me and I have everything I need." And Jacob kept urging him until he took it. 
Then Esau said, "Let's get moving. I'll go ahead of you." 
Jacob said to him, "My lord knows the children are young, and the flocks and herds with me have their little ones. If they're driven too hard for even one day, the whole flock will die. 
Please go on ahead of your servant. I'll move along slowly, at the pace of the animals and the children, until I come to you in Seir." 
Esau said, "Then let me leave some of my men with you." Jacob said, "Why bother? I've already found favor with you, my lord." 
So that day Esau turned back toward Seir. 
But Jacob went to Succoth, where he built himself a house and made shelters for his livestock. That's why the place is called Succoth. 
Jacob arrived safely at the city of Shechem in the land of Canaan, coming from Paddan Aram, and camped in front of the city. 
He bought the piece of land where he had pitched his tent from the sons of Hamor, Shechem's father, for a hundred pieces of silver. 
There he set up an altar and named it "God, the God of Israel."
