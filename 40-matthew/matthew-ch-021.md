When they came near to Jerusalem and came to Bethsphage, to the Mount of Olives, then Jesus sent two students, 
saying to them, "Go into the village that is opposite you, and immediately you will find a donkey tied, and a colt with her. Untie them and bring them to me. 
If anyone says anything to you, you will say, 'The Lord needs them,' and immediately he will send them." 
This happened so that what was spoken through the spokesman would come true: 
"Tell the daughter of Zion, look, your King comes to you, humble, and riding on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey." 
The students went and did just as Jesus commanded them, 
and brought the donkey and the colt and laid their clothes on them, and he sat on them. 
A very great crowd spread their clothes on the road. Others cut branches from the trees and spread them on the road. 
The crowds who went in front of him and those who followed kept shouting, "Hosanna to the Son of David! Flourishing is he who comes in the name of the Lord! Hosanna in the highest!" 
When he had come into Jerusalem, all the city was stirred up, saying, "Who is this?" 
The crowds said, "This is the spokesman, Jesus, from Nazareth of Galilee." 
Jesus entered the temple courts and drove out everyone who was buying and selling there. He overturned the tables of the money changers and the seats of those selling doves. 
He said to them, "It is written, 'My house shall be called a house of prayer,' but you have made it a hideout for robbers!" 
The lame and the blind came to him in the temple courts, and he healed them. 
But when the high priests and the Bible teachers saw the amazing things that he did, and the children who were crying out in the temple courts and saying, "Hosanna to the Son of David!" they were indignant, 
and said to him, "Do you hear what these are saying?" Jesus said to them, "Yes. Did you never read, 'From the mouths of children and nursing babies you have completed praise'?" 
He left them and went out of the city to Bethany, and camped there. 
Now in the morning, as he returned to the city, he was hungry. 
Seeing a fig tree by the road, he came to it and found nothing on it but leaves. He said to it, "Let no fruit ever come from you again!" Immediately the fig tree withered away. 
When the students saw it, they were amazed, saying, "How did the fig tree immediately wither away?" 
Jesus answered them, "Truly I tell you, if you have trust and don't doubt, you will not only do what was done to the fig tree, but even if you told this mountain, 'Be taken up and thrown into the sea,' it would be done. 
All things, whatever you ask in prayer, trusting, you will receive." 
When he had come into the temple courts, the high priests and the elders of the people came to him as he was teaching, and said, "By what authority do you do these things? Who gave you this authority?" 
Jesus answered them, "I will also ask you one question. If you answer me, I will also tell you by what authority I do these things. 
The immersion of John, where was it from? From the sky or from people?" They reasoned with themselves, saying, "If we say, 'From the sky,' he will ask us, 'Why then did you not trust him?' 
But if we say, 'From people,' we fear the crowd, for all hold John as a spokesman." 
They answered Jesus, and said, "We don't know." He also said to them, "Neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things. 
But what do you think? A man had two sons, and he came to the first, and said, 'Son, go work today in my vineyard.' 
He answered, 'I will not,' but afterward he felt sorry and went. 
He came to the second, and said the same thing. He answered, 'I'm going, sir,' but he didn't go. 
Which of the two did the will of his father?" They said to him, "The first." Jesus said to them, "Truly I tell you that the tax collectors and those who sleep around are entering into God's reign before you. 
For John came to you in the way of rightness, and you didn't trust him, but the tax collectors and those who sleep around trusted him. When you saw it, you didn't even have a change of heart afterward, so that you might trust him. 
"Hear another story. There was a man, the master of a house, who planted a vineyard. He put a hedge around it, dug a winepress in it, built a tower, leased it out to farmers, and went away to another country. 
When the season for the fruit came near, he sent his slaves to the farmers to receive his fruit. 
The farmers took his slaves, beat one, killed another, and stoned another. 
Again, he sent other slaves, more than the first, and they treated them the same way. 
But afterward he sent to them his son, saying, 'They will respect my son.' 
But the farmers, when they saw the son, said among themselves, 'This is the heir. Come, let's kill him and seize his inheritance.' 
So they took him and threw him out of the vineyard, then killed him. 
When therefore the lord of the vineyard comes, what will he do to those farmers?" 
They told him, "He will badly ruin those bad men, and will lease out the vineyard to other farmers who will give him the fruit in its season." 
Jesus said to them, "Did you never read in the Scriptures, 'The stone which the builders rejected was made the cornerstone. This was from the Lord. It is amazing in our eyes'? 
"That is why I tell you, God's reign will be taken away from you and will be given to a nation producing its fruit. 
He who falls on this stone will be shattered, but on whomever it falls, it will crush him to dust." 
When the high priests and the Pharisees heard his stories, they perceived that he spoke about them. 
When they sought to seize him, they feared the crowds, because they considered him to be a spokesman.
