God spoke to Moses and Aaron: 
"When a person has a swelling on his skin, or a scab, or a bright spot, and it becomes a serious skin disease, he is to be brought to Aaron the priest or to one of his sons, the priests. 
The priest is to examine the sore on his skin. If the hair in the sore has turned white and the sore looks deeper than the skin, it is a serious skin disease, and the priest is to declare him unclean. 
But if the bright spot on his skin is white and doesn't look deeper than the skin, and the hair in it hasn't turned white, the priest is to isolate the person for seven days. 
On the seventh day the priest is to examine him, and if the sore looks the same and hasn't spread on the skin, the priest is to isolate him for another seven days. 
On the seventh day the priest is to examine him again, and if the sore has faded and hasn't spread on the skin, the priest is to declare him clean. It was only a scab, and he is to wash his clothes and be clean. 
But if the scab spreads on the skin after he has shown himself to the priest and been declared clean, he is to show himself to the priest again. 
The priest is to examine him, and if the scab has spread on the skin, the priest is to declare him unclean. It is a serious skin disease. 
"When a person has a serious skin disease, he is to be brought to the priest. 
The priest is to examine him, and if there is a white swelling on the skin and it has turned the hair white and there is raw flesh in the swelling, 
it is a chronic skin disease on his skin, and the priest is to declare him unclean. He doesn't need to isolate him, because he is already unclean. 
"If the disease breaks out all over the skin and covers all the skin of the person from head to foot, as far as the priest can see, 
the priest is to examine him, and if the disease has covered all his flesh, he is to declare him clean. It has all turned white, so he is clean. 
But whenever raw flesh appears on him, he is unclean. 
When the priest sees the raw flesh, he is to declare him unclean, because raw flesh is unclean. It is a serious skin disease. 
But if the raw flesh changes back and turns white, he is to come to the priest, 
and the priest is to examine him. If the sore has turned white, the priest is to declare him clean, and he is clean. 
"When someone has a boil on his skin and it heals, 
and where the boil was there is a white swelling or a reddish white bright spot, he is to show it to the priest. 
The priest is to examine it, and if it looks deeper than the skin and the hair in it has turned white, the priest is to declare him unclean. It is a serious skin disease that has broken out in the boil. 
But if the priest examines it and there is no white hair in it and it doesn't look deeper than the skin and is faded, the priest is to isolate him for seven days. 
If it spreads on the skin, the priest is to declare him unclean. It is a sore. 
But if the bright spot stays where it is and doesn't spread, it is the scar from the boil, and the priest is to declare him clean. 
"When someone has a burn on his skin from fire and the raw flesh of the burn becomes a bright spot, reddish white or white, 
the priest is to examine it. If the hair in the bright spot has turned white and it looks deeper than the skin, it is a serious skin disease that has broken out in the burn, and the priest is to declare him unclean. 
But if the priest examines it and there is no white hair in the bright spot and it doesn't look deeper than the skin and is faded, the priest is to isolate him for seven days. 
On the seventh day the priest is to examine him, and if it has spread on the skin, the priest is to declare him unclean. It is a serious skin disease. 
But if the bright spot stays where it is and doesn't spread on the skin and is faded, it is the swelling from the burn, and the priest is to declare him clean, because it is only the scar from the burn. 
"When a man or a woman has a sore on the head or on the beard, 
the priest is to examine the sore, and if it looks deeper than the skin and the hair in it is yellow and thin, the priest is to declare him unclean. It is an itch, a serious skin disease of the head or the beard. 
But if the priest examines the itching sore and it doesn't look deeper than the skin and there is no black hair in it, the priest is to isolate the person for seven days. 
On the seventh day the priest is to examine the sore, and if the itch hasn't spread and there is no yellow hair in it and the itch doesn't look deeper than the skin, 
the person is to be shaved, but the sore itself is not to be shaved, and the priest is to isolate him for another seven days. 
On the seventh day the priest is to examine the itch, and if it hasn't spread on the skin and doesn't look deeper than the skin, the priest is to declare him clean. He is to wash his clothes and be clean. 
But if the itch spreads on the skin after he has been declared clean, 
the priest is to examine him, and if the itch has spread on the skin, the priest doesn't need to look for yellow hair. He is unclean. 
But if the itch looks the same and black hair has grown in it, the itch has healed and he is clean, and the priest is to declare him clean. 
"When a man or a woman has bright spots on the skin, white bright spots, 
the priest is to examine them, and if the bright spots on the skin are a dull white, it is a harmless rash that has broken out on the skin. He is clean. 
"If someone's hair has fallen out and he is bald, he is clean. 
If his hair has fallen out from the front of his head and his forehead is bald, he is clean. 
But if there is a reddish white sore on the bald head or the bald forehead, it is a serious skin disease breaking out on his bald head or his bald forehead. 
The priest is to examine him, and if the swelling of the sore is reddish white on his bald head or his bald forehead, looking like a skin disease on the body, 
he is a man with a serious skin disease and is unclean. The priest is to declare him unclean, because the sore is on his head. 
"The person with the disease is to wear torn clothes and let his hair hang loose, cover the lower part of his face and call out, 'Unclean! Unclean!' 
As long as he has the disease he stays unclean. He is to live alone, outside the camp. 
"When a garment has a mildew spot in it, whether a wool or a linen garment, 
in the lengthwise threads or the crosswise threads of linen or wool, or in leather or anything made of leather, 
if the spot is greenish or reddish in the garment, the leather, the lengthwise threads, the crosswise threads or anything made of leather, it is a spreading mildew, and it is to be shown to the priest. 
The priest is to examine the spot and isolate the item for seven days. 
On the seventh day he is to examine the spot, and if it has spread in the garment, the lengthwise threads, the crosswise threads or the leather, whatever the leather is used for, the spot is a destructive mildew and it is unclean. 
He is to burn the garment, the lengthwise threads or the crosswise threads, in wool or linen, or anything of leather the spot is in, because it is a destructive mildew. It is to be burned with fire. 
"If the priest examines it and the spot hasn't spread in the garment, the lengthwise threads, the crosswise threads or anything of leather, 
the priest is to order the item washed and isolate it for another seven days. 
Then the priest is to examine it after it has been washed, and if the spot hasn't changed color and hasn't spread, it is unclean. You are to burn it with fire. It is a mildew that eats into the item, whether the damage is on the inside or the outside. 
If the priest looks and the spot has faded after it has been washed, he is to tear it out of the garment, the leather, the lengthwise threads or the crosswise threads. 
If it appears again in the garment, the lengthwise threads, the crosswise threads or anything of leather, it is spreading, and you are to burn the item with fire. 
But the garment, the lengthwise threads or the crosswise threads, or anything of leather that you wash, from which the spot has gone away, is to be washed a second time, and it will be clean." 
This is the law about a mildew spot in a wool or linen garment, in the lengthwise threads or the crosswise threads, or in anything of leather, for declaring it clean or unclean.
