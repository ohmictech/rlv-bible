God spoke to Moses: 
"If anyone drifts and breaks faith against God by lying to his neighbor about something left in his care, or about a pledge, or about something stolen, or by cheating his neighbor, 
or by finding something lost and lying about it, or by swearing falsely about any of the things people do when they drift, 
then, once he has drifted and is guilty, he is to give back what he stole, or what he took by cheating, or what was left in his care, or the lost thing he found, 
or anything he swore falsely about. He is to pay it back in full and add a fifth of its value. He is to return it to its owner on the day he is found guilty. 
He is to bring his offering for guilt to God: a ram without defect from the flock, valued as you assess it, as an offering for guilt, to the priest. 
The priest will make things right for him before God, and he will be forgiven for whatever he did that made him guilty." 
God spoke to Moses: 
"Command Aaron and his sons: 'This is the law of the burnt offering. The burnt offering is to stay on the hearth of the altar all night until morning, and the fire on the altar is to keep burning. 
The priest is to put on his linen garment and his linen undergarments, and take up the ashes where the fire has burned the burnt offering on the altar, and put them beside the altar. 
Then he is to take off his garments, put on other garments, and carry the ashes outside the camp to a clean place. 
The fire on the altar is to keep burning and never go out. The priest is to burn wood on it every morning, lay the burnt offering out on it, and burn the fat of the offerings for wholeness on it. 
Fire is to keep burning on the altar all the time. It is never to go out. 
"'This is the law of the gift offering. The sons of Aaron are to offer it before God, in front of the altar. 
He is to take a handful of the fine flour of the gift offering, with its oil and all the frankincense on it, and burn it on the altar as its memorial portion, a soothing aroma to God. 
What is left of it Aaron and his sons are to eat. It is to be eaten without yeast in a holy place, in the courtyard of the Tent of Meeting. 
It is not to be baked with yeast. I have given it as their share of my offerings made by fire. It is most holy, like the offering for wrongdoing and the offering for guilt. 
Every male among Aaron's descendants may eat it. It is their share for the eras, throughout your generations, from God's offerings made by fire. Anyone who touches them becomes holy.'" 
God spoke to Moses: 
"This is the offering Aaron and his sons are to bring to God on the day he is anointed: a tenth of an ephah of fine flour as a gift offering, regularly, half of it in the morning and half of it in the evening. 
It is to be made with oil on a griddle. When it is soaked, bring it in, and offer the gift offering in baked pieces as a soothing aroma to God. 
The anointed priest who takes his place from among his sons is to offer it. It is a rule for the eras: it is to be burned completely to God. 
Every gift offering from a priest is to be burned completely. It is not to be eaten." 
God spoke to Moses: 
"Speak to Aaron and his sons: 'This is the law of the offering for wrongdoing. The offering for wrongdoing is to be killed before God in the place where the burnt offering is killed. It is most holy. 
The priest who offers it for wrongdoing is to eat it. It is to be eaten in a holy place, in the courtyard of the Tent of Meeting. 
Anything that touches its meat becomes holy. If any of its blood splashes on a garment, wash the garment in a holy place. 
A clay pot it is boiled in must be broken, and if it is boiled in a bronze pot, the pot must be scoured and rinsed with water. 
Every male among the priests may eat it. It is most holy. 
But no offering for wrongdoing whose blood is brought into the Tent of Meeting to make things right in the Holy Place may be eaten. It must be burned with fire.
