Now trust is being sure of what we hope for, and being convinced of what we cannot see. 
That is how the people of old were approved. 
By trust we understand that the universe was formed by God's word, so that what we see was not made out of things that can be seen. 
By trust Abel offered God a better sacrifice than Cain did. Because of that, he was given witness that he was right, when God spoke well of his gifts; and through that trust, though he is dead, he still speaks. 
By trust Enoch was taken up, so that he would not see death, and he could not be found, because God had taken him. For before he was taken, he was given witness that he had pleased God. 
And without trust it is impossible to please God, because anyone who comes to him must trust that he exists and that he rewards those who look for him. 
By trust Noah, when he was warned about things he could not yet see, was moved by devotion to God and built a boat to rescue his household. By that trust he passed sentence on the world and became an heir of the rightness that comes from trust. 
By trust Abraham, when he was called, obeyed and went out to a place he was going to receive as a charge handed down. He went out without knowing where he was going. 
By trust he lived as a foreigner in the land of promise, as in a land that was not his own, living in tents with Isaac and Jacob, who were heirs with him of the same promise. 
For he was looking for the city that has foundations, whose builder and maker is God. 
By trust even Sarah herself was given the power to have a child, and she gave birth even though she was past the age, because she counted the one who promised trustworthy. 
And so from one man, and him as good as dead, came descendants as many as the stars in the sky and as countless as the sand on the seashore. 
All these died in trust. They did not receive what was promised, but they saw it from far off and welcomed it, and they admitted that they were strangers and travelers on the earth. 
People who talk like that make it clear that they are looking for a homeland. 
If they had been thinking about the country they left, they would have had time to go back. 
But they were looking for a better country, a country in the sky. So God is not ashamed to be called their God, because he has prepared a city for them. 
By trust Abraham, when he was tested, offered up Isaac. He who had received the promises was offering up his only son, 
the one about whom it was said, "Your offspring will be counted through Isaac," 
He figured that God could raise people even from the dead, and in a way, he did receive him back from the dead. 
By trust Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau concerning things to come. 
By trust Jacob, when he was dying, blessed each of Joseph's sons, and bowed down, leaning on the top of his staff. 
By trust Joseph, when his end was near, spoke about the departure of the people of Israel, and gave instructions concerning his bones. 
By trust Moses, when he was born, was hidden for three months by his parents, because they saw that he was a beautiful child; and they were not afraid of the king's command. 
By trust Moses, when he had grown up, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter, 
and chose to be treated badly along with God's people rather than enjoy the pleasures of the mistake for a while, 
because he considered the shame of Christ greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt; for he kept his eyes on the reward. 
By trust he left Egypt, not afraid of the king's heat, because he held on, as though he could see the one who cannot be seen. 
By trust he kept the Passover and the sprinkling of the blood, so that the destroyer of the firstborn would not touch them. 
By trust they went through the Red Sea as if it were dry land. When the Egyptians tried it, they were drowned. 
By trust the walls of Jericho fell down after the people had marched around them for seven days. 
By trust Rahab the prostitute was not destroyed along with those who refused to obey, because she took the spies in and kept them safe. 
What more can I say? I do not have time to tell about Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel, and the spokesmen, 
who through trust conquered kingdoms, did what was right, received what was promised, and shut the mouths of lions, 
put out the power of fire, escaped the edge of the sword, were made strong out of weakness, became mighty in battle, and made foreign armies run. 
Women received their dead back by a rising again. Others were tortured and refused to be set free, so that they might rise again to something better. 
Others were tested with mocking and whipping, and also with chains and prison. 
They were stoned. They were sawn in two. They were tested. They were killed with the sword. They went around in sheepskins and goatskins, being poor, mistreated, and badly treated, 
the world was not worthy of them, wandering in deserts, mountains, caves, and the holes of the earth. 
All these were approved because of their trust, and yet they did not receive what was promised, 
because God had planned something better for us, so that they would not be made complete without us.
