So let's be careful that none of you seems to have fallen short of the promise of entering his rest. 
We have had the Good News announced to us, just as they did. But the message they heard did them no good, because it wasn't joined with trust in those who heard it. 
We who have trusted do enter that rest, just as he said, "So I swore in my heat, they'll never enter my rest," even though his works were finished from the founding of the world. 
He says somewhere about the seventh day, "God rested on the seventh day from all his works;" 
and here again, "They'll never enter my rest." 
So since it still remains for some to enter it, and those who first had the Good News announced to them didn't enter because they refused to obey, 
he sets another day, "today", saying through David so much later (as has been said), "Today, if you hear his voice, don't harden your hearts." 
If Joshua had given them rest, God wouldn't have spoken later about another day. 
So there still remains a rest for the Sabbath for God's people. 
Anyone who enters God's rest also rests from their own works, just as God did from his. 
So let's do our best to enter that rest, so that no one falls by following that same example of refusing to obey. 
God's word is alive and active, sharper than any double-edged sword. It cuts all the way through to where life and spirit divide, to joints and marrow, and it passes a verdict on the thoughts and the intentions of the heart. 
No creature is hidden from his sight. Everything is naked and laid bare before the eyes of the one we must answer to. 
So then, since we have a great high priest who has gone through the skies, Jesus the Son of God, let's hold tightly to what we declare. 
We don't have a high priest who can't feel for our weaknesses. We have one who has been tested in every way, just as we're, yet without drift. 
So let's come near to the throne of favor with boldness, so that we may receive mercy and find favor to help us when we need it.
