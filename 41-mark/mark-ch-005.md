They came to the other side of the sea, into the region of the Gadarenes. 
When he had come out of the boat, immediately a man with an unclean spirit came to meet him from the tombs. 
He lived among the tombs. Nobody could tie him up any longer, not even with chains, 
because he had often been tied up with shackles and chains, and the chains had been torn apart by him and the shackles broken in pieces. Nobody was strong enough to tame him. 
Night and day, among the tombs and in the mountains, he was crying out and cutting himself with stones. 
When he saw Jesus from a distance, he ran and bowed down to him, 
and crying out with a loud voice, he said, "What do I have to do with you, Jesus, Son of the Most High God? I put you under oath by God: don't put me in anguish." 
He said to him, "Come out of the man, you unclean spirit!" 
He asked him, "What is your name?" He said to him, "My name is Legion, for we are many." 
He begged him earnestly not to send them out of the region. 
Now on the mountainside there was a great herd of pigs feeding. 
The unclean spirits begged him, saying, "Send us into the pigs, so that we may enter them." 
At once Jesus gave them permission. The unclean spirits came out and entered the pigs. The herd of about two thousand rushed down the steep bank into the sea, and they were drowned in the sea. 
Those who fed the pigs fled and told it in the city and in the countryside. The people came to see what had happened. 
They came to Jesus and saw the man who had been possessed by unclean spirits sitting there, clothed and in his right mind, the man who had had the legion. And they were afraid. 
Those who saw it told them what had happened to the man who was possessed by unclean spirits, and about the pigs. 
They began to beg him to leave their region. 
As he was getting into the boat, the man who had been possessed by unclean spirits begged to go with him. 
He didn't let him, but said to him, "Go home to your own people, and tell them what great things the Lord has done for you and how he had mercy on you." 
He went his way and began to proclaim in the Decapolis how Jesus had done great things for him, and everyone was amazed. 
When Jesus had crossed back over in the boat to the other side, a great crowd gathered to him, and he was by the sea. 
Look, one of the leaders of the gathering place, Jairus by name, came and, seeing him, fell at his feet 
and begged him earnestly, saying, "My little daughter is at the point of death. Please come and lay your hands on her, so that she may be made healthy and live." 
He went with him, and a great crowd followed him and pressed in on him from all sides. 
A woman who had been bleeding for twelve years, 
and had suffered a great deal at the hands of many doctors, and had spent all that she had, and was no better, but only grew worse, 
having heard about Jesus, came up behind him in the crowd and touched his clothes. 
She said, "If I just touch his clothes, I will be made well." 
Immediately her bleeding stopped, and she felt in her body that she was healed of her sickness. 
Immediately Jesus, knowing in himself that power had gone out from him, turned around in the crowd and asked, "Who touched my clothes?" 
His students said to him, "You see the crowd pressing in on you, and you say, 'Who touched me?'" 
He looked around to see who had done this. 
But the woman, fearing and trembling, knowing what had been done to her, came and fell down before him and told him the whole truth. 
He said to her, "Daughter, your trust has healed you and made you whole. Go in wholeness, and be cured of your sickness." 
While he was still speaking, people came from the gathering place leader's house, saying, "Your daughter is dead. Why bother the Teacher any more?" 
But Jesus, overhearing what was said, immediately said to the gathering place leader, "Don't be afraid, just trust." 
He allowed no one to follow him except Peter, James, and John the brother of James. 
He came to the gathering place leader's house, and he saw an uproar, crying, and great wailing. 
When he had gone in, he said to them, "Why are you making an uproar and crying? The child isn't dead, but asleep." 
They laughed at him. But he put them all out and took the child's father and mother, and those who were with him, and went in where the child was lying. 
Taking the child by the hand, he said to her, "Talitha cumi!" which means, "Girl, I tell you, get up!" 
Immediately the girl got up and walked, for she was twelve years old. They were overcome with amazement. 
He strictly ordered them that no one should know this, and told them to give her something to eat.
