The Chief Musician. Jeduthun. A Psalm by David. I said, "I'll watch my ways, so that I don't drift with my tongue. I'll keep my mouth with a bridle while the guilty is before me." 
I was mute with silence. I held my peace, even from good. My sorrow was stirred. 
My heart was hot within me. While I meditated, the fire burned. I spoke with my tongue: 
"God, show me my end, what's the measure of my days. Let me know how frail I am. 
Look, you have made my days hand widths. My lifetime is as nothing before you. Surely every man stands as a breath." Selah. 
"Surely every man walks like a shadow. Surely they busy themselves in vain. He heaps up, and doesn't know who will gather. 
Now, Lord, what do I wait for? My hope is in you. 
Deliver me from all my rebellion. Don't make me the reproach of the foolish. 
I was mute. I didn't open my mouth, because you did it. 
Remove your scourge away from me. I'm overcome by the blow of your hand. 
When you rebuke and correct man for guilt, you consume his wealth like a moth. Surely every man is but a breath." Selah. 
"Hear my prayer, God, and give ear to my cry. Don't be silent at my tears. I'm a stranger with you, a foreigner, as all my fathers were. 
Oh spare me, that I can recover strength, before I go away and exist no more."
