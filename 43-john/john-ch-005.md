After these things, there was a Jewish feast, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem. 
Now in Jerusalem by the Sheep Gate, there's a pool, which is called in Hebrew, "Bethesda", with five covered walkways. 
In these lay a great crowd of those who were sick, blind, lame, or paralyzed, waiting for the moving of the water; 
for a messenger went down at certain times into the pool and stirred up the water. Whoever stepped in first after the stirring of the water was healed of whatever disease they had. 
A certain man was there who had been sick for thirty-eight years. 
When Jesus saw him lying there, and knew that he had been sick for a long time, he asked him, "Do you want to be made well?" 
The sick man answered him, "Sir, I have no one to put me into the pool when the water is stirred up, but while I'm coming, another steps down before me." 
Jesus said to him, "Get up, pick up your mat, and walk." 
At once the man was made well, and he picked up his mat and walked. Now that day was a Sabbath. 
So the Jewish leaders said to him who was cured, "It's the Sabbath. It isn't allowed for you to carry the mat." 
He answered them, "He who made me well said to me, 'Pick up your mat and walk.'" 
Then they asked him, "Who's the man who said to you, 'Pick up your mat and walk'?" 
But the one who was healed didn't know who it was, for Jesus had slipped away into the crowd that was in the place. 
Afterward Jesus found him in the temple courts and said to him, "Look, you're made well. Stop drifting, so that nothing worse happens to you." 
The man went away, and told the Jewish leaders that it was Jesus who had made him well. 
That's why the Jewish leaders began to hunt Jesus down and tried to kill him, because he did these things on the Sabbath. 
But Jesus answered them, "My Father is still working, so I'm working, too." 
That's why the Jewish leaders sought all the more to kill him, because he not only broke the Sabbath, but also called God his own Father, making himself equal with God. 
Jesus answered them, "Truly I tell you, the Son can do nothing on his own, only what he sees the Father doing. Whatever the Father does, the Son does the same way. 
The Father loves the Son and shows him everything he does. He'll show him greater deeds than these, so that you'll be amazed. 
As the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so the Son also gives life to whomever he chooses. 
The Father passes a verdict on no one, but he has given every verdict to the Son, 
that all may honor the Son, even as they honor the Father. Whoever doesn't honor the Son doesn't honor the Father who sent him. 
"Truly I tell you, whoever hears my word and trusts him who sent me has the real life of the era, and doesn't come under the verdict, but has crossed over out of death into life. 
Truly I tell you, the time comes, and now is, when the dead will hear the Son of God's voice; and those who hear will live. 
As the Father has life in himself, even so he gave to the Son also to have life in himself. 
He also gave him authority to pass a verdict, because he's the Human One. 
Don't be amazed at this, for the time comes in which everyone who's in the tombs will hear his voice 
and will come out; those who have done good, to the rising of life; and those who have done bad things, to the rising that brings a verdict. 
I can of myself do nothing. As I hear, I pass a verdict; and my verdict is right, because I don't seek my own will, but the will of my Father who sent me. 
"If I testify about myself, my witness isn't valid. 
It's another who testifies about me. I know that the witness which he testifies about me is true. 
You have sent to John, and he has testified to the truth. 
But the witness which I receive isn't from people. However, I say these things that you may be made whole. 
He was the burning and shining lamp, and you were willing to celebrate for a while in his light. 
But the witness which I have is greater than that of John; for the deeds which the Father gave me to finish, the very deeds that I do, testify about me, that the Father has sent me. 
The Father himself, who sent me, has testified about me. You have neither heard his voice at any time, nor seen his shape. 
You don't have his word making a home in you, because you don't trust him whom he sent. 
"You search the Scriptures, because you think that in them you have the real life of the era; and these are they which testify about me. 
Yet you refuse to come to me, that you may have life. 
I don't receive honor from people. 
But I know you, that you don't have God's love in yourselves. 
I have come in my Father's name, and you don't welcome me. If another comes in his own name, you'll welcome him. 
How can you trust, who receive honor from one another, and you don't seek the honor that comes from the only God? 
"Don't think that I'll accuse you to the Father. There's one who accuses you, even Moses, on whom you have set your hope. 
If you trusted Moses, you'd trust me; for he wrote about me. 
But if you don't trust his writings, how will you trust my words?"
