When he went into the house of one of the rulers of the strict religious ones on a Sabbath to eat bread, they were watching him. 
Look, a certain man whose body was swollen with fluid was there in front of him. 
Jesus spoke to the experts in the law and the strict religious ones, saying, "Is it allowed to heal on the Sabbath?" 
But they were silent. He took him, and healed him, and let him go. 
He answered them, "Which of you, if your son or an ox fell into a well, wouldn't immediately pull him out on a Sabbath day?" 
They couldn't answer him regarding these things. 
He spoke a story to those who were invited, when he noticed how they chose the best seats, and said to them, 
"When you're invited by anyone to a wedding feast, don't sit in the best seat, since perhaps someone more honorable than you might be invited by him, 
and he who invited both of you'd come and tell you, 'Make room for this person.' Then you'd begin, with shame, to take the lowest place. 
But when you're invited, go and sit in the lowest place, so that when he who invited you comes, he may tell you, 'Friend, move up higher.' Then you'll be honored in the presence of everyone who sits at the table with you. 
Everyone who lifts himself up will be humbled, and whoever humbles himself will be lifted up." 
He also said to the one who had invited him, "When you make a dinner or a supper, don't call your friends, nor your brothers, nor your kinsmen, nor rich neighbors, or perhaps they might also return the favor, and pay you back. 
But when you make a feast, ask the poor, the maimed, the lame, or the blind; 
and you'll be flourishing, because they don't have the resources to repay you. You'll be repaid at the rising of the upright." 
When one of those who sat at the table with him heard these things, he said to him, "Flourishing is he who will eat bread in God's reign!" 
But he said to him, "A certain man made a great supper, and he invited many people. 
He sent out his slave at supper time to tell those who were invited, 'Come, for everything is ready now.' 
They all as one began to make excuses. The first said to him, 'I have bought a field, and I must go and see it. Please have me excused.' 
"Another said, 'I have bought five yoke of oxen, and I must go try them out. Please have me excused.' 
"Another said, 'I have married a wife, and so I can't come.' 
"That slave came, and told his lord these things. Then the master of the house, being angry, said to his slave, 'Go out quickly into the streets and lanes of the city, and bring in the poor, maimed, blind, and lame.' 
"The slave said, 'Lord, it's done as you commanded, and there's still room.' 
"The lord said to the slave, 'Go out into the highways and hedges, and compel them to come in, that my house may be filled. 
I tell you, none of those men who were invited will taste my supper. Many are called, but few are chosen.'" 
Now great crowds were going with him. He turned and said to them, 
"If anyone comes to me, and doesn't disregard his own father, mother, wife, children, brothers, and sisters, yes, and his own life also, he can't be my student. 
Whoever doesn't bear his own cross and come after me, can't be my student. 
Which of you, desiring to build a tower, doesn't first sit down and count the cost, to see if he has enough to complete it? 
Or perhaps, when he has laid a foundation and isn't able to finish, everyone who sees begins to mock him, 
saying, 'This man began to build and wasn't able to finish.' 
Or what king, as he goes to encounter another king in war, won't sit down first and consider whether he's able with ten thousand to meet him who comes against him with twenty thousand? 
Or else, while the other is yet a great way off, he sends an envoy and asks for terms of wholeness. 
So whoever of you doesn't renounce all that he has can't be my student. 
"Salt is good, but if the salt becomes flat and tasteless, with what do you season it? 
It's fit neither for the soil nor for the manure pile. It's thrown out. Whoever has ears to hear, let him hear."
