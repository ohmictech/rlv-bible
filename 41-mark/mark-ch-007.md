Then the strict religious ones and some of the Bible teachers gathered to him, having come from Jerusalem. 
They saw that some of his students were eating bread with unclean hands, that is, unwashed hands, and they found fault. 
(For the strict religious ones and all the Jews don't eat unless they wash their hands and forearms, holding to the tradition of the elders. 
They don't eat when they come from the marketplace unless they immerse themselves, and there are many other things they have received to hold to: dippings of cups, pitchers, bronze pots, and couches.) 
The strict religious ones and the Bible teachers asked him, "Why don't your students walk according to the tradition of the elders, but eat their bread with unwashed hands?" 
He answered them, "Isaiah spoke well about you pretenders, as it is written, 'This people honors me with their lips, but their heart is far from me. 
They worship me for nothing, teaching as their teaching the rules made by people.'" 
"For you set aside the commandment of God and hold tightly to the tradition of people (the dipping of pitchers and cups), and you do many other things like this." 
He said to them, "You have a fine way of rejecting the commandment of God in order to keep your tradition. 
Moses said, 'Honor your father and your mother,' and, 'Whoever speaks maliciously of father or mother must be put to death.' 
But you say, 'If someone tells his father or his mother, "Whatever help you might have received from me is Corban, that is, given to God;"' 
"then you no longer let him do anything for his father or his mother, 
so you cancel the word of God by your tradition that you have handed down. And you do many things like this." 
He called all the crowd to himself and said to them, "Listen to me, all of you, and understand. 
There is nothing from outside a person that can make them unclean by going into them, but the things that come out of a person are what make a person unclean. 
If anyone has ears to hear, let him hear!" 
When he had gone into a house away from the crowd, his students asked him about the story. 
He said to them, "Are you also without understanding? Don't you see that whatever goes into a person from outside can't make them unclean, 
because it doesn't go into his heart, but into his stomach, and then out into the latrine, making all foods clean?" 
He said, "What comes out of a person, that is what makes the person unclean. 
From within, out of people's hearts, come malicious thoughts, cheating, sleeping around, murders, thefts, 
greed, malice, deceit, shamelessness, a jealous eye, slander, arrogance, and foolishness. 
All these malicious things come from within and make a person unclean." 
From there he got up and went away to the region of Tyre and Sidon. He entered a house and didn't want anyone to know it, but he couldn't escape notice. 
A woman whose little daughter had an unclean spirit, having heard of him, came and fell down at his feet. 
Now the woman was a Greek, a Syrophoenician by birth. She begged him to drive the unclean spirit out of her daughter. 
But Jesus said to her, "Let the children be filled first, for it isn't right to take the children's bread and throw it to the dogs." 
But she answered him, "Yes, Lord. Yet even the dogs under the table eat the children's crumbs." 
He said to her, "Because of this saying, go your way. The unclean spirit has gone out of your daughter." 
She went away to her house and found the child lying on the bed, with the unclean spirit gone out. 
Again he left the region of Tyre and Sidon and came to the Sea of Galilee through the middle of the region of the Decapolis. 
They brought to him a man who was deaf and had a speech impediment. They begged him to lay his hand on him. 
He took him aside from the crowd privately and put his fingers into his ears, and he spat and touched his tongue. 
Looking up to the sky, he sighed, and said to him, "Ephphatha!" that is, "Be opened!" 
Immediately his ears were opened, his speech impediment left him, and he spoke clearly. 
He commanded them to tell no one, but the more he commanded them, the more widely they proclaimed it. 
They were astonished beyond measure, saying, "He has done everything well. He even makes the deaf hear and the mute speak!"
