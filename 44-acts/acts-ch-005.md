But a certain man named Ananias, with Sapphira his wife, sold a possession, 
and kept back part of the price, his wife also being aware of it, then brought a certain part and laid it at the feet of the sent ones. 
But Peter said, "Ananias, why has the Adversary filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit and to keep back part of the price of the land? 
While you kept it, didn't it remain your own? After it was sold, wasn't it in your power? How is it that you have conceived this thing in your heart? You haven't lied to people, but to God." 
Ananias, hearing these words, fell down and died. Great fear came on everyone who heard these things. 
The young men arose and wrapped him up, and they carried him out and buried him. 
About three hours later, his wife, not knowing what had happened, came in. 
Peter answered her, "Tell me whether you sold the land for so much." She said, "Yes, for so much." 
But Peter asked her, "How is it that you have agreed together to test the Spirit of the Lord? Look, the feet of those who have buried your husband are at the door, and they will carry you out." 
She fell down immediately at his feet and died. The young men came in and found her dead, and they carried her out and buried her by her husband. 
Great awe came on the whole gathering, and on everyone who heard these things. 
By the hands of the sent ones many signs and wonders were done among the people. They were all with one accord in Solomon's covered walkway. 
None of the rest dared to join them; however, the people honored them. 
More who trusted were added to the Lord, crowds of both men and women. 
They even carried out the sick into the streets and laid them on cots and mattresses, so that as Peter came by, at least his shadow might overshadow some of them. 
The crowd also came together from the cities around Jerusalem, bringing sick people and those who were oppressed by unclean spirits; and they were all healed. 
But the high priest rose up, and everyone who was with him (which is the faction of the religious elite), and they were filled with jealousy 
and laid hands on the sent ones, then put them in public custody. 
But a messenger of the Lord opened the prison doors by night, and brought them out and said, 
"Go stand and speak in the temple courts to the people all the words of this real life." 
When they heard this, they entered into the temple courts about daybreak and taught. But the high priest and those who were with him came and called the council together, with all the senate of the children of Israel, and sent to the prison to have them brought. 
But the temple guards who came didn't find them in the prison. They returned and reported, 
"We found the prison shut and locked, and the guards standing before the doors, but when we opened them, we found no one inside!" 
Now when the high priest, the captain of the temple, and the chief priests heard these words, they were very puzzled about them and wondered what might come of this. 
One came and told them, "Look, the men whom you put in prison are in the temple courts, standing and teaching the people." 
Then the captain went with the temple guards, and brought them without violence, for they were afraid that the people might stone them. 
When they had brought them, they set them before the council. The high priest questioned them, 
saying, "Didn't we strictly command you not to teach in this name? Look, you have filled Jerusalem with your teaching, and intend to bring this man's blood on us." 
But Peter and the sent ones answered, "We must obey God rather than people. 
The God of our fathers raised up Jesus, whom you killed, hanging him on a tree. 
God exalted him with his right hand to be a Leader and a Restorer, to give a change of heart to Israel, and the release of mistakes. 
We are his witnesses of these things; and so also is the Holy Spirit, whom God has given to those who obey him." 
But they, when they heard this, were cut to the heart, and were determined to kill them. 
But one stood up in the council, a strict religious man named Gamaliel, a teacher of the law, honored by everyone, and commanded to put the sent ones out for a little while. 
He said to them, "Men of Israel, be careful concerning these men, what you are about to do. 
For before these days Theudas rose up, making himself out to be somebody, and about four hundred men joined him. He was slain; and all, as many as obeyed him, were dispersed and came to nothing. 
After this man, Judas of Galilee rose up in the days of the enrollment, and drew away some people after him. He also died, and all, as many as obeyed him, were scattered abroad. 
Now I tell you, withdraw from these men and leave them alone. For if this counsel or this work is of people, it will be overthrown. 
But if it is of God, you will not be able to overthrow it, and you would be found even to be fighting against God!" 
They agreed with him. Summoning the sent ones, they beat them and commanded them not to speak in the name of Jesus, and let them go. 
They therefore departed from the presence of the council, celebrating that they were counted worthy to suffer dishonor for Jesus' name. 
Every day, in the temple courts and at home, they never stopped teaching and announcing the good news that Jesus is the Christ.
