A Psalm. A song for the Sabbath day. It's a good thing to give thanks to God, to driftg praises to your name, Most High, 
to proclaim your loving kindness in the morning, and your faithfulness every night, 
with the ten-stringed lute, with the harp, and with the melody of the lyre. 
You, God, have made me glad through your work. I'll triumph in the works of your hands. 
How great are your works, God! Your thoughts are very deep. 
A senseless man doesn't know, neither does a fool understand this: 
though the guilty spring up as the grass, and all the those who do bad flourish, they'll be destroyed for the eras. 
But you, God, are on high for the eras more. 
Look, your enemies, God, look, your enemies will perish. All the those who do bad will be scattered. 
But you have exalted my horn like that of the wild ox. I'm anointed with fresh oil. 
My eye has also seen my enemies. My ears have heard of the guilty enemies who rise up against me. 
The righteous will flourish like the palm tree. He'll grow like a cedar in Lebanon. 
They're planted in God's house. They'll flourish in our God's courts. 
They'll still produce fruit in old age. They'll be full of sap and green, 
to show that God is upright. He's my rock, and there's no unrighteousness in him.
