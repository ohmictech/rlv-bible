Now in those days, when the number of the students was multiplying, a complaint arose from the Greek-speaking Jews against the Hebrew-speaking ones, because their widows were neglected in the daily service. 
The twelve summoned the crowd of students and said, "It isn't right for us to neglect the word of God in order to serve tables. 
So, select from among you, brothers and sisters, seven men of good reputation, full of the Holy Spirit and of wisdom, whom we can appoint over this business. 
But we'll continue steadfastly in prayer and in the ministry of the word." 
These words pleased the whole crowd. They chose Stephen, a man full of trust and of the Holy Spirit, Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolaus, a convert of Antioch, 
whom they set before the sent ones. When they had prayed, they laid their hands on them. 
The word of God increased and the number of the students greatly multiplied in Jerusalem. A great company of the priests were obedient to the trust. 
Stephen, full of trust and power, performed great wonders and signs among the people. 
But some of those from the gathering place called "The Freedmen", as well as Cyrenians, Alexandrians, and people from Cilicia and Asia, rose up and argued with Stephen. 
They weren't able to withstand the wisdom and the Spirit by which he spoke. 
Then they secretly persuaded some men to say, "We have heard him speak slanderous words against Moses and God." 
They stirred up the people, the elders, and the Bible teachers, and came against him and seized him, then brought him in to the council, 
and set up false witnesses who said, "This man never stops speaking slanderous words against this holy place and the law. 
We have heard him say that this Jesus of Nazareth will tear down this place, and will change the customs which Moses delivered to us." 
Everyone who sat in the council looked intently at him and saw that his face was like the face of a messenger.
