that I have great sorrow and ongoing pain in my heart. 
I could have it so that I would stand away from Christ for my brothers' sake, my relatives according to the flesh 
who are Israelites; whose is the adoption, the weight, the binding arrangements, the giving of the law, the service, and the promises; 
of whom are the ancestors, and from whom Christ came according to the flesh, who's over all, God, praised through all the eras. Amen. 
But it isn't as though the word of God has come to nothing. They aren't all Israel that are of Israel. 
Neither, because they're Abraham's offspring, are they all children. But, "your offspring will be accounted as from Isaac." 
That is, it isn't the children of the flesh who are children of God, but the children of the promise are counted as heirs. 
This is a word of promise: "At the appointed time I'll come, and Sarah will have a son." 
Not only so, but Rebekah also conceived by one, by our father Isaac. 
Before they were born or had done anything good or bad, so that the purpose of God in choosing would stand, not of deeds, but of him who calls, 
it was said to her, "The elder will serve the younger." 
Even as it's written, "Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated." 
What will we say then? Is there unrighteousness with God? Not a chance! 
He said to Moses, "I'll have mercy on whom I have mercy, and I'll have compassion on whom I have compassion." 
So then it isn't of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who has mercy. 
The Scripture says to Pharaoh, "For this very purpose I caused you to be raised up, so that I could show my power in you, and so that my name would be announced in all the land." 
So then, he has mercy on whom he desires, and he hardens whom he desires. 
You'll say then to me, "Why does he still find fault? Who withstands his will?" 
But indeed, O person, who are you to reply against God? Will the thing formed ask him who formed it, "Why did you make me like this?" 
Doesn't the potter have a right over the clay, from the same lump to make one part a vessel for honor, and another for common use? 
What if God, willing to show his heat and to make his power known, bore with much patience vessels of heat prepared for ruin, 
so that he could make known the riches of his weight on vessels of mercy, which he prepared beforehand for weight, 
us, whom he also called, not from the Jewish people only, but also from the nations? 
As he says also in Hosea, "I'll call them 'my people,' which weren't my people; and her 'truly loved,' who wasn't truly loved." 
"It'll be that in the place where it was said to them, 'You aren't my people,' there they'll be called 'children of the living God.'" 
Isaiah cries concerning Israel, "If the number of the children of Israel are as the sand of the sea, it's the remnant who will be rescued; 
for he'll finish the work and cut it short in rightness, because the Lord will make a short work on the land." 
As Isaiah has said before, "Unless the Lord of Armies had left us a seed, we'd have become like Sodom, and would have been made like Gomorrah." 
What will we say then? That the nations, who didn't follow after rightness, reached rightness, the rightness that comes by trust; 
but Israel, following after a law of rightness, didn't arrive at the law of rightness. 
Why? Because they didn't seek it by trust, but as if it were by deeds of the law. They stumbled over the stumbling stone, 
even as it's written, "Look, I lay in Zion a stumbling stone and a rock that trips people up; and no one who trusts in him will be disappointed."
