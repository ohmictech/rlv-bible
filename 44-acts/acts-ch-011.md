Now the sent ones and the brothers who were in Judea heard that the nations had also received the word of God. 
When Peter had come up to Jerusalem, those who were of the circumcision contended with him, 
saying, "You went in to uncircumcised men and ate with them!" 
But Peter began and explained everything to them in order: 
"I was in the city of Joppa praying, and in a trance I saw a vision: a certain container descending, like it was a great sheet let down from the sky by four corners. It came as far as me. 
When I had looked intently at it, I considered, and saw the four-footed animals of the land, wild animals, creeping things, and birds of the sky. 
I also heard a voice saying to me, 'Get up, Peter. Kill and eat!' 
But I said, 'Not so, Lord, for nothing unholy or unclean has ever entered into my mouth.' 
But a voice answered me the second time out of the sky, 'What God has made clean, don't you call unclean.' 
This was done three times, and all were drawn up again into the sky. 
Look, immediately three men stood before the house where I was, having been sent from Caesarea to me. 
The Spirit told me to go with them, making no distinction. These six brothers also accompanied me, and we entered into the man's house. 
He told us how he had seen the messenger standing in his house and saying to him, 'Send to Joppa and get Simon, who's called Peter, 
who will speak to you words by which you'll be made whole, you and all your house.' 
As I began to speak, the Holy Spirit fell on them, even as on us at the beginning. 
I remembered the word of the Lord, how he said, 'John indeed immersed in water, but you'll be immersed in the Holy Spirit.' 
If then God gave to them the same gift as us when we trusted in the Lord Jesus Christ, who was I, that I could withstand God?" 
When they heard these things, they quieted down and gave honor to God, saying, "Then God has also granted to the nations a change of heart that leads to life!" 
They who were scattered abroad by the oppression that arose about Stephen traveled as far as Phoenicia, Cyprus, and Antioch, speaking the word to no one except Jewish people. 
But there were some of them, men of Cyprus and Cyrene, who, when they had come to Antioch, spoke to the Greek-speaking Jews, announcing the Good News about the Lord Jesus. 
The hand of the Lord was with them, and a great number trusted and turned to the Lord. 
The report concerning them came to the ears of the gathering which was in Jerusalem. They sent out Barnabas to go as far as Antioch, 
who, when he had come and seen the favor of God, was glad. He urged them all to remain near to the Lord with purpose of heart. 
He was a good man, and full of the Holy Spirit and of trust, and many people were added to the Lord. 
Barnabas went out to Tarsus to look for Saul. 
When he had found him, he brought him to Antioch. A whole year they met together with the gathering, and taught many people. The students were first called Christians in Antioch. 
Now in these days, spokesmen came down from Jerusalem to Antioch. 
One of them named Agabus stood up and indicated by the Spirit that a great famine would spread over the whole world, which also happened in the days of Claudius. 
As any of the students had plenty, each determined to send relief to the brothers who lived in Judea; 
which they also did, sending it to the elders by the hands of Barnabas and Saul.
