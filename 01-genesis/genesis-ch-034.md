One day Dinah, the daughter Leah had borne to Jacob, went out to visit some of the young women of the land. 
Shechem son of Hamor the Hivite, the ruler of the region, saw her, grabbed her, and raped her. 
But he became deeply attached to Dinah, Jacob's daughter, and he loved her and spoke tenderly to her. 
So Shechem said to his father Hamor, "Get me this girl as my wife." 
When Jacob heard that Shechem had violated his daughter Dinah, his sons were out in the field with the livestock, so Jacob said nothing until they came home. 
Then Hamor, Shechem's father, went out to Jacob to talk with him. 
When Jacob's sons came in from the field and heard about it, they were horrified and furious, because Shechem had done something disgraceful in Israel by sleeping with Jacob's daughter. Such a thing should never be done. 
Hamor said to them, "My son Shechem is deeply in love with your daughter. Please give her to him as his wife. 
Intermarry with us. Give us your daughters and take ours. 
Live among us. The land is open to you, so settle wherever you like and do business here." 
Then Shechem said to Dinah's father and brothers, "If I can find favor with you, I'll give you whatever you ask. 
Set the bride price as high as you like, and I'll pay it. Just give me the girl as my wife." 
But Jacob's sons answered Shechem and his father Hamor deceitfully, because Shechem had violated their sister Dinah. 
They said to them, "We can't do that. Giving our sister to a man who isn't circumcised would be a disgrace to us. 
We'll agree on one condition: that you become like us, and every male among you is circumcised. 
Then we'll give you our daughters and take yours, and we'll live among you and become one people. 
But if you won't agree to be circumcised, we'll take our sister and leave." 
Hamor and his son Shechem liked what they heard. 
The young man didn't waste any time, because he was delighted with Jacob's daughter, and he was the most respected man in his father's household. 
So Hamor and his son Shechem went to the city gate and spoke to the men of the city: 
"These people are friendly toward us. Let them live in the land and do business here. The land is big enough for them. Let's marry their daughters and give them ours. 
But they'll only agree to live among us and become one people on one condition: that every male among us is circumcised, just as they are. 
Won't their livestock, their property, and all their animals become ours? Let's just agree to it, and they'll settle among us." 
Everyone who passed through the city gate listened to Hamor and his son Shechem, and every male in the city was circumcised. 
Three days later, while the men were still in pain, two of Jacob's sons, Simeon and Levi, Dinah's brothers, took their swords and attacked the unsuspecting city, killing every male. 
They put Hamor and his son Shechem to the sword, then took Dinah out of Shechem's house and left. 
Then the other sons of Jacob came upon the dead bodies and looted the city, because their sister had been violated. 
They seized their flocks, herds, and donkeys, and everything in the city and in the fields. 
They carried off all their wealth, took their little ones and their wives captive, and plundered everything in the houses. 
Then Jacob said to Simeon and Levi, "You've brought trouble on me. Now I'm hated by the people of this land, the Canaanites and the Perizzites. We're few in number, and if they join forces against me, they'll wipe out me and my whole household." 
But they answered, "Should he treat our sister like a prostitute?"
